Trade, Tariffs & Currency

Your sneakers, phone and hoodie probably crossed an ocean — maybe landing at the Port of Savannah, right here in Georgia. Why don’t countries just make everything themselves?

Your mission: make two islands richer with comparative advantage, set a tariff (watch out for payback!), then convert between dollars and yuan and find out who cheers — and who groans — when exchange rates move.

    Three trade barriers

    Tariff: a tax on imports — imported goods cost more.
    Quota: a limit on the amount imported — “50,000 cars a year, max.”
    Embargo: a ban on all or some trade with a country — usually for political or security reasons.

    Barrier detective: which one is it?

    Mission 3: dollars ⇄ yuan

    A US store pays in dollars, but a factory in China pays its workers in yuan (人民币) — so trade needs a way to swap currencies. The exchange rate is the price of one currency in another. Drag the year slider for real rates, or set your own.

    Challenges

      💱 Strong vs weak currency

      The yuan “strengthens” when $1 buys fewer yuan (the number falls). Then US trips get cheaper for Chinese tourists, but Chinese exports cost more in the US. A weaker yuan does the opposite.

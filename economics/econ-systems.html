Economic Systems

Every country has to answer the same three questions: What do we make? How do we make it? Who gets it? Some answer with grandparents’ traditions, some with a government plan, some with market prices. If you ran an island, which would you pick?

Your mission: run the same 100-person island three ways — traditional, command and market — through storms and inventions; place 14 countries on the command-to-market line; then scrub a timeline to see how China went from a planned economy to a socialist market economy.

🧭 Mixed economiesNo real country is a “pure” command or “pure” market economy. Every country is mixed — they just sit at different spots between the two ends.
🪶 Do traditional economies still exist?

Yes — usually in parts of a country, not whole countries: Maasai herders in East Africa, Inuit hunters in the Arctic, some Amazon communities. They also use markets and modern tools, so their countries are still mixed.

📋 Command in history: the Soviet Union

From 1928 the Soviet Union ran its whole economy with Five-Year Plans: the state planning committee (Gosplan) decided how much steel, how many tractors and shoes each factory made. Heavy industry grew fast, but everyday goods were often short and lines were long. After the USSR broke up in 1991, Russia moved toward markets.

🏷️ Markets: the “invisible hand”

In 1776 the Scottish economist Adam Smith wrote in The Wealth of Nations that when people pursue their own interest, the market works like an “invisible hand,” guiding them to make what others need.

How China officially describes its system

China’s basic economic system, in its own words: public ownership as the mainstay, with many forms of ownership developing together; distribution mainly according to work, alongside other forms; and a socialist market economy. In 2013 the Third Plenary Session of the 18th CPC Central Committee said the market should play the decisive role in allocating resources, and the government should play its role better. (8th-grade 道德与法治 textbook, “Basic Economic System.”)

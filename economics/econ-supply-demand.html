Supply, Demand & Prices

Why does a $90 concert ticket resell for $400+? Why did a dozen eggs average over $6 in March 2025 — and fall back to about $2 a year later? Nobody ordered it. Supply and demand were playing tug-of-war.

Your mission: drag the supply and demand curves to find the equilibrium price, cap ticket prices and watch a shortage appear, run a lemonade stand for 7 days for maximum profit — and call which curve every event shifts.

Mission 1: the market lab — drag the curves

Pick a market. Drag the blue demand curve (D) or the orange supply curve (S) to shift it; drag anywhere else to move the price line up or down. Below the equilibrium price you get a shortage; above it, a surplus.

Mission 2: the 7-day lemonade stand

Each morning, read the forecast, then set your price per cup and how many cups to make. Each cup costs $0.25 to make (lemons, sugar, cup). Make too many and you’re stuck with a surplus; too few and customers walk away (a shortage). More profit over 7 days = more stars.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Standards (Georgia Standards of Excellence, Social Studies, 2016): SS6E1a / SS7E1a (compare how traditional, command and market economies answer the three basic questions — a market economy answers them through prices), SS6E1b / SS7E1b (mixed economies) and SS7E7c (compare the economic systems of China, India, Japan, North Korea and South Korea). The high-school Economics standards SSEMI2 (how the laws of demand and supply and prices determine production and distribution in a market economy) and SSEF2 (rational decisions and incentives) state this content fully; this lesson is the middle-school on-ramp.

China links: 统编版《道德与法治》 Grade 8, Vol. 2, Lesson 5 “Our country’s basic systems” (the basic economic system, including the socialist market economy); “let the market play the decisive role in allocating resources and let the government play its role better” has been the official wording since 2013. In China, “supply and demand set prices, prices adjust supply and demand” is studied systematically in senior-high 思想政治 (the older Compulsory 1 《经济生活》 Lesson 2 “Changing prices”; the newer Compulsory 2 《经济与社会》 covers the market mechanism) — check unit names against the edition in use.

Notes: the market-lab curves and numbers are a teaching model (straight lines). The lemonade game uses demand = base customers × (1 − price ÷ highest price anyone will pay); “best possible profit” is computed over $0.25 price steps. Egg prices are real BLS monthly data; the bird-flu explanation is kept general (outbreaks in 2022–2023 and 2024–2025 sharply reduced the number of laying hens) without specific flock counts.

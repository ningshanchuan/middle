🧩 What is scarcity?

People’s wants are unlimited, but time, money, land, workers and machines are limited. That gap — limited resources, unlimited wants — is scarcity. Because of it, every person, business and country has to choose.

⚖️ Trade-off vs opportunity cost

A trade-off is everything you give up. The opportunity cost is only the most valuable one of those — the option that would have been your second choice.

🍱 There’s no such thing as a free lunch

A free ticket still costs your time — time you could have spent working, studying or sleeping. Economists shorten it to TINSTAAFL: There Is No Such Thing As A Free Lunch.

    👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

    Standards (Georgia Standards of Excellence, Social Studies, 2016): the grade 6–7 economic understandings rest on scarcity forcing every society to answer what, how and for whom to produce — SS6E1a / SS7E1a (compare how economic systems answer the three basic questions) and SS6E13a / SS7E10a (“income from work is limited” — scarcity in personal finance). The high-school Economics standards SSEF1 (scarcity, opportunity cost and trade-offs) and SSEF2 (marginal benefits and costs, incentives) state these concepts fully; this lesson is the middle-school on-ramp.

    China links: PEP Geography Grade 8, Vol. 1, Ch. 3 “China’s Natural Resources” (Sec. 2 “Land resources”: little farmland per person; “treasure and use every piece of land wisely and strictly protect farmland” is a basic national policy; Sec. 3 “Water resources”); 统编版《道德与法治》 Grade 9, Vol. 1, Unit 3 “Civilization and Home,” Lesson 6 “Building a Beautiful China” (resource and environment challenges, saving resources). In China, “opportunity cost” and the PPF are senior-high/university economics; this lesson introduces them at a middle-school level.

    Notes: the scenarios, prices and points in Missions 1–2 are invented for teaching; the PPF numbers come from a model (a quarter-ellipse, rounded to 100/98/92/80/60/0). The farmland figures are real World Bank data.

    🚩 Four signs of a scam (FTC)

    1) They pretend to be an organization you know (bank, government, delivery, game company). 2) They say there’s a problem or a prize. 3) They pressure you to act right now. 4) They tell you to pay a specific way — gift cards, wire transfers, crypto — or ask for passwords and codes.

    🛡️ What to do

    Don’t click the link or call the number in the message. Open the official app yourself, or call the number on the back of your card. Tell a parent first. In the US, report scams at ReportFraud.ftc.gov; in China, the National Anti-Fraud Center app helps, and a call from 96110 is an anti-fraud warning line — pick up.

    📱 Careful with QR codes too

    In China, paying by QR code is everywhere. Scammers paste fake codes over real ones or say “scan to claim your prize.” Check the payee’s name before you pay.

    Standards (Georgia Standards of Excellence, Social Studies, 2016): SS6E13 and SS7E10 “a basic principle of effective personal money management is to live within one’s income” (a income from work is limited; b a budget is a tool for planning spending and saving; c reasons for and benefits of saving; d uses and costs of credit); SS8E3 “explain the principles of effective personal money management” (a income is where personal financial management begins; b why households budget and the benefits; c why people save and the benefits; d how debt is used and its risks).     Math notes: the rocket deposits P = daily amount × 365 ÷ 12 each month at monthly rate i = annual rate ÷ 12, compounded monthly; over n months from the starting age to 65, FV = P × [(1 + i)ⁿ − 1] ÷ i (end-of-month deposits). Example: $5/day at 6% from 12 to 65 → about $96,725 put in, about $695,233 at the end. Credit simulator: monthly interest = balance × APR ÷ 12 (real cards usually use a daily rate, with similar results); minimum = the greater of 2% of the balance or $25; at $1,000 and 21.19%, the minimum takes about 71 months with about $750 of interest, $100/month about 12 months with about $110.

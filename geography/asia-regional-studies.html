Position and extent

Asia is the largest continent, mostly in the Northern and Eastern hemispheres. It spans about 11°S to 81°N and stretches east from 26°E across the 180° meridian to about 170°W. The Equator crosses some Southeast Asian islands.

Asia borders the Arctic Ocean to the north, the Pacific to the east, and the Indian Ocean to the south. It joins Europe to the west. The Suez Canal marks the boundary with Africa, the Bering Strait separates it from North America, and Oceania lies to the southeast.

Why so many climates?

Much of the Arctic coast has a tundra climate. Parts of equatorial Asia are hot and rainy year-round. The interior has many dry areas and a broad belt of temperate continental climate. The Tibetan Plateau is generally colder than lower land at the same latitude.

East Asia has temperate and subtropical monsoon climates. Tropical monsoon climate covers much of South Asia.

A monsoon (jìfēng, 季风) is a prevailing wind that shifts direction with the seasons. Land and sea heat differently, helping drive the circulation. Terrain and the source of moisture also affect rainfall.

Southeast Asia · Peninsulas, islands and a strait

Southeast Asia includes the Indochinese Peninsula and the Malay Archipelago. Much of the mainland has a tropical monsoon climate with wet and dry seasons. Near the Equator, tropical rainforest climates stay warm and wet year-round. Rice grows widely on plains and river deltas.

Exchange in rice country and port cities

On Bali, Indonesia, farmers share water through the subak irrigation system. Channels carry water between rice terraces on the slopes.

Southeast Asia has many Chinese migrants and people of Chinese descent. In port cities, families shared traditions with their neighbors over generations. In Singapore, Peranakan cooking blends Chinese techniques with spices and ingredients from the Malay region.

Japan · Islands, plates and ports

Japan’s four main islands are Hokkaido, Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu, roughly north to south. Japan lies near several plate boundaries, where subduction helps explain its frequent earthquakes and many volcanoes.

Domestic supplies of fossil fuels and many industrial raw materials are limited in Japan. Coastal ports make it easier to import materials such as iron ore and export finished goods such as cars. These are examples of the steps in processing trade: import, manufacture and export.

From Tang Chang’an to Nara

During China’s Tang dynasty, Japanese envoys traveled to China to study its institutions and culture. Planners of Heijō-kyō, in present-day Nara, drew on the layout of the Tang capital Chang’an, now Xi’an.

India · The rhythm of the rains

June through September is the main rainy season for much of India, when the southwest monsoon blows. The Ganges rises in the Himalayas, crosses northern India’s plains and joins the Brahmaputra in Bangladesh. The shared delta opens into the Bay of Bengal. Rice and wheat grow on the Ganges Plain.

Farms, software and medicines

Monsoon rains support irrigation and refill reservoirs. India also has software and IT service industries and a pharmaceutical industry that makes products such as generic medicines. Software services can be delivered remotely. Medicines are made, tested and shipped.

Russia · Across Eurasia

Russia is the world’s largest country by area and spans Europe and Asia. Its population is concentrated in the European west, where Moscow is located. The Ural Mountains mark part of the Europe–Asia boundary.

Siberia, east of the Urals, has major oil, gas and coal deposits. The Trans-Siberian Railway runs from Moscow across southern Siberia to Vladivostok, linking inland cities with the Pacific coast.

Why do three large rivers flow north?

The lower Ob, Yenisei and Lena all flow north toward the Arctic Ocean. Follow the arrows and compare the rivers with the railway. The rivers flow in the direction where the land gets lower.

Environmental issues, climate & where people live

Industry made Europe rich but also brought acid rain, smog and a nuclear disaster. Australia’s location and dry climate decide where its people live.

🌲Acid rain in Germany

Black Forest · Europe

In the 1970s–80s, sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides from coal power plants and factories formed acid rain. Trees in places like the Black Forest died (“Waldsterben”), lakes turned acidic and old stone churches were eaten away.

What is being done about it?

Fixes: scrubbers on power plants, catalytic converters on cars and less coal have greatly reduced acid rain.

🏭Air pollution in the United Kingdom

London · Europe

Britain started the Industrial Revolution and burned huge amounts of coal. The Great Smog of December 1952 killed thousands of Londoners and led to the Clean Air Act of 1956.

Tell me more

Today the main problem is traffic exhaust; London charges polluting vehicles in its Ultra Low Emission Zone.

☢️The Chernobyl nuclear disaster

Ukraine · Europe

On 26 April 1986, reactor 4 at the Chernobyl power plant exploded during a test. Radioactive dust blew over Belarus, Russia, Scandinavia and much of Europe.

Tell me more

About 350,000 people were moved, and a 30 km exclusion zone is still closed. Use the ☢️ slider on the globe to watch the cloud spread.

🌊The North Atlantic Drift warms Europe

Western Europe

London is about as far north as Canada’s Hudson Bay, yet its winters are much milder. A warm ocean current from the Gulf of Mexico (the North Atlantic Drift) and westerly winds bring mild, wet air.

Tell me more

Mild climate, the fertile North European Plain and navigable rivers make Western Europe crowded and great for trade.

🏙️Where do Australians live?

Australia

About 70% of Australia is dry or semi-dry outback. The Great Dividing Range catches moist Pacific air, so the east coast is rainy and the interior is dry.

Tell me more

So most Australians live in east and south-east coastal cities such as Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane.

🪸The Great Barrier Reef at risk

Coral Sea · Australia

Warmer seas cause coral bleaching; farm runoff adds mud and fertilizer, and crown-of-thorns starfish eat the coral.

Tell me more

The reef is a World Heritage Site, and reef tourism brings Australia billions of dollars and many jobs.

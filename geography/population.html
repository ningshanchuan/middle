⚠️ Common mix-up:A big population does not mean high density! The USA has many people (about 335 million) but so much land that its density is only about 34 per km². Bangladesh has about 170 million people squeezed into a small country: over 1,100 per km².

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports the Georgia Social Studies SS6G and SS7G elements that ask students to explain the impact of location, climate, physical characteristics and natural resources on population distribution (e.g., SS6G3, SS6G10, SS6G13, SS7G3, SS7G7, SS7G11), and supports discussion of migration push and pull factors. Country figures are approximate (about 2023); city figures are approximate UN urban-agglomeration estimates, interpolated between 1950, 2000 and 2025.

Ideas: ① Turn off Density colors, turn on Night lights, and have students name the brightest regions, then compare with the density map. ② Drag the year from 1950 to 2025 and count megacities: which continents grew fastest? ③ Use the calculator to compare Egypt’s average density with life along the Nile and discuss the limits of averages. ④ In the migration sim, add obstacles and discuss why some people stay even when conditions are hard.

How location shapes climate: latitude and altitude

The farther from the equator, the more slanted the sunlight and the colder it gets. The higher you climb, the colder it gets too (about 6.5 °C cooler for every 1,000 m). Pick a latitude, then climb the mountain to watch the temperature and plants change.

Six things that shape a place’s climate

📐

Latitude

The farther from the equator, the more slanted the sunlight and the colder the climate. This is the biggest factor.

🏔️

Altitude

About 6.5 °C cooler per 1,000 m, which is why La Paz and Lhasa are cool.

🌊

Distance from water

Oceans heat and cool slowly, so coasts have milder winters and cooler summers than continental interiors.

🔄

Ocean currents

The warm North Atlantic Current keeps London far milder than places at the same latitude in Canada.

⛰️

Mountains (rain shadow)

Moist air rises and rains on the windward side; the dry air sinks on the far side, creating a dry rain shadow.

💨

Prevailing winds

Winds from the sea bring moisture; winds from land or deserts are dry. Monsoon winds switch direction with the seasons.

Why is Sydney hottest in January?

Sydney is in the Southern Hemisphere. Earth is tilted as it circles the Sun: when the north leans toward the Sun it is summer there and winter in the south. The two hemispheres always have opposite seasons.

Why does Singapore’s temperature hardly change?

Singapore sits almost on the equator (1°N), where the Sun is high all year. There is no winter, only rainy months and rainier months.

Why is Moscow so much colder than Atlanta in winter?

Moscow is at 56°N, far closer to the pole than Atlanta (34°N), so winter sunlight is weak and brief. It is also deep inside a continent, with no ocean nearby to keep it mild.

Environmental issues & where people live

The land shapes where people live and work, and people’s activities change the land in return.

🏭Air pollution in Mexico City

Mexico · Latin America

About 22 million people live in a high mountain valley. Exhaust from cars and factories forms smog, and the mountains plus temperature inversions trap it over the city.

What is being done about it?

Solutions: “Hoy No Circula” driving limits, cleaner fuel, metro and rapid buses.

🌳Amazon deforestation

Brazil · Latin America

Since 1970 about one-fifth of Brazil’s Amazon rainforest has been cleared for cattle ranches, soybean farms, logging, mining and roads.

What happens next?

Effects: lost habitat for plants and animals, soil erosion, and more carbon dioxide in the air.

🛢️Oil pollution in Venezuela

Lake Maracaibo · Latin America

Venezuela has the world’s largest oil reserves. Old, leaking pipelines and wells spill oil into Lake Maracaibo and the coast.

What happens next?

Effects: fish die, fishing families lose income, and drinking water and health are at risk.

🌧️Acid rain in Canada

Ontario & Quebec · Canada

Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides from smelters, power plants and cars mix with water vapor to make acid rain. Much of it blows north from the USA.

Tell me more

Thin Canadian Shield soils can’t neutralize the acid, so lakes and forests suffer. The 1991 Canada–US Air Quality Agreement cut emissions.

🐟Pollution of the Great Lakes

Canada & USA

Factory waste, city sewage and farm fertilizer badly polluted the lakes, and invasive zebra mussels upset the ecosystem.

What is being done about it?

In 1972 Canada and the USA signed the Great Lakes Water Quality Agreement to clean them up together.

⛏️Mining the Canadian Shield

Central & northern Canada

The Shield holds nickel, iron, gold, copper and uranium, plus forests and hydropower. Using them creates jobs.

Tell me more

But mining, smelting and logging can pollute air and water and destroy forests and wildlife habitat.

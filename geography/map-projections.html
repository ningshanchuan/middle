Maps & Projections: Flattening the Earth

On many wall maps Greenland looks as big as Africa. Is it? (No: Africa is about 14 times bigger!) Earth is round, but maps are flat. What happens when you peel a globe and press it flat? Drag the slider to unwrap the globe into Mercator, Robinson and other map projections — and watch what gets stretched.

Scrub through time: how world maps got better

Why can a flat world map never be perfect?

Try pressing an orange peel flat: it has to tear or stretch. A round surface cannot be flattened perfectly, so every projection gives up something: shape, size, distance or direction.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia Social Studies SS6G / SS7G Map & Globe Skills: compare and contrast the uses of different maps; use a map key/legend; use graphic scales to determine distances; use inset maps. Suggestions: (1) play the “Peel the globe” story, then turn on the distortion circles and compare identical circles across projections; (2) turn on “Greenland vs Africa” and switch between Mercator and Gall–Peters — ask why Greenland looks huge on web maps; (3) click countries at different latitudes (Canada vs DR Congo) and record how many times bigger each looks; (4) in Measure mode, have students write the equation: map distance × scale = real distance.

Read any coordinate in four steps

Latitude and longitude are angles measured in degrees (°) from Earth's center. A coordinate is just the crossing point of two lines on a giant grid.

1

Latitude first

Latitude tells how many degrees north or south of the equator a place is: 0° to 90°.

33.7°N
2

Then longitude

Longitude tells how many degrees east or west of the prime meridian: 0° to 180°.

84.4°W
3

Always add a direction

Latitude needs N or S, longitude needs E or W. Only the 0° lines and the 180° meridian do not.

33.7°N, 84.4°W
4

Degrees → minutes

1 degree = 60 minutes (′), so 33.75° can be written 33°45′. Each degree of latitude is about 111 km (69 miles).

33°45′N
⬆️

Northern Hemisphere

North of the equator: North America, Europe, most of Asia — and Georgia.

⬇️

Southern Hemisphere

South of the equator: Australia, Antarctica, most of South America and part of Africa.

⬅️

Western Hemisphere

West of the prime meridian to 180°: all of North and South America.

➡️

Eastern Hemisphere

East of the prime meridian to 180°: Asia, Australia and most of Africa and Europe.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Latitude lines run side to side (east–west) but tell you how far NORTH or SOUTH you are. Longitude lines run up and down but tell you how far EAST or WEST you are. Also: some maps (including Chinese textbooks) split the Eastern and Western Hemispheres at 20°W and 160°E so that Africa and Europe are not cut in half; US classes usually use the prime meridian and the 180° meridian.

Scrub through a year: latitude changes the length of your day

Day length is calculated from the latitude with a formula (mid-month, sunrise to sunset), so it is approximate.

Why does Quito, on the equator, hardly change?

On the equator, day and night are each about 12 hours all year. The farther from the equator (the higher the latitude), the longer the summer days and the shorter the winter days.

How can Tromsø have 24 hours of daylight?

Tromsø is north of the Arctic Circle (66.5°N). In summer the Sun never sets there: the “midnight sun”. In winter it does not rise for weeks: the “polar night”.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia Social Studies SS6G / SS7G Map & Globe Skills: use latitude and longitude to determine location, compare hemispheres, and use a grid system. Suggestions: (1) play the “How coordinates work” story and have students name which line moves at each step; (2) turn on “See the angles” to stress that latitude and longitude are angles measured from Earth's center, not distances; (3) have students use the two sliders to find their own city (Atlanta ≈ 33.7°N, 84.4°W), then a city in the Southern and Eastern Hemispheres for contrast; (4) use the city game as a warm-up and race for the lowest average miss.

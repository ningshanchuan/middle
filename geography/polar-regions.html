Why is Antarctica colder?

Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station (interior)Annual mean

Utqiaġvik uses the 1991–2020 climate normal. The South Pole value comes from the station area’s management plan.

The Sun stays low at both poles. Antarctica’s thick ice sheet adds height to the interior plateau, where the air is colder. Snow and ice reflect much of the sunlight. The Arctic Ocean stores heat and moderates winter temperatures nearby.

Antarctica is cold, dry and windy. Its interior gets so little precipitation that it is a polar desert. Dense cold air can flow downhill off the ice sheet and accelerate into strong downslope winds.

Watch out In polar day the Sun stays up, yet it can still be cold because the Sun is low. Inside the polar circles, continuous day and night generally last longer nearer the pole.

Earth’s axis is tilted about 23.5° from a line perpendicular to the orbit. It keeps pointing in the same direction as Earth orbits. The short dashed lines mark the polar circles. At an equinox the Sun’s center is on the horizon at both poles in this model, which ignores refraction and the Sun’s apparent size.

Wildlife and Arctic communities

Find the right habitat

Tap an animal and then its polar home, or drag the card into place. Some species also live beyond the polar regions.

Common mix-up Wild polar bears live in the Arctic and nearby areas, including Canada’s Hudson Bay. Wild penguins do not live in the Arctic. Emperor penguins live in the Antarctic. Some other penguins live in warmer places, such as the Galápagos Islands near the equator.

Polar bears often hunt seals from sea ice. Emperor penguins feed at sea, and many colonies breed on sea ice attached to the coast.

Inuit life

Inuit are Indigenous Arctic peoples with homelands in places such as Alaska, northern Canada and Greenland. In many communities, people travel, fish and hunt on sea ice, using local knowledge alongside modern transportation and communications.

When sea ice forms late or melts early, some traditional routes become harder to travel safely. Antarctica has no Indigenous population. Research teams live and work at stations.

Some parts of the Arctic have fishery, oil and gas resources. Antarctica’s ice sheet stores vast amounts of fresh water and preserves clues to past climates. Resource use and environmental protection are closely linked.

Read an ice core

Layers of snow build up and compress into ice. Scientists drill out a cylinder called an ice core. Bubbles hold ancient air, the ice chemistry gives clues to temperature, and dust or volcanic ash can help identify past events.

Read the long-term trend

September ice extent rises and falls from year to year. For 1979–2026, NSIDC found a long-term decline of about 11.6% per decade relative to the 1981–2010 September average. Warming caused by increased greenhouse gases drives the long-term loss.

Bright sea ice reflects much of the sunlight. When it melts, the darker exposed ocean can absorb more solar energy and amplify warming.

Two kinds of ice Sea ice forms when seawater freezes and floats on the ocean. Ice sheets form from snow compressed on land. When land ice or its meltwater enters the ocean, sea level rises.

The Antarctic Treaty

The Antarctic Treaty was signed in 1959 and took effect in 1961. It reserves Antarctica for peaceful purposes and supports scientific investigation, cooperation and sharing results. Its Environmental Protocol prohibits mineral-resource activities except for scientific research.

Sea ice in both hemispheres grows and shrinks seasonally. Their long-term records show different patterns.

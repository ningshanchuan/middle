World Wars & the Great Depression: boom, bust and victory

In 1929 only about 3 in 100 American workers were jobless. By 1933 it was nearly 1 in 4. Ten years later, factories ran day and night, women drove rivets into bombers and kids collected scrap metal for the war. How did one country go from boom to bust — and then become the “arsenal of democracy”?

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Standards: Georgia Standards of Excellence for Social Studies (2016, still in use in 2026), Grade 5: SS5H2 “Describe U.S. involvement in World War I and post-World War I America” (incl. the Harlem Renaissance, Lindbergh, Ford); SS5H3 “Explain how the Great Depression and New Deal affected the lives of millions of Americans” (stock market crash, Hoover, FDR, the Dust Bowl, CCC, WPA, TVA, Social Security); SS5H4 “Explain America’s involvement in World War II” (Pearl Harbor, D-Day, the atomic bombs, the Holocaust, rationing, women in the workforce, Japanese American incarceration). Geography SS5G1 (locate Pearl Harbor, HI) and SS5G2; economics SS5E1 (opportunity cost in wartime choices). Note: the site catalog labels this lesson SS5H3–4; under the 2016 GSE it covers SS5H2–SS5H4.

China curriculum link: the national unified-edition World History, Grade 9 Vol. 2, Unit 3 (World War I and the post-war world) and Unit 4 (the Great Depression and World War II — the New Deal, fascist aggression, World War II). The “Meanwhile in China” lane matches Chinese History Grade 8 Vol. 1: the May Fourth Movement (sparked by the Shandong decision at the post-WWI peace conference), the 18 September 1931 Incident, the 7 July 1937 Incident and the nationwide War of Resistance, and victory in 1945. Lesson numbers vary by printing.

Notes: the map is schematic (modern borders; Alaska and Hawaii as insets). Great Migration totals are commonly cited approximations (about 1.6 million in 1910–1940 and about 5 million in 1940–1970); the share of Black Americans living in the South is from the census (about 89% in 1910, about 53% in 1970). Home-front numbers in the game (e.g. 3 gallons of gas a week, the number of victory gardens) are common approximations. The Holocaust, the atomic bombs and the incarceration of Japanese Americans are presented factually and calmly; the US Holocaust Memorial Museum’s student encyclopedia is a good companion for discussion.

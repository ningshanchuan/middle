Central idea & summary

You’ve just read an article. Can you say what it’s about in one sentence? The central idea is the point the author most wants you to remember. A summary shrinks the article — keeping the central idea and key support, cutting minor details and opinions.

Your mission: find the main idea of each paragraph and the central idea of a honeybee article, build an objective summary of a monarch butterfly article, then sort the details of the Great Molasses Flood.

Mission 1: the central-idea finder

One paragraph at a time: in the paragraph with the dashed outline, tap the sentence that sums up the whole paragraph (it isn’t always the first one!). After all five, choose the central idea of the whole article.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Standards: Georgia K-12 ELA Standards (2023): 6-8.T.T.2.a (expository techniques — main ideas, facts, statistics, key details, information from text features, sense of closure); K-12.P.EICC.3.d (summarize and visualize sections of a text to maintain understanding); 7.T.T.2.d (apply main idea, facts, statistics, key details in writing).

Also in China’s curriculum: Chinese (语文) reading asks students to summarize paragraph meaning (概括段落大意), the main content (主要内容) and the central idea (中心意思/主旨), and in expository units to locate key sentences and filter information (e.g. Grade 8 Vol. 1 Unit 5).

All articles are original. Facts: “one in three bites of food depends on pollinators” is a USDA figure; US annual colony losses come from yearly beekeeper surveys (recently roughly 30–55%; we say “a third or more”); von Frisch’s Nobel Prize was 1973; monarch areas are WWF-México/CONANP data. Molasses Flood (15 January 1919): about 2.3 million gallons, a wave about 25 ft high at about 35 mph, 21 deaths, about 150 injured — commonly cited, approximate values. The account avoids graphic detail.

Write a Story People Can’t Put Down

Why do some stories grab you in line one while others put you to sleep by page two? It isn’t luck. Writers use tools: hooks, dialogue, pacing and details that show instead of tell.

Your mission: spot the hooks in classic openings, build a story, fix dialogue punctuation, control time with the pacing slider and upgrade flat “telling” sentences into vivid scenes.

    📏 Four dialogue rules

    1Punctuation goes inside the closing quotation mark: “Run,” she said.

    2Use a comma between the speaker tag and the quote: She said, “Run.”

    3A ? or ! replaces the comma: “Why?” he asked.

    4New speaker, new paragraph.

    Chinese vs. English: Chinese puts a colon after the speaker (他说：“快跑！”). English uses a comma: He said, “Run!” If a quote is split by the speaker tag and the sentence isn’t finished, the second part starts lowercase.

    Mission 4: Pacing director

    Pacing is how much story time passes compared with how many words the reader reads. Slow down for big moments (write a scene); speed through boring parts (summarize). Drag the slider to pick the best pacing for each moment, then press “That’s a wrap!”.

🧠 Three steps to crack a word

1Find the root (the core meaning), then the prefix (often direction or “not”) and suffix (often the part of speech).

2Put the meanings together, often back to front: spect-ator = “one who looks”.

3Check it in the sentence: does it make sense? Not sure? Look it up.

Just like Chinese radicals! 氵 means water (河, 湖, 海); 讠 means speech (说, 话, 读). English roots work the same way: dict = say (predict, dictionary), aud = hear (audio, audience).

🔍 Five kinds of context clues

Definition

The sentence explains the word: look for “which means”, “is”, “called”, commas or dashes.

Synonym

A word with a similar meaning nearby, often after “or”, “also”, “like”.

Antonym (contrast)

An opposite nearby; signal words: but, unlike, however, instead.

Example

Examples follow; signal words: such as, for example, including.

Inference

Nothing says it directly; reason it out from the whole situation.

Where do English words come from?

English is a patchwork: Anglo-Saxons brought Germanic words, the Normans brought French in 1066, and scholars borrowed heavily from Latin and Greek.

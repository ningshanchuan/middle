📏 Six comma rules

1Items in a series: apples, pears, and plums

2After an introductory word, phrase or clause: After lunch, we left.

3Before FANBOYS (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) joining two independent clauses.

4Around extra, removable information: My dog, Biscuit, snores.

5Direct address: Maria, look!

6Dates and places: July 4, 1776, … / Atlanta, Georgia, …

Tip for Chinese speakers: Chinese has both 、and ，; English has only the comma. Chinese often joins two complete sentences with a comma. In English that is a comma splice, an error!

Mission 3: Fragment & run-on detective

Every sentence is a suspect. Is it complete, a fragment (missing a subject or verb, or only a dependent clause), a run-on (two sentences jammed together with nothing between) or a comma splice (two sentences joined by only a comma)?

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Georgia K-12 ELA Standards (approved 2023): 6-8.L.GC.2.b (use simple, compound, complex and compound-complex sentences to condense and combine ideas), 6-8.L.GC.2.a (apply syntax to comprehension); the 6-8.L.GC.1 Grammar, Usage & Mechanics (GUM) chart’s comma skills: series, coordinating conjunctions joining independent clauses, direct address, introductory phrases and clauses, and nonrestrictive elements (commas, parentheses, dashes); 6.T.SS.2.c (formal style uses complete sentences; fragments belong to informal style). China link: 人教版初中英语 sentence types (简单句、并列句、复合句) and the adverbial, object and attributive clauses taught in grades 8–9. English “clauses” have no exact counterpart in Chinese grammar, so make the idea explicit.

Notes: the lab treats a comma before an end-of-sentence dependent clause as a style note, not an error; in the comma fixer the serial (Oxford) comma is accepted either way (U.S. style guides differ).

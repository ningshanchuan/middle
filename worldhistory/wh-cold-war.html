The Cold War & Independence: two superpowers, a hundred new nations

In 1945 the United Nations had 51 members; by 1991 it had 166 — over a hundred nations in Asia and Africa won independence from colonial rule. At the same time the USA and the USSR faced each other with tens of thousands of nuclear warheads for more than 40 years, yet never fought each other directly. Why did this “cold” war never turn hot?

🎯 Your mission: drag the years and watch NATO, the Warsaw Pact and new nations appear on the map; in the Crisis Room, handle Berlin, Korea, Cuba and more without starting a nuclear war; guess who won each “first” of the space race; and watch the Berlin Wall rise and fall.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Georgia Standards of Excellence (2016): Grade 6 SS6H3 b–c (origins of the Cold War and the rise of superpowers; how the collapse of the Soviet Union ended the Cold War and led to German reunification); Grade 7 SS7H1 (Africa: nationalism and independence in Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa; Pan-Africanism) and SS7H3 (Southern and Eastern Asia: independence of India and Vietnam, Gandhi’s non-violence, Mao Zedong’s impact on China, the Korean War, US involvement in Vietnam). The catalog lists SS6H / SS7H.

China curriculum: unified-edition World History Grade 9 Vol. 2, Unit 5 — the Cold War (Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, NATO and the Warsaw Pact, the two-pole world) and new nations in Asia, Africa and Latin America (1960, “Year of Africa”); Unit 6 — the post-Cold War world (changes in Eastern Europe, the USSR’s collapse, the end of the two-pole world). The China strip matches Chinese History Grade 8 Vol. 2: founding of the PRC, the Korean War (抗美援朝, “resist America, aid Korea”), Zhou Enlai’s “seek common ground while reserving differences” at Bandung, “two bombs and one satellite”, restoration of China’s UN seat (1971) and Nixon’s visit (1972). Lesson numbers vary by printing.

Notes: the map is schematic (modern borders); East Germany is a simplified polygon. Independence years are the internationally used dates; some countries declared independence earlier than it was recognized (e.g., Indonesia 1945/1949, Vietnam 1945/1954). Warhead totals are approximate (Federation of American Scientists estimates). Crisis-card scoring follows how each crisis was actually defused, and the feedback gives several viewpoints; the page aims to present both superpowers and other nations fairly.

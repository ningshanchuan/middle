⚠️ Don’t mix up:The Roman Republic (509–27 BCE) is not the Roman Empire (27 BCE – 476 CE). The Republic was run by elected consuls and the Senate; only when Octavian (Augustus) became first emperor did the Empire begin. And when the Western Empire fell in 476, the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire lasted almost 1,000 more years, until 1453.

🗳️ Athens: who could vote?

Around 430 BCE, Attica had roughly 250,000–300,000 people, but only adult male citizens with Athenian parents — about 30,000–40,000 — could vote in the Assembly. Women, foreigners and enslaved people could not. Each dot ≈ 1% of the people.

Real example: the Pont du Gard

The aqueduct to Nîmes (southern France) is about 50 km long and drops only about 12.6 m — about 25 cm per km (roughly 1 in 4,000). Where it crosses the Gardon valley it rides on the Pont du Gard, about 49 m (160 ft) high — a 16-storey building. Water took over a day to arrive and the city got perhaps 35,000–40,000 m³ a day (estimates).

Grade 6 SS6G (Europe geography: locate the Mediterranean Sea, Italy, Greece etc.).

China link: Chinese textbook World History, Grade 9 Vol. 1, Unit 2 “Ancient European Civilizations”: Lesson 4 “Greek city-states and Alexander’s empire” (polis, Athenian democracy, the age of Pericles, Alexander’s campaign east), Lesson 5 “The Roman city-state and Roman Empire” (Republic, Twelve Tables, Punic Wars, Augustus and the principate, the split of 395, the fall of the West in 476, rise of Christianity), Lesson 6 “Classical culture of Greece and Rome” (architecture, philosophy, Roman law). Notes: the map is schematic — empire areas are rough polygons by period, drawn on land only, with no modern borders; “Roman land” uses common estimates (e.g. Taagepera) with interpolation, for comparison only. Athenian voter numbers, Roman population, Han wall length and aqueduct flow are common approximations. The aqueduct sim is simplified from the Nîmes aqueduct (≈50 km long, ≈12.6 m total fall); its terrain and costs are invented, and water speed is a rough estimate (≈0.6 m/s at 25 cm per km).

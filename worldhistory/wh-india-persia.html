Ancient India & Persia: cities, faiths and the Royal Road

4,500 years ago, cities on the Indus River had straight streets and covered sewers. 2,500 years ago, a Persian king’s message could cross 2,700 km (1,700 miles) in about a week. Ancient India gave the world Buddhism and the numerals we use (including zero); Persia built the biggest empire the world had yet seen. How?
Your mission: scrub through 3,000 years of empires, design the Persian king’s relay system, plumb a city in Mohenjo-daro, then lead a caravan along the Silk Road — make a fortune and carry Buddhism to China.

🇺🇸 Why is a Greek sentence about Persian couriers carved on a New York post office?

The Greek historian Herodotus admired the Persian riders: “Neither snow nor rain nor heat nor gloom of night stays these couriers from the swift completion of their appointed rounds.” Those words are carved on the James Farley Post Office in New York — the unofficial motto of US mail carriers. America’s Pony Express (1860–61) used the same trick: about 190 stations, roughly 3,100 km (1,900 mi) in about 10 days.

The brick secret: 1 : 2 : 4

Cities more than 1,000 km apart used baked bricks in the same proportions — thickness : width : length = 1 : 2 : 4. Someone set a standard, the way every LEGO brick fits every other one.

How big was the “Great Bath”?

About 12 m long, 7 m wide and 2.4 m deep (40 × 23 × 8 ft) — about as long as a school bus — sealed with bricks and natural tar. Its purpose is still debated; many scholars link it to religious bathing.

Hinduism and Buddhism: two faiths from one land

Both religions began in India and both teach rebirth and karma (actions have consequences) — but they answered differently about caste and about how to be freed from the cycle of rebirth. First look at the varna (caste) system, then compare.

The varna (caste) system — tap each level

Note: varna was fixed by birth, marriage between groups was usually forbidden and status was passed down; outside the four varnas were people treated as “untouchable”, who faced harsh discrimination. India’s constitution (1950) outlaws caste discrimination.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

China: Chinese textbook World History, Grade 9 Vol. 1, Unit 1 “Ancient civilizations of Asia and Africa”, Lesson 3 “Ancient India” (the Indus civilization, the caste system, the founding and spread of Buddhism; the side note that “Arabic” numerals were invented in ancient India). There is no separate Persia lesson: the Persian Empire appears in Unit 2, Lesson 4 “Greek city-states and Alexander’s empire” (Greco-Persian Wars, Alexander’s conquest of Persia). “Meanwhile in China” matches Chinese History Grade 7 Vol. 1 (Xia–Shang–Zhou, Spring and Autumn & Warring States, Qin–Han, Zhang Qian, the Silk Road, Buddhism reaching China).

Georgia (from memory — check against the current GSE and the 2024 revision): high-school World History SSWH1 (early river civilizations incl. the Indus Valley), SSWH2 (classical India: Mauryan and Gupta empires, origins and beliefs of Hinduism and Buddhism, Ashoka); Grade 7 SS7G geography of Southern and Eastern Asia / Southwest Asia (Indus, Ganges, Persian Gulf, Iran).

Notes: the map is schematic with approximate borders; no modern borders and no Chinese dynastic territories are drawn (use the standard maps in the China history lessons). Many dates are approximate: the Buddha’s dates are given as c. 563–483 BCE or c. 480–400 BCE; this lesson follows the Chinese textbook’s “6th century BCE”. Royal Road numbers (≈2,700 km, 111 stations, ≈90 days on foot) come from Herodotus; the 7–9 day courier time is a modern estimate. Prices in the Silk Road game are invented teaching values showing only what was cheap or dear where. Religion and caste are presented factually and respectfully; remind students that Hindu and Buddhist beliefs and practices today are very diverse.

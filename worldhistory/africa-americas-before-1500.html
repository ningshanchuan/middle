US-style example. Read a stack with one dot at the top, a shell in the middle, and a bar at the bottom. Name the digits, then use expanded form to find the decimal value.

Step 1. The digits are 1, 0, and 5 from top to bottom. Step 2. Multiply each digit by its place value and add.

1 × 400 + 0 × 20 + 5 × 1 = 405

Given: the three place digits are 1, 0, 5 from top to bottom. Find the value in decimal.

Work: expand by place value as shown above. Answer: the value is 405 in decimal.

Challenge: predict which places change when 399 increases by one, then reveal the steps.

All three places change. The ones carry into the twenties, which then carry into the four-hundreds.

399 + 1 = 19 × 20 + 20 = 20 × 20 = 1 × 400
In-class check · The Americas

How we know about everyday life

Read each clue and match it to the kind of evidence it describes.

  • Farmers fed cities. Near Tenochtitlan, people dug canals and built up fields. They could carry produce to market in canoes. In the Andes, people built terraces to grow crops on slopes.
  • Salt merchants crossed the Sahara, and booksellers in Timbuktu traded handwritten books. In the Andes, weavers used fibers from animals such as alpacas to weave intricate geometric patterns. Metalworkers shaped gold-silver alloy into small figures.
Look closely at the evidence
What can each source tell us?
  • Archaeology. Where an object was found and what was buried with it can help date it and explain its use. Imported porcelain shows that goods moved between places. Identifying the trader takes more evidence.
  • Manuscripts. Writing preserves what people studied. The astronomy manuscript pictured here shows that this knowledge was still being copied in 1733. For a claim about the years before 1500, look for earlier evidence as well.
  • Oral tradition. West African storytellers passed down accounts of Sundiata and Mali’s rise. Versions may emphasize different details. Historians compare them with one another, with written sources and with archaeological evidence.

After 1492

Choose a date to see how sustained contact across the Atlantic changed the Americas.

Distinguish a government from its people. Spanish colonial rule spread the Spanish language and Catholicism across much of the Americas. Indigenous communities carried their languages and traditions forward. People in the Andes still speak Quechua today.

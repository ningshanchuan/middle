The Age of Exploration: when the oceans joined the world

In 1405 China’s admiral Zheng He sailed into the Indian Ocean with more than 200 ships and over 27,000 sailors. Eighty-seven years later Christopher Columbus set out with 3 small ships and about 90 men. Yet it was the small fleets that ended up tying the Americas, Europe, Africa and Asia together. Why?

Zheng He’s treasure ship vs Columbus’s ship: how big is the gap?

Switch between the size recorded in old Chinese books and what many modern scholars estimate. Compare with a football field and a school bus.

Data (approx.): the Ming History records the largest treasure ships as “44 zhang long, 18 zhang wide” (at about 3.1 m per Ming zhang ≈ 137 m / 450 ft). Many scholars doubt a wooden ship could be that big and estimate about 60–75 m. Santa María ≈ 19 m (from replicas). A football field with end zones ≈ 110 m (120 yd); a school bus ≈ 12 m (40 ft).

⚖️ Two fleets, two goals

Why didn’t the giant fleet change the world, while the small ones did?

Zheng He’s fleet went to show the Ming emperor’s power, make friends and collect tribute gifts; the voyages stopped after 1433 and the Ming then limited sea trade. European voyages hunted for gold and spices and new trade routes, kept funded by kings and merchants — so fleet after fleet sailed, and conquest and colonies followed.

The Columbian Exchange: what went where?

After 1492 plants, animals, germs and people crossed the Atlantic in both directions — historians call it the Columbian Exchange. Drag each item to the direction it travelled.

🌽 Whose dinner changed?

Europe and China: American crops

Potatoes became a main food in Ireland, Germany and Russia. Maize (corn) and sweet potatoes reached China in the late Ming; they grow on hills and dry land and helped China’s population soar under the Qing (about 150 million around 1700, over 300 million by 1800, approx.). Chili peppers came from the Americas too — think Sichuan food!

The Americas: horses, cattle — and deadly germs

Horses changed hunting and life for Plains Indians. But smallpox, measles and other Old World diseases killed huge numbers of Native Americans, who had no immunity; in many regions the population fell by a very large share within a century (estimates vary widely).

A forced migration: the Atlantic slave trade

To grow sugar, tobacco and cotton on American plantations, European slave traders forced Africans across the Atlantic. From the 1500s to the 1860s about 12.5 million Africans were forced onto slave ships and about 10.7 million survived the crossing (Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database estimates). China’s textbook calls it the “triangle trade”.

China curriculum: unified-edition World History, Grade 9 Vol. 1, Unit 5 “Toward the Modern Age” — the lesson on new sea routes (Dias, da Gama, Columbus, Magellan; causes: gold and spices, Ottoman control of old trade routes; conditions: the round-Earth idea, ship and navigation technology, royal sponsors; result: the world began to become one connected whole) and the lesson on early colonial plunder (Spanish and Portuguese colonies, the triangle trade). Zheng He is in Chinese History Grade 7 Vol. 2, “Ming foreign relations” (seven voyages 1405–1433, as far as the east coast of Africa and the Red Sea). Lesson numbers vary by printing.

Notes: routes are schematic, drawn from the general course in the sources; Zheng He’s seven voyages are combined. Ship, crew and day counts are commonly cited approximations: Zheng He’s first voyage “62 treasure ships, over 27,800 men” (Ming History), with 200+ ships in total often quoted; da Gama left with about 170 men and about 55 came home; Magellan’s fleet had about 270 men, and only 18 reached Spain on the Victoria in 1522. Treasure-ship size is disputed, so both the recorded size and a modern estimate are shown. The mission’s day counts follow the history (Columbus, Canaries → Bahamas ≈ 36 days; Magellan’s Pacific crossing ≈ 98 days, about 19 deaths according to Pigafetta); rations and the scurvy model are simplified for teaching. Encounters, conquest and the slave trade are presented factually and from several viewpoints.

Renaissance & Reformation

In 1450 a book had to be copied by hand — a whole Bible took a scribe more than a year. By 1500 Europe had printed well over ten million books. In the same years painters, builders and scholars in Florence put people at the centre of their work, and one German monk’s 95 Theses split the Western Church in two. How did one machine, a few cities and a handful of people change Europe?
Your mission: watch the Renaissance and the printing press spread across the map, run Gutenberg’s press in a race against a scribe, judge which of Leonardo’s inventions would really work, find the vanishing point like Brunelleschi, and work out why the Reformation happened.

⚠️ Don’t mix up:The Renaissance was not just “copying ancient art”. Its core was humanism: putting people, not only God, at the centre — human dignity, talent and life in this world. And Gutenberg did not invent movable type first: Bi Sheng (毕昇) in Song-dynasty China made movable type in the 1040s, about 400 years earlier. Gutenberg’s breakthrough was metal type plus a press, which made printing fast and cheap.

🔍 Why does the scribe win for one copy but lose badly for many?

Setting type is slow: one page of the Gutenberg Bible has roughly 2,600 letters, placed one by one. But once the form is locked, each extra copy is just ink–press–peel, well under a minute. A scribe must start from scratch every time. The more copies you need, the bigger the press’s lead: set once, print hundreds.

Mission 3: find the vanishing point like Brunelleschi

1517: a monk, a list of theses and a printing press

In the early 1500s Church officials sold “indulgences”, said to reduce punishment for sins. Martin Luther, a monk and professor at the University of Wittenberg, objected and wrote his 95 Theses in 1517. Printing presses carried his words across the German lands within weeks — and the Reformation began.

📜 What did Luther argue?

People are saved by faith (“justification by faith”), not by buying indulgences; the Bible is the highest authority, and everyone should be able to read it in their own language. In 1522 he translated the New Testament into German — the first printing of roughly 3,000 copies sold out within months (approximate).

⛪ What happened next?

Western Christianity split into Catholic and Protestant churches (Lutheran, Calvinist/Reformed, Anglican…). The Catholic Church reformed itself at the Council of Trent (1545–1563). The Peace of Augsburg (1555) let each German prince choose his land’s religion. In 1999 the Catholic Church and the Lutheran World Federation signed a joint declaration on justification.

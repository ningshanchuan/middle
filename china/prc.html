New China: standing up, growing prosperous, becoming strong

In 1949 China was a poor farming country worn out by decades of war. About 70 years later it had its own space station and the longest high-speed rail network on Earth. What happened in between? This lesson is the big picture of the People’s Republic of China (PRC, 中华人民共和国), founded on 1 October 1949 — the way students in China learn it. Drag the year slider, launch a rocket, and see the real photos.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports the PRC part of the “Modern and Contemporary Chinese History” strand of the 2022 national history curriculum standard: the founding of the PRC, the War to Resist US Aggression and Aid Korea, land reform, the First Five-Year Plan and socialist transformation; the Third Plenary Session of the 11th Central Committee as a great turning point; reform and opening up; “one country, two systems” and the return of Hong Kong and Macao; achievements such as “two bombs and one satellite”, hybrid rice and crewed spaceflight. Textbook: national unified edition, Chinese History, Grade 8 Volume 2, Units 1–6.

Suggestion: use lesson steps 6–8 to build the opening-up pattern (special economic zones → coastal open cities → coastal open economic regions → inland). The year slider in the 3D hall lets students name achievements in time order; pair it with the year game.

This page is the overview of 1949–today for US middle-schoolers new to Chinese history: key terms carry pinyin and a meaning, and each event on the year slider has a “meanwhile in the US” anchor. The anchors only help students place the date; they are not textbook content. Four deeper lessons (linked at the top) cover each period. The lesson presents the topic as students in mainland China learn it, including China’s standard map (with Taiwan Province and the South China Sea islands); the map is schematic. A good discussion: pick one year on the slider and compare how a US textbook would describe it.

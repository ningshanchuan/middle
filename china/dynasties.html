Dynasty Challenge: 4,000 years on one map

The USA is about 250 years old. China’s Tang dynasty alone lasted 290 years — and from the first dynasty to the last is almost 4,000 years. Could you put them all in order?

Your mission: scrub 4,000 years of maps, measure every dynasty, learn the Dynasty Song — then beat the Timeline Challenge and earn the top exam rank, zhuàngyuan (状元).

Mission 1: 4,000 years, one map

Drag the year (2070 BCE → 1912 CE) or press “Next dynasty”. The territory morphs, the star marks the capital, and the card tells you each era’s big moment. Visit all 19 eras to finish the mission.

Why is Qin just a sliver on the strip?

The strip is drawn to scale. Qin lasted 15 years; Shang about 550. In 4,000 years a short dynasty is a hair-thin line — tap it to jump there.

How exact are these maps?

They are schematic (示意图): simplified borders, each showing roughly one moment (Tang about 669, Yuan about 1330, Ming about 1433, Qing about 1820). Your textbook’s maps have the details.

Mission 2: how long did each one last?

Every bar is to scale. The orange dashed line is the whole history of the USA (1776 → today, about 250 years). First play “Guess the length”: a bar is hidden — you guess how many years.

Data: China’s national history textbooks (Grade 7) and the standard “Chinese dynasties and Western years” table. Xia and Shang dates are approximate (from the Xia–Shang–Zhou Chronology Project); Qing counted from 1644. “≈” marks approximate values.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Curriculum: the 2022 national history standard (ancient China) asks students to know the order of the major dynasties and their key facts, and to read timelines and historical maps. This lesson reviews Grade 7 Book 1 (Units 2–4) and Book 2 (Units 1–3) of the national textbook.

Dates follow the textbook. Xia and Shang dates are approximate. Western Han is given as 202 BCE–8 CE (Wang Mang’s Xin began in 9 CE; Eastern Han in 25; some reference tables count Western Han as 206 BCE–25 CE). Eastern Zhou ended in 256 BCE, the Warring States period in 221 BCE. Lin’an fell in 1276, the last Song resistance in 1279. Qing took its name in 1636 and entered Beijing in 1644; the slider counts from 1644. Maps are schematic, traced roughly from textbook maps.

Beyond the textbook: the “Meanwhile…” world-history comparisons (pyramids, Olympics, Rome, Columbus, US independence) and the USA-length benchmark are added to give US students a time anchor.

Suggested use: (1) students try the Timeline Challenge cold and note their time; (2) use the map and length chart to draw out the big pattern — unity (Qin–Han) → division (Three Kingdoms to Northern & Southern) → unity (Sui–Tang) → coexisting regimes (Five Dynasties, Liao–Song–Xia–Jin) → unity (Yuan–Ming–Qing); (3) retry the challenge and compare.

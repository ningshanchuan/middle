Mission 1: cross the sea — the Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge

The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge (港珠澳大桥 Gǎng-Zhū-Ào Dàqiáo, opened 2018) links Zhuhai and Macao with Hong Kong across the mouth of the Pearl River. It is about 55 km (34 miles) long: bridges, two artificial islands and an undersea tunnel. Drag the slider and drive it.

Why does the road dive under the sea in the middle?

Near Hong Kong the crossing meets the Lingding Channel (伶仃航道), a shipping lane busy with giant ships heading to the ports of Guangzhou and Shenzhen. Right next to it is Hong Kong International Airport, and planes need empty sky on their approach. Try each design and watch the ships and planes.

How do you build a tunnel under the sea?

Workers dredged a deep trench in the seabed. Giant concrete tunnel sections were cast in a factory — 33 of them; a standard one is 180 m long (almost two football fields) and weighs about 80,000 tonnes. Tugs floated each one out, water was pumped in to sink it, and it was joined to the last one underwater. Then the trench was filled back in. The immersed tube is about 5.7 km (3.5 miles) long and reaches more than 40 m below the surface.

What are the artificial islands for?

In the middle of the sea, the road has to switch from bridge (above the water) to tunnel (below the seabed). Each island — about 100,000 m², like 14 football fields — is the switching station. To build them, workers drove 120 giant steel cylinders, each 22 m (72 ft) across, into the seabed to form the walls, then filled them with sand.

⚠️ Don’t mix up:“About 55 km” counts the link roads at both ends; the main sea crossing (bridge + islands + tunnel) is about 29.6 km. It joins Hong Kong with Zhuhai and Macao — it does not go to Shenzhen.

Mission 2: a railway on the roof of the world — the Qinghai–Tibet Railway

The Qinghai–Tibet Railway (青藏铁路 Qīng-Zàng Tiělù) runs 1,956 km (1,215 miles) from Xining in Qinghai to Lhasa in Tibet (Xizang). Its Golmud–Lhasa section opened on 1 July 2006. It is the world’s highest plateau railway; its top, the Tanggula Pass (唐古拉山口), is 5,072 m (16,640 ft) high — higher than any mountain in the lower 48 US states. Ride it first!

The big problem: permafrost (多年冻土)

About 550 km of the line sits on permafrost — ground that stays frozen all year and is full of ice. Only the top layer (the “active layer”) thaws in summer. If the embankment lets heat into the ground, the ice melts, the soil turns to mush and sinks, and the rails bend. Drag the years, switch the engineering tricks on and off, and compare.

🪨 How can a pile of rocks keep ground cold?

The embankment is built from fist-to-head-sized rocks with gaps between them. In winter, cold heavy air sinks into the gaps and pushes warm air out — carrying heat away from the ground. In summer, the hot air on top is light and stays put, so the rock layer acts like a blanket that keeps heat out. A one-way air conditioner with no power!

🌡️ How does a thermosyphon work?

A thermosyphon (热棒 rèbàng, “heat rod”) is a sealed steel pipe: the bottom is buried in the permafrost, the finned top sticks up into the air, and inside is liquid ammonia. In winter the ground is warmer than the air, so the ammonia boils at the bottom, the vapour rises, gives off its heat at the fins, condenses and trickles back down. In summer the air is warmer, so the cycle simply stops. Heat only goes out, never in. The Trans-Alaska Pipeline uses the same trick.

🌉 Why build bridges on flat ground?

Where the ground holds the most ice, engineers skipped the embankment and built bridges: deep piers reach stable frozen ground, and trains pass overhead without warming the surface. Animals such as Tibetan antelope can walk underneath. The Qingshuihe Bridge (清水河特大桥) is about 11.7 km (7 miles) long.

Mission 3: move gas from west to east — the West–East Gas Pipeline

Much of China’s natural gas is in the west — for example under the Tarim Basin in Xinjiang. But most gas is used in the crowded, factory-filled east. The first West–East Gas Pipeline (西气东输 Xī Qì Dōng Shū, “send western gas east”) runs about 4,000 km (2,500 miles) from Lunnan in the Tarim Basin to Shanghai — almost coast to coast across the USA. It went into full operation in 2004. Click a pipeline on the map.

🧑‍🔧 Chief engineer’s decision #3: where do the compressor stations go?

As gas flows, rubbing against the pipe wall slowly drains its pressure. A compressor station is a giant pump that pushes the pressure back up. Simplified rules: pressure drops 1 MPa every 100 km, starts at 10 MPa, and the gas stalls if it falls below 6 MPa. Click the pipe to place or remove a station, then open the valve. Fewer stations = more stars!

More mega-projects

📡

FAST, the “Eye of Heaven”

The Five-hundred-metre Aperture Spherical radio Telescope opened in 2016 in Pingtang County, Guizhou. It is the world’s largest single-dish radio telescope, set into a natural bowl in the karst hills, so engineers didn’t have to dig one.

How big is it?

The dish is 500 m (1,640 ft) across — about 30 football fields of surface — made of over 4,000 triangular panels that can change shape to point at different parts of the sky. Compare: Puerto Rico’s Arecibo dish was 305 m across; it collapsed in 2020.

✈️

Beijing Daxing International Airport

Opened in September 2019, its terminal is shaped like a phoenix spreading its wings. It sits on the Beijing–Hebei border, with high-speed rail and metro lines running right underneath — a hub for the Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei region.

Why the star shape?

Five piers spread out from a central hub, so even the farthest gate is only a short walk from security — a design aimed at saving passengers’ steps.

Prehistoric China: Origins of Civilization

Nobody could write yet — so how do we know who lived in China 1.7 million years ago? From teeth, bones, burnt seeds and broken pots. Words you will need (click to open)

Site and fossil 遗址 yízhǐ · 化石 huàshí

A site is a place where ancient people left things behind. A fossil is a bone or tooth turned to stone. With no writing, these are our evidence.

Yuanmou Man 元谋人 Yuánmóu rén

The earliest humans confirmed in China, about 1.7 million years ago. Named after Yuanmou county in Yunnan, in the far southwest.

Peking Man 北京人 Běijīng rén

Early humans who lived in caves near Beijing 700,000–200,000 years ago and used fire. “Peking” is an old spelling of Beijing.

Millet 粟 sù

A small, tough grain that grows in dry soil — the main food of north China for thousands of years. Rice (水稻 shuǐdào) needed the wet south.

Stilt house and pit house 干栏式 gānlán · 半地穴式 bàn dìxué

In the wet south, houses stood on wooden posts to stay dry. In the cold north, houses were dug half into the ground to stay warm.

Legend 传说 chuánshuō

A story handed down by word of mouth, like those of the Yellow Emperor (黄帝 Huángdì). Not proven fact — but often a memory of something real.

Three groups of early humans in China

Archaeologists study early humans through fossils and sites. Compare them: how did life improve step by step?

🦴

Yuanmou Man

about 1.7 million years ago · Yuanmou, Yunnan
  • The earliest early humans so far confirmed in China.
  • Two front-tooth fossils and stone tools were found.
🔥

Peking Man

about 700,000–200,000 years ago · Zhoukoudian, Beijing
  • Kept some ape features, but hands and feet had clearly different jobs; walked upright.
  • Made chipped stone tools; used natural fire and kept it burning.
  • Lived in groups by gathering and hunting.
🪡

Upper Cave Man

about 30,000 years ago · cave at the top of Dragon Bone Hill
  • Looked basically like modern people.
  • Could polish and drill; sewed clothes with bone needles.
  • Made and wore ornaments.
💡 Memory rhymeYuanmou’s first at 1.7 million; Peking Man had fire and chipped stone; Upper Cave sewed with bone needles; Hemudu: rice + stilt house; Banpo: millet + half-sunk house.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Aligned with the 2022 Compulsory Education History Curriculum Standard, Ancient China, “Prehistoric period” (in summary): know the early humans found in China (e.g. Yuanmou Man, Peking Man); learn about early farming life through sites such as Banpo and Hemudu; understand the origins of Chinese civilization through legends. Textbook: Unified (Tongbian) edition, Chinese History, Grade 7 Vol. 1, Unit 1 (Prehistoric period).

Core-competency ideas: (1) sense of time and space: the animated map and log timeline show the gap between “millions” and “thousands” of years; (2) evidence: tell fossils and sites (physical evidence) apart from legends; (3) historical materialism: drag the humidity slider in 3D and discuss how environment shaped houses and crops. Ask: what would go wrong if Banpo’s house were built at Hemudu?

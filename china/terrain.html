China’s Landforms: Three Great Steps

What if a whole country were built like a giant staircase? China is: high in the west, low in the east, dropping in three great steps from the Tibetan Plateau (青藏高原 Qīng–Zàng Gāoyuán, the “Roof of the World”, higher than almost every mountain top in the lower 48 states) down to the sea. First step

Average over 4000 m (13,000 ft): about as high as the top of Pikes Peak in Colorado. The Tibetan (Qinghai–Tibet) Plateau, including the Qaidam Basin.

Boundary with step 2: Kunlun – Qilian – Hengduan Mountains

Second step

1000–2000 m (3,300–6,600 ft): about as high as Denver, the “Mile-High City”. Inner Mongolian, Loess and Yunnan–Guizhou Plateaus; Tarim, Junggar and Sichuan Basins.

Boundary with step 3: Greater Khingan – Taihang – Wushan – Xuefeng

Third step

Mostly below 500 m (1,600 ft), like the US Midwest and East Coast. Northeast, North China and Middle–Lower Yangtze Plains; Liaodong, Shandong and Southeast Hills.

Further east lies the shallow continental shelf.

🌧️

Brings rain inland

Sloping down towards the ocean lets moist sea air move far inland and drop rain.

🚢

Rivers flow east

Many great rivers flow from west to east, linking the coast with the interior.

⚡

Rich in hydropower

Rivers drop sharply where steps meet, holding huge water power (e.g. the Three Gorges Dam near the step 2/3 boundary).

🏔️ How high is the Tibetan Plateau, really?

Its average height is over 4,000 m (13,000 ft). The highest peak in the lower 48 states, Mount Whitney, is about 4,421 m (14,505 ft): barely higher than the plateau’s ordinary ground. And on the plateau’s edge stands Mount Qomolangma (珠穆朗玛峰 Zhūmùlǎngmǎ Fēng, known in English as Everest) at 8,848.86 m (29,032 ft).

🧭 Plateau, basin, plain, hills: what is the difference?

A plateau (高原 gāoyuán) is high but fairly flat on top, like the Colorado Plateau. A basin (盆地 péndì) is a low bowl ringed by mountains, like the Great Basin in Nevada. A plain (平原 píngyuán) is low and flat, like the Great Plains. Hills (丘陵 qiūlíng) are low and gently rolling. Mountains (山地 shāndì) are high and steep, like the Rockies.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports the 2022 Geography Curriculum Standard, theme “Understanding China”: use relief maps and cross-sections to describe the main features of China’s landforms and terrain, and explain how terrain affects climate and rivers. Textbook: PEP Geography Grade 8 Vol. 1, Chapter 2 “China’s Natural Environment”, Section 1 “Terrain and Landforms”.

Ideas: (1) hide labels and let students find the three steps in the elevation colours, then check with step colours; (2) cut at 32°N and 40°N and compare the two profiles; (3) stress that the Qaidam Basin is on step 1 while the Sichuan Basin is on step 2.

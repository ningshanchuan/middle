Qin & Han: building a unified empire

Seven kingdoms with seven kinds of money, different writing and even different widths of cart axle: how do you turn them into one country? In 221 BCE the king of Qin (秦 Qín, said “chin”) did it in about ten years and called himself First Emperor. The Han (汉 Hàn) dynasty that followed lasted over 400 years and was about as big and as populous as the Roman Empire on the other side of the world. Words you will need (click to open)

Emperor 皇帝 huángdì

A new title invented in 221 BCE by Qin Shi Huang (秦始皇 Qín Shǐhuáng, “First Emperor of Qin”). China had emperors for the next 2,132 years, until 1912.

Commanderies and counties 郡县制 jùnxiàn zhì

The empire was cut into districts run by officials the emperor hired and fired — like US states and counties, except that nobody was elected and nothing was inherited.

Han 汉 Hàn

The dynasty founded by Liu Bang in 202 BCE. Most Chinese people today call themselves “Han people” and call their writing “Han characters”.

Xiongnu 匈奴 Xiōngnú

Horse-riding herders of the northern grasslands (today’s Mongolia) who raided the farming lands to the south. The Great Wall was built to keep them out.

Western Regions 西域 Xīyù

The Han name for the lands west of the Jade Gate pass: today’s Xinjiang and Central Asia beyond.

Silk Road 丝绸之路 sīchóu zhī lù

Not one road but a chain of trade routes about 7,000 km (4,300 miles) long, from the Han capital to the Roman Empire, which the Chinese called Daqin (大秦 Dàqín).

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Aligned with the 2022 national History Curriculum Standard (compulsory education), ancient China: the founding and consolidation of a unified multi-ethnic state under Qin and Han; textbook: national unified edition, Grade 7 Vol. 1, Unit 3 (Lessons 9–15: Qin unification, the late-Qin uprisings, the Western Han and the Wen–Jing era, Emperor Wu, the Eastern Han, the Silk Road, Han science and culture).

Suggestions: (1) use the slider to let students name the problems before unification (different coins, scripts and measures; separate walls) before summarizing why Qin’s measures mattered; (2) use the Qin/Ming toggle to correct the idea that today’s brick wall is the Qin wall; (3) map steps 5–7 build the chain Zhang Qian → Silk Road → Protectorate of the Western Regions; (4) after the game, ask which road (north/south) students chose and what goods travelled each way.

Three Kingdoms to the Northern & Southern Dynasties: division and blending

What happens when an empire falls apart — and stays in pieces for almost 400 years? After the Han dynasty collapsed, China split into rival kingdoms from about 200 to 589 CE. The Roman Empire was breaking up in the very same centuries. Rome never came back together; China did. Along the way, millions of people moved, the south filled with farms and cities, and the peoples of the north blended into one. Drag the era slider and watch the map change colour again and again.

Words you will need (click to open)

Three Kingdoms 三国 Sānguó

Wei (魏 Wèi) in the north, Shu (蜀 Shǔ) in the south-west and Wu (吴 Wú) in the south-east, 220–280 CE. The novel, films and video games called “Three Kingdoms” are set here.

The two Jin dynasties 晋 Jìn

The Western Jin (266–316) briefly reunited China; after it fell, the Eastern Jin (317–420) held only the south.

Northern and Southern Dynasties 南北朝 Nánběicháo

420–589: one line of dynasties ruled the north and another ruled the south, facing each other across the Huai and Yangtze rivers. For 170 years there were, in effect, two Chinas side by side.

Xianbei 鲜卑 Xiānbēi

A herding people from the northern grasslands. One Xianbei clan founded the Northern Wei dynasty and united north China.

Jiangnan 江南 Jiāngnán

“South of the River” — the lands south of the Yangtze. Once thinly settled, they boomed when refugees from the north arrived with their tools and skills.

Ethnic blending 民族交融 mínzú jiāoróng

Peoples living side by side trading, marrying and borrowing each other’s food, clothes and words until the lines blur. Emperor Xiaowen (孝文帝 Xiàowéndì) pushed it on purpose.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Aligned with the 2022 national History Curriculum Standard (compulsory education), ancient China: political division and the blending of peoples in the Three Kingdoms, Jin and Northern & Southern Dynasties; textbook: national unified edition, Grade 7 Vol. 1, Unit 4 (Lessons 16–20).

Suggestions: (1) use the era slider to let students summarize the pattern “divide – unite – divide – unite”; (2) compare the two migrations in steps 6–7 (peoples moving into the Central Plains vs northerners moving south) and analyse why the south developed (labour and skills, natural conditions, relative peace); (3) after the reform game, discuss why the reform promoted the blending of peoples; (4) extend the sort to cultural achievements of north and south.

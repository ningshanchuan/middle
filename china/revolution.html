The New Democratic Revolution: 1919–1949

In October 1934 more than 80,000 soldiers walked out of the hills of southeast China, hunted by an army many times their size. A year and about 12,500 km (7,800 miles) later — like crossing the United States on foot almost three times — the survivors reached safety. Fourteen years after that, their party founded the People’s Republic of China. How did the side that was running for its life end up winning the whole country? Drag the years, march the route and find out.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports the New Democratic Revolution part of the “Modern and Contemporary Chinese History” strand of the 2022 national history curriculum standard: May Fourth, the founding of the CPC, KMT–CPC cooperation and the Northern Expedition, the road of surrounding the cities from the countryside, the Long March, Japan’s invasion and the War of Resistance, and the victory of the People’s War of Liberation. Textbook: national unified edition, Chinese History, Grade 8 Volume 1, Unit 4 (Lessons 13–14) to Unit 7 (Lessons 15–24).

Suggestion: use lesson steps 5–6 together with the 3D relief map; ask students to name the main places and dates of the Long March, and connect with Mao Zedong’s poem “The Long March” from their Chinese textbook. Use the ordering game as a warm-up; the sort game builds the four-period framework.

The English is written for US middle-schoolers new to Chinese history: names carry characters, pinyin and a meaning (see “8 words”), with familiar anchors (miles, US states, “Meanwhile in the US”). The lesson presents the period as students in mainland China learn it, including the textbook’s terms and evaluations; a useful discussion is to compare it with how a US textbook covers the “China theatre” of World War II. The year-slider map is a schematic partial map; the day and distance counters under the 3D map are interpolated between dated places and are only approximate.

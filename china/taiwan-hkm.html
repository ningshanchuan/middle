Taiwan Province, Hong Kong & Macao

How does a city get bigger without moving its borders? Hong Kong and Macao build brand-new land out of the sea. Across the Taiwan Strait, Taiwan Island hides mountains almost twice as tall as anything in the eastern US, and the Tropic of Cancer runs right through its middle.

Taiwan Province (short name 台 Tái) is made up of Taiwan Island, the Penghu Islands, Diaoyu Dao, Chiwei Yu and other islands. Taiwan Island is China’s largest island. ✅ Mission checklist

    ☆☆☆
    🌡️ One line, two climates

    The Tropic of Cancer (about 23.4°N) crosses the middle of the island. North of it: subtropical monsoon climate. South of it: tropical monsoon climate. Winters are warm, summers hot and rainy. Taipei’s January averages about 16 °C (61 °F), like a winter day in central Florida (around Orlando).

    🌧️ Why so much rain?

    Sea on every side. The summer monsoon, the winter northeast monsoon and typhoons all carry moist air, and the high mountains force that air upward, so the windward slopes get soaked. Most of the island gets over 2,000 mm (79 in) a year. Huoshaoliao (火烧寮) in the northeast averages over 6,000 mm (236 in), one of the wettest places in China. Seattle gets about 39 in.

    🍍 What grows on the “Treasure Island”?

    On the warm, wet western plains, rice gives two or three harvests a year, plus sugar cane and tea. So many tropical fruits grow here (bananas, pineapples, mangoes…) that Taiwan is called a “home of fruit”. The mountains are thick with forest, including camphor trees (camphor output was once among the world’s highest), and the seas are rich fishing grounds.

    💻 Industry: from export factories to computer chips

    Little land, many people, few raw materials, so Taiwan built an export economy: import materials, process them, ship products out. Kaohsiung set up an export processing zone; the Hsinchu Science Park led the growth of electronics, especially semiconductor chips, which go into phones and computers worldwide. Kaohsiung and Keelung are the two main ports, and most cities and railways sit on the western plain.

    🤝 Two sides of the strait, one family

    Most people in Taiwan have family roots in Fujian and Guangdong. The two sides speak the same languages (Mandarin, Minnan/Hokkien, Hakka), celebrate the same festivals (Spring Festival, Mid-Autumn) and honour Mazu, a sea goddess whose temple tradition began on Meizhou Island in Fujian. Trade across the strait is huge, and since 2008 there have been direct mail, trade and flights, the “Three Links” (三通).

    Mission 2 · Build land out of the sea

    Hong Kong (returned to China on 1 July 1997) and Macao (returned on 20 December 1999) are China’s two special administrative regions (特别行政区), run under “one country, two systems”. Both are crowded and short of flat land, so they keep making new land by filling in the sea: 填海造地 (tián hǎi zào dì). Drag the time slider and watch the coastline march out into the water.

    🏭 “Shop in front, factory behind”: place the jobs

    A US store orders 100,000 toy robots. Who does each job: Hong Kong (the “shop in front”) or the Pearl River Delta (the “factory behind”)?

    🏙️ Hong Kong: the “Pearl of the Orient”

    Hong Kong is made up of Hong Kong Island, Kowloon and the New Territories: about 1,100 km² (430 sq mi) and about 7.5 million people. Victoria Harbour is deep and sheltered, and Hong Kong is a world centre of finance, trade and shipping, and a famous place to shop.

    ✈️ An airport built in the sea

    Hong Kong International Airport opened in 1998 on a platform made by flattening two small islands (Chek Lap Kok and Lam Chau) and filling the sea around them. In 2024 a third runway, also on reclaimed land, came fully into use.

    🎡 Macao: a world centre of tourism and leisure

    Macao is the Macao Peninsula plus Taipa and Coloane islands (the sea between those two has been filled in to make Cotai, 路氹城). It covers only about 33 km² (13 sq mi), with about 680,000 people, one of the most crowded places on Earth. Tourism is the backbone of its economy, and its Historic Centre became a World Heritage Site in 2005.

    🌉 The bridge and the Greater Bay Area

    The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macao Bridge, about 55 km (34 mi) long, opened in 2018. It is a chain of bridges, an undersea tunnel and artificial islands. Together with nine cities in Guangdong’s Pearl River Delta, Hong Kong and Macao form the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area. Most of Hong Kong’s fresh water comes from the Dongjiang (East River) in Guangdong, and much of its food comes from the mainland too.

    👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

    Supports the 2022 Compulsory-Education Geography Curriculum Standard (“Understanding China”, regional geography): use maps and data to describe a region’s location and extent, explain how natural features shape production and life, and use Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan to show links within and between regions. Textbook: PEP Geography Grade 8 Vol. 2, Chapter 7 “The Southern Region”, Section 3 “Pearls of the Orient: Hong Kong and Macao” and Section 4 “Sacred Territory of the Motherland: Taiwan Province” (section numbering varies slightly by edition).

    Suggested flow: ① finish Mission 1 on the 3D map, stressing “high in the middle, low around the edges” and the climate on each side of the Tropic of Cancer; ② compare Hong Kong and Macao with the reclamation slider and discuss why reclaim land, and what it costs (marine life, shipping channels); ③ after “shop in front, factory behind”, have students name each side’s advantages; ④ play Geography Detective in teams.

    Beyond the textbook (for reference): the US state comparisons; the reclamation totals by year are rough estimates compiled from public sources (Hong Kong ≈ 70 km² reclaimed by about 2016; Macao ≈ 11.6 km² in 1912 and ≈ 33.3 km² in 2024), with in-between years interpolated; the reclamation maps are schematic, not accurate maps. The 3D relief is generated from the positions of the main ranges and the heights of peaks such as Yushan. Semiconductors, the 2024 third runway and the 2008 “Three Links” are additions beyond the textbook.

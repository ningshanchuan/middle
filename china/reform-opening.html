History of China · 1978–2012

How did a fishing-village county become a city bigger than New York?

In 1980 Shenzhen (深圳 Shēnzhèn), next door to Hong Kong, was a county of farms and fishing villages with about 330,000 people. Forty years later it had 17 million — twice New York City. The change began in December 1978, when China’s leaders chose “reform and opening up” (改革开放 gǎigé kāifàng): reform the economy at home, open the door to the world. ⚠️ Common mix-up:The order of opening is: special economic zones (1980) → open coastal cities (1984) → coastal open economic regions (1985) → inland. 👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

This lesson follows China’s national curriculum (History Curriculum Standard for Compulsory Education, 2022 edition) and the national unified textbook Chinese History, Grade 8 Volume 2, Unit 3 “The road of socialism with Chinese characteristics”. Periodisation, terms and evaluations are the textbook’s. The English text adds pinyin and US/world anchors for American middle-schoolers; these anchors are not part of the Chinese textbook.

Suggestion: let students drag the slider and work out the pattern for themselves (points → a line along the coast → whole regions → inland), then check it with the ordering game. GDP figures are rounded official statistics and Shenzhen’s population figures are rounded census/yearbook data, shown for the trend only; ordinary 3D blocks are schematic. Maps are schematic.

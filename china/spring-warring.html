Spring & Autumn and Warring States: Change & the Hundred Schools

What happens when a king is too weak to control his own lords? In China it meant 550 years of rivalry and war (770–221 BCE) — and the greatest burst of new ideas in Chinese history. Confucius lived in this age, at about the same time as the Buddha in India and just before Socrates in Greece. Start with a 2,200-year-old water system that still works today, then drag the year slider and watch more than a hundred states shrink to seven, then to one.

Words you will need (click to open)

Spring & Autumn · Warring States 春秋 Chūnqiū · 战国 Zhànguó

The two halves of this age: 770–476 BCE and 475–221 BCE. “Spring and Autumn” is the title of a history book; “Warring States” says exactly what it was.

Lords and their states 诸侯 zhūhóu

Rulers who had been given land by the Zhou king. Each ran his own small state with its own army — a bit like a governor who stops taking orders from Washington.

Hegemon 霸主 bàzhǔ

The top lord: the one strong enough to give orders to all the others. Several lords won the title, one after another.

Reform 变法 biànfǎ

Literally “changing the laws”. States rewrote their rules to get stronger; the reforms of Shang Yang (商鞅 Shāng Yāng) in Qin went furthest.

Hundred Schools of Thought 百家争鸣 bǎijiā zhēngmíng

Thinkers of many “schools” arguing about how to live and how to rule: Confucians, Daoists, Mohists, Legalists and more.

Dujiangyan 都江堰 Dūjiāngyàn

An irrigation system built about 256 BCE that tames a river without a dam. It still waters farmland in Sichuan today.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Aligned with the 2022 Compulsory Education History Curriculum Standard, Ancient China, “Spring and Autumn and Warring States” (in summary): understand the social changes of the period (rivalry among the lords, Shang Yang’s reforms, etc.); know water projects such as Dujiangyan; learn about Laozi, Confucius and the Hundred Schools, and the flowering of thought. Textbook: Unified edition, Chinese History, Grade 7 Vol. 1, Unit 2 (lessons on the Spring and Autumn period, changes in the Warring States, and the Hundred Schools).

Ideas: (1) 3D Dujiangyan: let students predict which river carries more water in each season, then test the “60/40 split” with the slider; switch on Sand and discuss why the Feisha Weir flushes sand by itself, showing how ancient builders worked with nature. (2) Step 5 of the animation links Shang Yang’s reforms to Qin’s unification (cause and effect). (3) Extension: the unit also covers iron farm tools and ox-ploughing; connect these to “private ownership of land” in the reforms.

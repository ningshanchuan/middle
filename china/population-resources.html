📏 What is the Heihe–Tengchong Line?

In 1935 the geographer Hu Huanyong (胡焕庸) drew a straight line from the city of Heihe (黑河 Hēihé) in the far northeast to Tengchong (腾冲 Téngchōng) in the far southwest. It is also called the Hu Line. Southeast of it the land is lower, wetter and has been farmed for thousands of years. Northwest of it lie high plateaus, mountains and deserts. The US has a similar divide: far more Americans live east of the 100th meridian, where there is enough rain to farm without irrigation.

📉 Is China still growing?

Growth was fast from the 1950s to the 1970s. From the 1970s China made family planning (计划生育 jìhuà shēngyù) a national policy and growth slowed. Growth then stalled, and since 2022 the population has been shrinking slightly and getting older, so the policy has been relaxed: two children for every family from 2016, three from 2021.

56 ethnic groups: mixed together, with home regions of their own

China is one country with 56 officially recognised ethnic groups. The Han (汉族 Hànzú) are by far the largest; the other 55 are called ethnic minorities (少数民族 shǎoshù mínzú). After centuries of contact they live “mixed together over large areas, in compact communities over small ones” (大杂居、小聚居 dà zájū, xiǎo jùjū).

Where they live

The Han live mainly in the east and centre; minorities mainly in the southwest, northwest and northeast. Yunnan has the most ethnic groups of any province.

🏛️

Regional ethnic autonomy

Areas where minorities live together practise regional ethnic autonomy. The 5 autonomous regions: Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang Uygur, Guangxi Zhuang, Ningxia Hui and Tibet (Xizang).

🤝

Ethnic policy

All ethnic groups are equal; China upholds ethnic unity and common prosperity, and a shared sense of the Chinese nation.

Natural resources: a big pie, cut into 1.4 billion slices

Natural resources are either renewable (land, water, living things, climate) or non-renewable (minerals). China’s totals are among the largest in the world. But divide them among 1.4 billion people and each share is small: “large in total, small per person” (总量大、人均少).

Water: in the wrong place at the wrong time

  • In space: more in the south and east. The north has much farmland but little water.
  • In time: more in summer and autumn, less in winter and spring, and it varies a lot from year to year.
  • Solutions: move water between river basins to fix “wrong place” (the South-to-North Water Diversion, 南水北调 Nánshuǐ Běidiào); build reservoirs to fix “wrong time”; save water and stop pollution.
  • The US does this too: California’s aqueducts carry water from the wet north and the Colorado River to dry Los Angeles.
⚠️ Common mix-up:Diversion fixes the uneven distribution in SPACE; reservoirs fix it in TIME. Don’t swap them.

East Route

Pumps water from the lower Yangtze at Jiangdu (Yangzhou, Jiangsu) and lifts it step by step along the ancient Grand Canal, through several lakes, under the Yellow River and on to Tianjin, over 1,100 km (700 miles). A branch goes east to the Jiaodong Peninsula. Opened 2013.

Middle Route

Takes water from the Danjiangkou Reservoir on the Han River, runs north along the eastern foot of the Funiu and Taihang Mountains, crosses the Yellow River and flows mostly by gravity to Beijing and Tianjin. Opened 2014.

West Route (planned)

Planned to move water from the upper Yangtze (Tongtian, Yalong and Dadu Rivers) into the upper Yellow River for the northwest. It must cross the Bayan Har Mountains, so it is still being planned.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports the 2022 Compulsory-Education Geography Curriculum Standard (“Understanding China”): describe China’s population size and distribution and the distribution of ethnic groups using maps and data; state the basic features of China’s natural resources and give examples of the distribution and wise use of water and land. Textbook: Grade 8 Vol. 1, Ch. 1 §2 Population, §3 Ethnic Groups; Ch. 3 Natural Resources (features, land, water).

Ideas: ① toggle Density/Population and ask why Xinjiang is huge but sparsely populated; ② show the Heihe–Tengchong Line and explain the pattern with terrain, climate and history; ③ compare the three water routes: their sources, the areas they cross and their difficulties (pumping for the East, gravity for the Middle, high mountains for the West).

China’s New Era: chasing the “Chinese Dream”

In 2012 almost 99 million people in China’s countryside lived in poverty — more people than live in California, Texas and Florida put together. Less than ten years later that number was zero, and China had built its own space station. What happened in between? Drag the time slider and find out.

Time machine: from 2012 toward 2049

Drag the slider (or tap the chart, or press Play). The chart shows the number of rural people in poverty falling year by year; the card tells you what happened that year. The last two stops — 2035 and 2049 — are goals, not events that have happened.

    👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

    This lesson follows China’s national History Curriculum Standard for Compulsory Education (2022 edition), strand “Contemporary Chinese History — socialism with Chinese characteristics in the new era”, and uses the facts, dates and wording of the national unified-edition textbook Chinese History, Grade 8 Volume 2: the lesson “Striving to realise the Chinese Dream” (Unit 3) and the lesson on achievements in science and culture (lesson numbers differ slightly between printings). It presents the topic as students in mainland China learn it; the English is written for US middle-schoolers new to Chinese history, with pinyin and meanings for key terms.

    Data notes: poverty figures are the official count of rural poor under China’s current standard (National Bureau of Statistics); high-speed-rail lengths are rounded year-end figures. Events from 2023–2024 (C919 enters service, Chang’e-6) are newer than some printings of the textbook and are included as extension material.

    Suggested use: have students drag the time slider and answer “In which year did the poverty count reach zero? When was the first centenary goal met?”; then assemble the station in 2021–2022 order; finish with the sort game to tell the five development ideas apart. Discussion prompt: compare Tiangong with the ISS, and BeiDou with GPS.

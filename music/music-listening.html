      How many moods can one tune have?

      Keep tempo and dynamics steady as you compare major and minor. Then move one slider and replay one version. What changes? Does your first impression still fit?

      Each block is a note. Its width shows duration and its height follows amplitude. The current note lights up.

      Lowering the third from E to E♭ gives this tune a minor sound. Listeners familiar with Western music may hear it as sad. Does it feel different when you speed it up or play it louder?

      Hear the interval

      C to E spans four semitones. C to E♭ spans three. Hear each pair together and compare which pitches sit closer.

      How long is a beat? 课外拓展 · Enrichment

      Divide 60 seconds per minute by the tempo in beats per minute to get seconds per beat. Multiply by the number of beats for the phrase length.

      Worked example at 120 beats/min

      Given a tempo of 120 beats/min and a phrase of 5.5 beats.

      Find the time per beat and the length of the phrase.

      Divide (60 s/min) by (120 beats/min) to get 0.50 s/beat. Multiply 5.5 beats × 0.50 s/beat to get 2.75 s.

      Set the tempo to 60 beats/min. Predict the phrase length, then check the calculation above and listen.

      Creative challenge and example

      Pair Moonlit Terrace at the top of the page with the opening of Debussy’s Clair de lune. Where does the painter place the light? Where does the piano fade? Use one detail from each to describe the mood of the night.

      Pick a night scene and make two soundtracks. Tap four beats or use the demo tune. Start with a quiet version, then change just the tempo or dynamics to suggest motion. Can a listener say which change altered the mood?

      Example: At the start of Clair de lune, the soft piano fades like light on water. To make the scene grow tense, I’d keep the pitches and speed up.

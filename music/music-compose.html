Thousands of pop songs use the very same four chords. Why do those four work so well — and how do songwriters put a melody on top that you can’t get out of your head?

Mission 1: the chord lab

A chord = three notes at once, skipping every other note (1-3-5). The Roman numeral tells you which scale step it starts on: I is home, V wants to go home, IV is a walk outside, vi is home’s sad cousin. Tap a bar slot, then a chord, to build a four-bar progression.

    Why does V want to go back to I?

    In C major the V chord (G-B-D) contains B, just a half step below C. Your ear expects it to climb up to C. That pull is called tendency.

    What is a cadence?

    A cadence is musical punctuation: V→I is a full stop (authentic); IV→I is the “A-men” (plagal); stopping on V is a question mark (half cadence); V→vi is a plot twist (deceptive).

    Upper- vs lower-case numerals

    Upper case (I, IV, V) = major chords, bright. Lower case (ii, iii, vi) = minor chords, darker.

    👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

    US standards: Georgia Standards of Excellence, Middle School General Music — MSGM6–8.CR.1 (improvise simple harmonic accompaniments with common chords; improvise short melodies in given styles, meters and tonalities), MSGM6–8.CR.2 (compose and arrange within guidelines; use traditional and digital sound sources), MSGM6–8.RE.2 (develop criteria to evaluate work and suggest improvements). National Core Arts Standards: MU:Cr1.1 (imagine), MU:Cr2.1 (plan and make, document with notation), MU:Cr3.1 (evaluate and refine), MU:Cr3.2 (present).

    China: Compulsory Education Arts Curriculum Standards (2022), Music, Stage 4 (Grades 7–9), the practice of creating — composing melodies with learned musical elements and adding simple harmony (the “creative practice” core competency), plus the traditional Chinese phrase structure 起承转合. Both countries ask students to create within guidelines and evaluate and revise their work.

    Notes: the share code just encodes the song as letters and digits — nothing is uploaded. The coach’s rules (mostly steps, end on the tonic, repeat a motif) are common beginner guidelines, not laws.

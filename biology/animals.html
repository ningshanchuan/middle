The Animal Kingdom: backbone or not?

What do a sponge, a jellyfish and you have in common? You are all animals! From sponges with no symmetry to mammals that make milk, scientists sort animals by their body features. Spin the turntable to meet 11 animal groups, use the X-ray to find backbones, and check how each one is symmetrical.

⭕

No symmetry

No cut gives matching halves. Example: most sponges.

✳️

Radial symmetry

Body parts are arranged around a center, like a wheel or pizza. Examples: jellyfish, adult sea stars.

🦋

Bilateral symmetry

Only one cut makes matching left and right halves; usually a head end. Examples: worms, insects, all vertebrates.

Lab: ectotherms vs. endotherms

A lizard is an ectotherm: its body temperature follows its surroundings. A mouse is an endotherm: it burns food to stay near 37 °C. Change the air temperature and compare their body heat and energy use.

⚠️ Common mix-up:“Cold-blooded” is misleading. A lizard basking in the sun can be warmer than you! Ectotherms just can’t make enough body heat to keep a steady temperature.

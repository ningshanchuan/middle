Why classify?

Classification lets scientists study living things in an organized way, share information clearly, and see how organisms are related.

📜

Carolus Linnaeus (1707–1778)

Swedish scientist who created the ranked classification system, the “father of taxonomy”. Early classification was based mainly on physical traits like body shape.

🏷️

Binomial nomenclature

Every species gets a two-part Latin name: genus + species, e.g. Homo sapiens (humans). The genus is capitalized and the name is written in italics.

🧬

Today: DNA too

Modern scientists compare DNA as well as physical traits. DNA studies showed archaea are very different from bacteria, which led to the three-domain system.

Use a dichotomous key

“Dichotomous” means “dividing in two”. At each step you answer one yes/no question and follow the key until you reach the organism’s name. Pick a mystery organism to start!

    Build your own key

    Write a dichotomous key for these 6 sea animals. For each group, choose a question that splits it into “yes” and “no”, until every animal is alone. A good question splits a group into two parts!

    🧮 How efficient is your key?

    Each yes/no question can at best cut a group in half, so n organisms need at least log₂ n questions: 6 → log₂ 6 ≈ 2.58, so the longest path needs at least 3. A key to all ~2 million named species would need at least log₂ 2,000,000 ≈ 21 questions!

    🎯 Challenge: Build a key with the lowest possible average number of questions (the best is 16 ÷ 6 ≈ 2.67).

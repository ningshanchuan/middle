Three words, one rule

These three words always describe the solution OUTSIDE the cell, compared with the inside of the cell. Water follows salt (solute).

⬅️💧 Hypertonic

MORE solute outside (less water) → water moves OUT
Animal cell: shrivels (crenation)
Plant cell: plasmolysis
  • Examples: egg in corn syrup, Elodea in salt water, a salted slug, pickles

⇄ Isotonic

SAME solute inside and out → water moves both ways equally (no net change)
Animal cell: normal
Plant cell: flaccid (limp)
  • Examples: 0.9% saline IV, contact-lens solution, an egg in an egg-matched solution

💧➡️ Hypotonic

LESS solute outside (more water) → water moves IN
Animal cell: swells → may burst (lysis)
Plant cell: turgid (firm, healthy)
  • Examples: egg in distilled water, Elodea in tap water, raisins soaking in water, a lake for a freshwater fish
💡 Memory hints
  • Hyper = over, too much (like “hyperactive”): too much solute outside.
  • Hypo = under, too little (like a “hypodermic” needle that goes under the skin). The cell swells up like the letter O in hypO!
  • Iso = equal (like an isosceles triangle with two equal sides).
⚠️ Common mix-ups:
  • “Hypertonic” means MORE solute, so the solution has LESS water.
  • It is the WATER that moves, not the salt or sugar. The membrane holds back most solute.
  • Always say “compared with the cell”. A solution can be hypertonic to one cell and hypotonic to another.

🔬 Virtual Elodea lab

Make a wet mount, focus from low to high power, then add salt water at one edge of the coverslip and wick it through with a paper towel. Watch the chloroplasts pull away from the cell walls (plasmolysis), then bring the cells back (recovery). Drag the view to move the slide.

      👩‍🏫 Teacher / parent notes

      Supports Georgia GSE S7L2 (how cell structures, especially the membrane and the wall, control what enters and leaves and help the cell stay stable). The “3 Types of Solutions” video introduces the concepts, which students apply in the Elodea lab and the classic egg osmosis experiment. The basic idea of osmosis is in the Osmosis lesson; this lesson focuses on lab skills: microscope use, exchanging the liquid under a coverslip, measuring, data tables, percent change, line graphs and CER writing.

      Chinese curriculum link: 人教版 Grade 7 (first semester), “细胞是生命活动的基本单位”: using the microscope, making a temporary wet mount, observing plant cells. How plant cells gain and lose water (植物细胞的吸水和失水) appears in Grade 8 with root water uptake; the plasmolysis and recovery experiment (质壁分离和质壁分离复原) and hypertonic / hypotonic / isotonic solutions are senior-high 必修1《分子与细胞》, used here as enrichment. Elodea (黑藻) leaves are only one or two cells thick with big chloroplasts, which is why both US labs and Chinese textbooks use them.

      Lab tips: real classes use 5–10% salt water and see plasmolysis within minutes; cells left in strong salt too long die and won’t recover. Egg lab: vinegar (about 5% acetic acid) reacts with the calcium carbonate shell, releasing carbon dioxide bubbles; the shell is gone in 24–48 h. Vinegar is mostly water, so the naked egg usually gains a little mass. Eggs float in corn syrup (it is denser) and lose 30–50% of their mass in 2–3 days. Rinse and blot eggs before weighing (syrup is sticky), use the same scale, and tare it first. Common misconceptions: salt or sugar goes into the cell and “pulls” water; “a hypertonic solution has more water”; drawing a conclusion from one day or one egg.

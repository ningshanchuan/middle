Endocytosis: How Cells Eat

Definition: endocytosis

Endocytosis: the cell uses energy to move substances into the cell. The membrane surrounds the material and pinches off a vesicle. (endo- = “into”, cyto = “cell”.)

Your hyperdoc lists it under active transport because the cell spends energy (ATP). Some books call it bulk transport: it moves big things, a whole bubble at a time.

👩‍🏫 Teacher / parent notes

Supports GSE S7L2 (cell structures and functions; the cell membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell) and the “Endocytosis” row of the “Comparing Cell Transport” hyperdoc. Suggested flow: watch the two hyperdoc videos in Observe; play Amoeba hunt and pause at “Pinch off” to ask: where did the vesicle’s membrane come from? (From the cell membrane itself.) Try Cell drinking to see the membrane fold inward. Then students practice in Record before writing their own sentences in the hyperdoc (the checker looks for key ideas, not exact words). Advanced mode adds receptor-mediated endocytosis (LDL cholesterol) and a surface-area calculation (4πr²) that connects to math.

Chinese curriculum: 人教版 Grade 7 Biology (七年级上册) teaches that the cell membrane controls what enters and leaves (细胞膜控制物质的进出) and introduces the paramecium’s food vacuoles (食物泡) and contractile vacuoles (伸缩泡). 胞吞 / 胞吐 (endocytosis / exocytosis) are formally taught in high school (必修1 分子与细胞), so this is an enrichment preview. Note the naming difference: 必修1 does not call 胞吞/胞吐 主动运输 (active transport); it lists them separately as the way large molecules and particles cross the membrane, which also uses energy. US middle-school materials and this hyperdoc group them under active transport because they use energy.

Science notes: the ATP numbers in the model are only for comparison. In real cells, pseudopods are pushed out by actin filaments (ATP), vesicles are pinched off by dynamin (GTP), and motor proteins (ATP) carry them inside. Common misconceptions: big particles diffuse through the membrane; vesicles appear from nowhere (they are made from the membrane); endocytosis needs no energy.

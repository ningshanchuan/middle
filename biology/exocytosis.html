Exocytosis: How Cells Ship Out

You eat a cookie and your blood sugar goes up. Cells in your pancreas must get insulin into your blood, but insulin is too big to slip through the membrane. So the cell builds it, packs it into a bubble and ships it out, like a factory sending a package!

Definition: exocytosis

Exocytosis: the cell uses energy to move things out of (exit) the cell. A vesicle carrying the material travels to the membrane, fuses with it and spills its contents outside. (exo- = “out”, like exit.)

It is the reverse of endocytosis. This lesson groups it under active transport because the cell spends energy (ATP). Some books call it bulk transport.

👩‍🏫 Teacher / parent notes

Supports GSE S7L2 (cell structures and functions; the cell membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell). Suggested flow: watch the 1-minute animation; play the first factory order (insulin) and, at the Golgi sorting step, ask why digestive enzymes must not be shipped out; try the Paramecium pump, raise the salt slider and discuss why only freshwater protists need contractile vacuoles (links to the osmosis lesson); have students write and revise answers in Record in their own words. Advanced mode adds regulated vs constitutive exocytosis, Palade’s pulse-chase timetable and a membrane-area calculation.

Chinese curriculum: 人教版 Grade 7 (七年级上册) teaches that the cell membrane controls what enters and leaves, and the paramecium’s collecting canals and contractile vacuoles (收集管、伸缩泡) that expel extra water; Grade 7 (下册) covers insulin and blood sugar (激素调节). The full secretory pathway is high-school 必修1 (分泌蛋白的合成和运输: ribosome → ER → Golgi → membrane, powered by mitochondria, with the guinea-pig pancreas ³H-leucine experiment: label in the ER at about 3 min, Golgi at 17 min, secretory vesicles and secretion at 117 min), so this lesson is an enrichment preview. Note: 必修1 does not call exocytosis 主动运输 (active transport); some US materials group it under active transport because it uses energy.

Science notes: vesicles are carried along microtubules by motor proteins (ATP) and fused by SNARE proteins; regulated exocytosis (insulin, neurotransmitters) is triggered by Ca²⁺. Synaptic vesicles are recycled and refilled right at the nerve ending instead of returning to the Golgi each time. Exactly how the paramecium’s contractile vacuole empties is still studied; here it is presented as an exocytosis-like, energy-using way to push water out. Common misconceptions: exocytosis leaves a hole in the membrane (the vesicle membrane becomes part of the cell membrane); materials jump straight from the Golgi out of the cell; exocytosis needs no energy.

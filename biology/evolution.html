Natural Selection & Evolution

How did a whole population of moths change from light to dark in just a few decades? Living things don’t change because they “want” to, but populations change over generations. Natural selection in four steps

After studying animals on the Galápagos Islands and elsewhere, Charles Darwin explained that whenever these four conditions are true, a population changes over time.

1

Overproduction

Organisms have far more offspring than can survive.

2

Variation

Individuals of a species differ, and many differences are inherited.

3

Selection

Individuals with helpful traits are more likely to survive and reproduce (“survival of the fittest”).

4

Inheritance

Survivors pass helpful traits to their offspring; over many generations the trait becomes common.

💡 Memory hintRemember “VIST”: Variation, Inheritance, Selection, Time. “Fittest” means best suited to the environment and leaving the most offspring, not necessarily the strongest.

Evidence that supports evolution

🦴

Fossil record

Fossils in rock layers show life changing over time, extinct species, and in-between forms (like Archaeopteryx and early whales with legs).

🖐️

Homologous structures

Different animals share the same bone pattern, pointing to a common ancestor. Compare them in the 3D model above!

🧬

DNA comparisons

The more closely related two species are, the more similar their DNA. Humans and chimpanzees share about 98% of their DNA.

🐣

Embryos

Early embryos of fish, chickens and humans look very similar: all have a tail and throat pouches.

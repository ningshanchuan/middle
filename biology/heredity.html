Chromosomes come in pairs

You get one set of chromosomes from your mother and one set from your father. That is why you have two copies of every gene.

🧍

Humans: 23 pairs

Most human body cells have 46 chromosomes: 22 matching pairs + 1 pair of sex chromosomes.

🫛

Pea plants: 7 pairs

Pea cells have 14 chromosomes. Mendel used peas to discover the rules of heredity.

♀️♂️

Sex chromosomes: XX or XY

Females are usually XX and males XY. Every egg carries an X; a sperm carries either X or Y.

🥚

Gametes: only half

Sperm and egg cells have only 23 chromosomes (one from each pair). Fertilization brings the number back to 46.

⚠️ Common mix-up:“Gene” and “allele” are not the same thing. A gene is the DNA section for a trait (the “height gene”); alleles are the different versions of that gene (tall T or short t).

Supports GSE S7L3a (explain with evidence the role of genes and chromosomes in inheriting a specific trait) and S7L3b (develop and use a model, such as a Punnett square, to describe how sexual reproduction produces genetic variation). Suggested flow: ① play the Story and have students predict Tt × Tt before step 4; ② practice with “Quiz me” in the builder; ③ in “Breed 100”, compare results after +10 and after +100 to discuss sample size and probability.

Note: for clarity the three genes are drawn on one chromosome pair; in real pea plants they sit on different chromosomes. A good prompt for discussing the limits of models.

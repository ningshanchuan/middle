Reproduction & Selective Breeding

Can a living thing have just one parent? Living things make offspring in two ways: one parent copies itself (asexual reproduction), or genes from two parents combine (sexual reproduction). Asexual vs sexual reproduction

Each way has strengths and weaknesses. The key difference: are the offspring’s genes identical to the parent’s, or a brand-new mix?

🧫

Asexual: 1 parent

Offspring are clones with identical genetic information.

  • Binary fission: bacteria split in two
  • Budding: hydra and yeast grow buds
  • Spores: molds and ferns release spores
  • Vegetative propagation: potato eyes, strawberry runners
  • Regeneration: a planarian cut in pieces regrows into new worms

👍 Advantages

  • Fast, no need to find a mate
  • One individual can start a whole population

👎 Disadvantages

  • No genetic variation: one disease or change can wipe them all out
🌸

Sexual: 2 parents

A sperm (or sperm from pollen) joins an egg (fertilization). Offspring get half their genes from each parent.

  • Animals: fish, frogs, birds, humans
  • Flowering plants: pollen + ovule → seed
  • Each offspring is a unique mix (except identical twins)

👍 Advantages

  • Creates genetic variation: some offspring may survive changes better

👎 Disadvantages

  • Slower, needs a mate, uses more energy
  • Each parent passes on only half its genes
⚠️ Common mix-up:“Asexual” does not mean “only simple organisms”. Many plants (strawberries, potatoes) and some animals (sea stars, aphids) can reproduce both asexually and sexually.

Teosinte → corn

About 9,000 years ago, farmers in Mexico began choosing teosinte with bigger, softer ears, slowly creating corn.

🐕

Wolves → dog breeds

All dogs came from wolves. Choosing for size, coat or behavior produced hundreds of breeds, from Chihuahuas to sheepdogs.

🧪

Genetic engineering

Scientists move a gene directly into another organism, e.g. bacteria that make insulin for diabetes, or insect-resistant (Bt) corn.

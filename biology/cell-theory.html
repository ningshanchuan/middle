Levels of organization

Multicellular organisms are built in layers, from small to big. Click a level and the 3D model zooms there.

🦠

Unicellular organisms

The whole organism is ONE cell, and that cell does everything: eating, moving, getting rid of waste and reproducing. Examples: bacteria, paramecium, amoeba, yeast.

🐘

Multicellular organisms

Made of many cells that divide up the work: different cells form tissues, organs and organ systems. Examples: humans, elephants, oak trees, mushrooms.

⚠️ Common mix-up:An elephant is bigger than a mouse NOT because its cells are bigger, but because it has far MORE cells! Most animal cells are about the same size.

The three ideas of the cell theory

The cell theory is one of the most important ideas in biology. Many scientists built it step by step over almost 200 years.

1

All living things are made of cells

Some have just one cell; others have trillions.

2

The cell is the basic unit of life

The cell is the basic unit of structure and function: the smallest unit that can carry out life on its own.

3

Cells come from existing cells

New cells are made only when existing cells divide. They never appear from nothing.

⚠️ Common mix-up:The “cells” Hooke saw were only the empty walls of dead cork cells. The first person to see LIVING cells was Leeuwenhoek.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S7L2: students construct an explanation of the cell theory and how cells, tissues, organs, organ systems and organisms are organized (S7L2a–e). Suggested flow: use the 3D zoom and ask students to describe what each level is made of; then use the virtual microscope to practice “low power first, fine focus only on high power” and calculate total magnification (10× eyepiece × objective). Discussion: why are heart muscle cells packed with mitochondria? (The heart never stops beating, so it needs lots of energy.)

Common misconceptions: organelles are “tiny organs” and so a level of organization; bigger organisms have bigger cells; Hooke saw living cells. Extension: viruses are not cells, so they don’t fit the cell theory.

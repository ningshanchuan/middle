How organisms interact

In a community, different species interact in many ways. Symbiosis is a close, long-term relationship between two species, and it comes in three kinds.

+ / +

Mutualism

Both species benefit.

🐝 A bee drinks nectar and carries pollen for the flower. 🐠 A clownfish hides safely in a sea anemone and chases off its enemies.
+ / 0

Commensalism

One species benefits; the other is neither helped nor harmed.

🐋 Barnacles ride on a whale to reach food. 🌳 Spanish moss hangs from Georgia’s oak trees but does not take the tree’s nutrients.
+ / −

Parasitism

The parasite benefits; the host is harmed.

🦟 A mosquito drinks human blood. 🐕 A tick feeds on a dog’s blood and can spread disease. Parasites usually do not kill their host quickly.
🥊

Competition

Two or more organisms need the same limited resource, such as food, water, sunlight or space. In the savanna’s dry season, every animal crowds around the same water hole.

🦁

Predation

A predator hunts and eats its prey. Predation is not symbiosis, but it helps control the size of prey populations.

⚠️ Common mix-up:“Symbiosis” does not always mean both partners are happy. Parasitism and commensalism are symbiosis too. Decide by asking whether each partner is helped (+), harmed (−) or unaffected (0).

Suggested activity: turn on the Biotic / abiotic view and have students list 3 biotic and 3 abiotic factors for each biome; then compare the temperature and rainfall bars of two biomes and explain why their plants differ.

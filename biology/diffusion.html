Diffusion in the Body

You breathe about 15 times a minute, but you never “tell” oxygen to jump into your blood. How does sugar from your lunch get into your blood? How do your cells get rid of their trash? The answer to all three is one free process: diffusion.

🎯Mission: follow oxygen, food and waste on a trip through your body, and prove that diffusion costs zero energy!

Need a quick review? 👉 see also Diffusion & Osmosis overview · next: Osmosis: Water on the Move

Passive transport: no energy needed

Diffusion is the movement of molecules from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration. It does not require energy.

  • Random motion: molecules never stop jiggling and bumping.
  • Net flow: the result is always from crowded to less crowded (high → low).
  • Does it stop? When both sides are equal (equilibrium), the net flow stops, but molecules keep moving.

Supports Georgia GSE S7L2 (how cell structures, especially the cell membrane that controls what enters and leaves, and tissues, organs and systems work together to meet an organism’s needs). Built for the “Diffusion” row of the class hyperdoc “Comparing Cell Transport”: watch the hyperdoc video, explore the body-journey model, then practice the Record questions. The Record section is practice, not an answer sheet: students write first, self-check against key ideas, then write their own sentences in the hyperdoc.

Chinese curriculum link: 人教版 Grade 7 (vol. 1) “细胞的生活” (the cell membrane controls what goes in and out); Grade 7 (vol. 2) units on nutrition (absorption by 小肠绒毛, the villi), breathing (gas exchange between alveoli and blood by diffusion) and removing body wastes.

Suggested flow: ① warm-up: change only the temperature and time how long the dye takes to even out (controlled variable). ② Lungs: with breathing and blood flow OFF, watch the net flow slow to equilibrium; switch them ON and discuss why the difference stays large. ③ Body cell: raise the activity and watch diffusion speed up. Common misconceptions: molecules stop at equilibrium; the lungs or cells actively “pull” oxygen in; diffusion uses energy. Advanced mode introduces Fick’s law (rate ∝ area × difference ÷ distance); link it to emphysema (less area).

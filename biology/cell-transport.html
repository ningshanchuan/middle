Diffusion & Osmosis

Why does a wilted lettuce leaf turn crisp again in a bowl of water? The cell membrane is a picky gatekeeper: some things slip through freely, some need a “tunnel”, and some must be pumped in using energy. Drop a red blood cell into salty or fresh water and watch the water rush in and out!

Three solutions, three results

Water always moves across the membrane from where there is LESS dissolved stuff (like salt) to where there is MORE. In other words: water follows salt.

💡 Memory hint“Water follows salt”: whichever side has more salt, water moves toward it. Cells swell in fresh water and shrivel in very salty water.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S7L2 (cell structures and functions, especially how the cell membrane controls what enters and leaves). Suggested flow: in the 3D red-blood-cell view drag the salt from 0.9% to 0% and to 3% and ask students to describe the net direction of water; switch to the plant cell and discuss why plant cells don’t burst (cell wall) and why vegetables wilt. Use the membrane close-up and the story to separate simple diffusion, facilitated diffusion (channel proteins) and active transport (pumps that use ATP). The diffusion lab supports a controlled experiment: change only the temperature and time how long it takes to reach equilibrium.

Common misconceptions: salt moves into the cell during osmosis (it is the WATER that moves); particles stop moving at equilibrium; active transport is just “faster diffusion”.

What moves air in and out?

During inhalation, the diaphragm contracts and flattens. The ribs move up and out. The chest cavity grows, the lungs expand, and the pressure inside them temporarily falls below outside pressure, drawing air in. During quiet exhalation, the muscles relax and the lungs recoil, raising pressure briefly and pushing air out.

Two gases moving in opposite directions

In the lungs, oxygen diffuses from alveolar air into blood, and carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into the alveolus. In tissues, oxygen diffuses from blood toward cells, and carbon dioxide produced by cells enters the blood. Each gas moves overall from higher to lower partial pressure.

How does partial pressure set the direction?

In air, partial pressure is one gas’s share of the total pressure. Gases dissolved in blood also have a partial pressure. Each gas moves overall from higher to lower partial pressure, so oxygen and carbon dioxide can diffuse in opposite directions at the same time.

Common mix-upBreathing moves air in and out of the lungs. Diffusion moves gases across the walls of alveoli and capillaries.

Many alveoli provide a large surface area. Thin alveolar and capillary walls give gases a short distance to travel. Review diffusion in the body

Compare the three vessel types

VesselStructure and function
ArteryCarries blood away from the heart. Usually thick, elastic walls withstand higher pressure.
CapillaryPart of a network of tiny vessels between small arteries and small veins. Its wall is mainly one layer of cells, suited for exchange.
VeinReturns blood to the heart. Walls are usually thinner than those of comparable arteries. Many limb veins have valves that limit backflow.

A heartbeat is a cycle of heart contraction and relaxation. A pulse is the rhythmic expansion of an artery as a pressure wave passes after the heart ejects blood. At rest, a wrist pulse count usually matches the number of heartbeats in the same time.

The two loops

Systemic circulation starts in the left ventricle, takes blood through the body’s tissue capillaries, and ends in the right atrium. Pulmonary circulation starts in the right ventricle, takes blood through lung capillaries, and ends in the left atrium.

Common mix-upThe pulmonary arteries carry oxygen-poor blood away from the heart. The pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood back. Artery and vein describe the direction of flow. Oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor describe how much oxygen the blood carries.

The diagram uses blue for oxygen-poor blood. Real blood is red and looks darker when it carries less oxygen.

Why does blood type matter?

Type A red cells have A antigen, type B has B, type AB has both, and type O has neither. If the recipient’s antibodies encounter the matching antigen on donor red cells, the cells can clump and be destroyed. For red-cell transfusions, the same ABO and RhD type is generally preferred.

Rh type, crossmatching, and donation

The plus or minus in a blood type shows RhD status. Before a red-cell transfusion, a blood bank typically checks ABO and RhD type, screens for other red-cell antibodies, and crossmatches donor cells.

Donated blood can be separated into red cells, platelets, and plasma for different treatments. In China, eligible people can donate through a blood center voluntarily and without pay.

How does tobacco smoke affect gas exchange?

Tobacco smoke can damage the cilia that help clear mucus from airways. Long-term smoking can destroy alveolar walls, reducing the surface available for gas exchange. Regular physical activity helps keep the heart and lungs healthy.

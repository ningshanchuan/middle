Worked example · Compare peanut and walnut

Given: Each cup contains 50.0 g of water at 20.0 °C. Burning 1.00 g of each sample raises the water to 32.0 °C for peanut and 35.0 °C for walnut.

Find: Which sample transferred more heat to the water per gram?

The temperature rises are 32.0 °C − 20.0 °C = 12.0 °C for peanut and 35.0 °C − 20.0 °C = 15.0 °C for walnut. Use water’s specific heat, 4.18 J/(g·°C), to find the heat the water gains, then divide by the mass burned.

Six kinds of nutrients and what they do
Carbohydrates
A main energy source. Rice, noodles and potatoes contain starch.
Fats
Supply and store energy and help absorb certain vitamins. Nuts and oils contain fat.
Proteins
Supply amino acids for growth and repair. They can also provide energy. Tofu, fish and eggs contain protein.
Water
Carries substances, helps regulate temperature and provides a medium for cell reactions.
Minerals
Calcium helps build bones. Iron is part of hemoglobin. Different minerals have different roles.
Vitamins
Needed in small amounts for many body processes. For example, vitamin A supports vision in dim light.
Carbohydrates, fats and proteins can supply energy. Water, minerals and vitamins are essential for other functions and supply no energy.

Chemical digestion

Bile contains no digestive enzymes. It spreads large fat droplets into smaller ones (emulsification). This exposes more surface for lipase, the enzyme that breaks bonds in fat molecules.
What do starch, protein and fat become?

Starch starts breaking down in the mouth into maltose and other shorter carbohydrates. Digestion continues in the small intestine, yielding glucose.

Protein digestion starts in the stomach, producing shorter chains called peptides. Digestion continues in the small intestine, yielding amino acids.

Most fat digestion takes place in the small intestine. The usual middle-school summary is “glycerol and fatty acids.” The main products of pancreatic lipase are fatty acids and monoglycerides.

Design and evidence

A and B each start with 2 mL of the same starch solution. Add 1 mL of saliva to A and 1 mL of water to B. Hold both in a 37 °C (about 99 °F, close to body temperature) water bath for the same time. Test equal amounts of liquid from each with the same amount of iodine and record the color.

The independent variable is whether saliva is added. The dependent variable is the color after adding iodine. Keep starch concentration and volume, temperature, reaction time, sample volume and iodine amount the same in A and B.

The PEP activity uses steamed buns or rice to investigate digestion in the mouth. To model chewing, you can compare a chunk of steamed bun with crumbs under otherwise identical conditions. This version uses the same starch solution in both tubes to isolate the effect of saliva.

The timing is simulated. In a real experiment, the result can vary with factors such as the amount of amylase in the saliva.

An amber result means starch was below the test’s detection limit. Identifying which sugars formed would require a different test.

How do temperature and pH affect enzymes?

Challenge · Which enzyme is near its peak at pH 2?

Pepsin. It works well in the acidic stomach, while salivary amylase works better near neutral pH. Cooling usually slows enzyme reactions, and excessive heat can denature enzymes.

How does the small intestine absorb nutrients?

Raise the folds, then unfold the lining. How much extra surface can fit into the same length of intestine?

Geometry model: a 12 cm base, 2 cm wide, with three rectangular folds. Each extra centimeter of height adds a centimeter of lining on each side of a fold.

Real intestine has circular folds covered with finger-like villi. Absorptive cells on the villi have even smaller projections called microvilli. Each level adds surface in contact with digested food.

Glucose and amino acids cross the intestinal lining into capillaries. After intestinal cells process most long-chain fat products, lymph vessels carry them first and deliver them to the blood later.

Zoom in · Trace two routes through a villus

Red: blood capillaries. Green: lymph vessel inside the villus.

Water, minerals and vitamins can be absorbed without being broken into smaller molecules. Most water is absorbed in the small intestine, and the large intestine absorbs some of what remains.

Worked example and challenge · Surface area

Given: A strip has a 12 cm base, a width of 2 cm and three folds that each rise 2 cm.

Find: The area of the folded lining.

First add both sides of every fold to the base length. Then multiply by the width.

The model has 48 cm² of lining, twice the flat area of 24 cm².

Challenge · How many times the flat area at 3 cm?

The unfolded length is 30 cm. Multiply by 2 cm to get 60 cm². Divide by 24 cm² to get 2.5 times the flat area.

    Food safety · Clean, separate, cook, chill

    Clean hands, utensils and counters. Separate raw meat from ready-to-eat foods. Cook foods to a safe internal temperature and check with a food thermometer. Chill perishable foods promptly.

    Check the label, date and storage instructions on packaged food. Food can smell normal and still be unsafe.

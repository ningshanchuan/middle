Biotechnology starts in the kitchen

Biotechnology means using living things or parts of them to make products or solve problems. People have used fermentation for thousands of years. Bread, yogurt, steamed buns, wine, soy sauce and vinegar can all be made with fermentation. Today, scientists also use genes to make medicines.

Study of ancient fermented drinks in China

Yeast is a fungus. When oxygen is scarce, yeast breaks down glucose, releasing a little energy and producing alcohol and carbon dioxide. Dough rises as it traps the gas bubbles. In milk, lactic acid bacteria use lactose. The acid lowers the pH, and milk proteins form a gel that thickens the milk.

Alcoholic fermentation in yeast · glucose → alcohol (ethanol) + carbon dioxide

Lactic acid fermentation (simplified) · glucose → lactic acid

Both pathways let cells get a little energy from sugar without oxygen. Yogurt bacteria use lactose, a sugar made of glucose and galactose. Lower pH means stronger acidity. Fermentation reference

Temperature in real fermentation

Refrigeration slows baker’s yeast. Many doughs rise well in the twenties Celsius, and baker’s yeast can ferment quickly around 30–35 °C (86–95 °F). As dough heats during baking, the yeast eventually dies. The strain, dough and heating time matter. Too much sugar can also slow fermentation. Baking reference · Yeast heat study · Sugar and gas production study

Typical yogurt starter contains Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus delbrueckii subsp. bulgaricus. These cultures work well around 43–46 °C (109–115 °F). FDA yogurt standard · Utah State University Extension

Quick check

Which conditions slow or stop microbial growth?

As bacteria, yeasts and molds grow, they can change a food’s smell, texture or color. Cold, drying, salt and sugar can make growth harder. Heat can kill many microbes. Some disease-causing microbes can be present even when food looks and smells normal.

Louis Pasteur found that microbes could spoil wine and that controlled heating helped preserve it. Today people also use pasteurization to kill disease-causing microbes in milk.

Surviving microbes can grow again after frozen food thaws. Perishable food still needs refrigeration or freezing in a vacuum package.

Selective breeding: How big will the next fruits be?

Pick two parent plants and grow the next generation. Try choosing the two largest-fruited plants for several rounds, then switch to the two smallest.

Solid bars show this generation. Outlines show the previous one. The horizontal scale stays fixed.

Each circle is a plant labeled with its fruit diameter in centimeters. The model treats fruit size as inherited and varies offspring around the parents’ average within a 1–9 cm range. In real plants, growing conditions also affect fruit size.

In selective breeding (artificial selection), people choose parents with desired inherited traits. Over many generations, those traits can become more common. In natural selection, the environment affects which individuals leave more offspring.

Dogs descend from ancient wolves, and people later bred dogs for different traits. Growers bred wild cabbage relatives to produce cabbage, broccoli and kale. Early farmers in Mexico domesticated maize from teosinte. Plant breeders also select for traits such as high yield. Dog ancestry study · Cabbage domestication study · Smithsonian on maize

Worked example: Compare two generations
  1. Reset the model. The 10 starting diameters add to 43 cm, so the mean is 43 cm ÷ 10 = 4.3 cm.
  2. Pick the plants with fruits measuring 6 cm and 7 cm. The parent mean is (6 cm + 7 cm) ÷ 2 = 6.5 cm.
  3. Grow the next generation. Its mean rises by 6.5 cm − 4.3 cm = 2.2 cm in the model, and individual fruits still vary in size.
China story: Yuan Longping, hybrid rice and the field teams

Yuan Longping began researching hybrid rice in 1964. Researchers and farmers in different regions searched for parent plants, ran field trials and measured harvests. In 1973, teams established a system that used three breeding lines to produce hybrid seed. Large-scale planting in China began in 1976. Hybrid breeding crosses different parent lines. When offspring outperform their parents in a trait such as yield, this is called hybrid vigor. Hybrid rice research history

In demonstration trials in Hengnan, Hunan, two harvests together yielded about 1,500 kg per mu in 2020 and about 1,600 kg per mu in 2021. A mu is about 667 m². 2020 trial · 2021 trial

    In one kind of genetic engineering, a useful gene is moved into another organism so its cells can make a new protein. For insulin, a small ring of DNA called a plasmid carries genetic instructions into bacteria. A single cell can be a tiny factory.

    The animation shows the basic idea of moving a gene into a bacterium. To first produce human insulin in bacteria, researchers built DNA instructions that the bacteria could use. History of recombinant human insulin

    Patients who needed insulin once relied on insulin purified from pigs or cattle. In 1982 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved human insulin made using bacteria, giving patients a new source. FDA insulin history

    Selective breeding chooses parents to pass on traits. Transgenic engineering moves a gene directly.

    Two examples from crops

    Bt cotton carries genetic instructions from the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis. The cotton makes a protein that controls certain insect pests. Zhongmiansuo 41, a variety developed in China, carries both a Bt gene and another insect-resistance gene. CAAS variety profile

    Golden Rice grains contain extra beta-carotene, which the body can turn into vitamin A. The Philippines ran a pilot rollout. A court halted commercial planting in 2024.     Cloning and tissue culture

    A mint cutting that grows into a new plant is a clone. Plant tissue culture can also produce many new plants from a small piece of plant tissue. A clone has the same or nearly the same genetic information as its source.

    课外拓展 · Enrichment

    Dolly was the first mammal cloned from an adult body cell. She was born in 1996 and announced in 1997. A Chinese Academy of Sciences team in Shanghai produced the first monkeys cloned by somatic cell nuclear transfer, meaning the nucleus came from a body cell. The macaques Zhong Zhong and Hua Hua were born in 2017, and the study was published in 2018. Roslin Institute on Dolly · CAS on Zhong Zhong and Hua Hua

    Dolly’s nuclear DNA came from the donor body cell. Her mitochondrial DNA came from the egg cell. A clone’s memories come from its own experiences.

    Vaccines and medicines

    Biotechnology helps make vaccines. For example, yeast cells can make a viral protein used in hepatitis B vaccines. Vaccination helps the immune system learn to recognize that protein.

    From claims to evidence

    Biotechnology can help food keep longer and make medicines available. For a new technology, ask how it might affect people and ecosystems, who can afford to use it, and how animal welfare is protected when animals are involved.

    Scientists use controlled comparisons and repeated measurements to study a product’s benefits and risks. Regulators review the evidence and apply the rules for that product when deciding whether and how it may be used. FDA example of biotech oversight

    Switch to Advanced to explore how yeast gets energy and how scientists work with DNA.

    课外拓展 · Enrichment

    Aerobic respiration and alcoholic fermentation. Yeast gets much more usable energy from the same amount of glucose through aerobic respiration than through alcohol fermentation. With little oxygen, fermentation lets cells keep getting a little energy from sugar. Some school texts use “anaerobic respiration” for this process. Microbiology distinguishes fermentation from anaerobic respiration.

    Plasmids and restriction enzymes. Many bacteria have plasmids, DNA rings that can copy independently of the chromosome. Restriction enzymes recognize short DNA sequences and cut DNA at particular sites. DNA ligase can join DNA pieces.

    CRISPR gene editing. A guide RNA acts like GPS for molecular scissors called Cas9, which cut at a target in DNA. Scientists can use the cell’s repair process to change the DNA. Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna won the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing a genome-editing method. Nobel announcement

    Polymerase chain reaction (PCR). PCR copies a chosen stretch of DNA many times. In each cycle the two DNA strands separate, short primers mark the region to copy, and DNA polymerase builds new strands. Repeating the cycle supplies enough DNA to detect or study the stretch. NHGRI on PCR

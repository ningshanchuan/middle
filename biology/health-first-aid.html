How does the WHO define health?

The World Health Organization describes health as well-being in your body, your mind, and your social life. Being free of illness is only part of it. Sleep helps your body recover, coping with stress supports mental health, and people you trust help you feel connected.

Ask how your body is doing, how you feel, and how things are going with others.

Prescription (Rx) and over-the-counter (OTC)

A prescription medicine requires a prescription from a health professional. You can buy over-the-counter medicine without one. For both, check the directions, expiration date, and warnings. If you’re unsure, ask a parent or caregiver to help you check with a pharmacist.

Common mix-up · “Take antibiotics for the flu.” The flu is caused by a virus. Antibiotics treat certain bacterial infections and have no effect on viruses.

What should you check before using two medicines?

Two products with different brand names can contain the same active ingredient. Taking both can exceed the safe dose. Check with a caregiver and pharmacist, and never take someone else’s prescription medicine.

First aid at the scene

Where should you press on a bleeding arm?

Choose a type of bleeding, then tap a spot. The dashed ring marks the wound. Vessel colors and positions are simplified.

Check that the scene is safe and use gloves if available. Cover the wound with gauze or a clean cloth and press firmly without letting up. If blood is pooling, pouring, or spurting, have someone call emergency services right away, or call on speakerphone while pressing.

Common mix-up · “First identify the vessel.” Start with direct pressure for most external bleeding. If an object is stuck in the wound, leave it in place and press around it. Follow the dispatcher’s instructions for severe arm or leg bleeding.

Practice an emergency call

Give the exact location first. Explain what happened, how many people need help, whether they respond and breathe normally, and how to call you back. Stay on the line and follow the dispatcher’s instructions.

Thermal burns and scalds

Once clear of the heat, cool the burn right away under clean, cool running water. For a small burn, aim for about 20 minutes and keep the rest of the body warm. Avoid putting ice directly on the skin or using toothpaste.

After cooling, loosely cover the area with a clean nonstick dressing. Get medical help promptly for large or deep burns, or burns to the face, hands, or feet. Call emergency services right away if the person has trouble breathing or may have inhaled smoke.

Nosebleeds

Sit upright, lean slightly forward, and breathe through your mouth. Pinch the soft part below the bridge for 10 to 15 minutes without checking. Keep the head forward. Get medical help if it still bleeds after 15 minutes or if the person feels lightheaded.

Choking · The Heimlich maneuver

If the person can cough forcefully, encourage coughing and watch closely. If a responsive adult or child age 1 or older cannot speak, cry, cough effectively, or breathe, have someone call emergency services. If you’re alone, use speakerphone if you can.

  1. Give 5 back blows. Support the chest, lean the person forward, and strike firmly between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
  2. Give 5 abdominal thrusts (the Heimlich maneuver). Stand behind the person. Place a fist just above the belly button and below the breastbone. Grasp it with your other hand and thrust quickly inward and upward.

Stop the blows and thrusts if the person can cough effectively, speak, or cry again, and keep watching them. If they become unresponsive, lower them to a firm, flat surface and start CPR with chest compressions, following the dispatcher’s guidance. When opening the airway for breaths, remove only an object you can see.

Use chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts in late pregnancy or if you cannot reach around the abdomen. For infants under 1, alternate 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts. Learn the techniques in hands-on training.

CPR and the AED

An adult collapses · Walk through the response

Select each step. Cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) uses chest compressions to keep some blood flowing. An automated external defibrillator (AED) checks the heart rhythm and can deliver a shock when needed. Use it as soon as it arrives.

If a teen or adult suddenly collapses and is unresponsive and not breathing normally, an untrained bystander can give continuous chest compressions with dispatcher guidance. This is called hands-only CPR. For children, infants, or drowning emergencies, follow the dispatcher’s instructions.

Compression metronome

Move the slider to change pace. The first square lights up as soon as you start. Each square represents one compression in a practice set of 30.

T is the time between beats in seconds, and r is the compression rate in beats per minute.

Worked examples · Time between beats

US example · At 120 beats per minute, divide the 60 seconds in one minute by the 120 beats in that minute. Each beat is half a second apart.

T = (60 s/min) ÷ (120/min) = 0.50 s

Chinese-style example · Given 100 beats per minute, find the time between beats. Divide seconds per minute by beats per minute.

T = (60 s/min) ÷ (100/min) = 0.60 s

Answer · There are 0.60 seconds between beats.

Practice · How long from beat 1 to beat 30?

Set the rate to 120 per minute. Beat 1 lights up when you press Start. How many gaps are there before beat 30 lights up, and how long does that take?

Show steps
(30 − 1) × 0.50 s = 14.5 s

There are 29 gaps between 30 beats. It takes 14.5 seconds to go from beat 1 to beat 30.

Make healthy choices

What would you do?

Build habits you can keep

Keep a regular sleep schedule and put screens away before bed. Pick a kind of movement that you enjoy and can do safely. If offered a cigarette, vape, alcohol, or an unknown pill, you can say no, leave, and ask a trusted adult for help.

How much sleep and activity?

CDC recommendations are 9 to 12 hours of sleep per day for ages 6 to 12, and 8 to 10 hours for ages 13 to 17. Ages 6 to 17 should get at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily, such as brisk walking, dancing, or playing ball.

Why avoid smoking, alcohol, and drug misuse?

Smoking harms the lungs. Most vapes contain nicotine, which can lead to addiction. Alcohol impairs judgment and coordination and raises the risk of injury. Unknown pills can contain unexpected dangerous ingredients. Use prescription medicine only when prescribed to you and as directed.

Try an opening line

Something has been worrying you and making it hard to sleep. Choose someone you could talk to.

When should you ask for help?

It’s common to feel stressed before a test or when things change. Try naming the feeling, taking a short break, or splitting a task into smaller steps. If stress lasts or gets in the way of sleep, school, or friendships, talk to a trusted adult early. They can help you reach a counselor or health professional.

Vaccination and herd immunity

Enrichment · 课外拓展

When a vaccine reduces infection or transmission, enough immune people can make an infection harder to spread. This indirectly protects some people who lack immunity. We call that herd immunity. The effect varies by disease and vaccine.

Think it through · What if a vaccine only lessens illness?

Imagine a vaccine that only lowers the risk of severe illness. It helps protect the people who get it from severe illness. If infection and transmission stay the same, the pathogen still spreads as before. The indirect protection of herd immunity comes from breaking chains of transmission.

Photosynthesis & Respiration

Where does a giant tree get the material to build itself? (Hint: mostly from the air!) Plants pack sunlight into sugar, and almost every living thing then breaks that sugar apart for energy. Step inside a leaf cell and watch the chloroplast and the mitochondrion trade carbon dioxide, water, oxygen and glucose.

Two reactions that run in opposite directions

The outputs of photosynthesis are exactly the inputs of respiration, and the other way round. Switch between words and formulas to read both equations.

🌿Photosynthesis

Where: chloroplasts of plant and algae cells · needs light

Light energy is captured by chlorophyll and stored as chemical energy in glucose.

🔥Cellular respiration

Where: mitochondria of almost all cells (plants too!) · day and night

The energy stored in glucose is released and turned into ATP, the energy the cell can use directly.

🌿 Chloroplast

Captures energy → makes glucose, releases O₂

⇄
glucose + O₂ ⟶ ⟵ CO₂ + H₂O

🔥 Mitochondrion

Releases energy → makes ATP, gives off CO₂ + H₂O

⚠️ Common mix-up:“Plants do photosynthesis, animals do respiration” is wrong! Plants have mitochondria too and respire day and night. In daylight, photosynthesis is usually faster than respiration, so overall a plant gives off oxygen.

Lab: balance the sealed jar

A sealed jar holds water, a water plant (elodea) and a snail. The water contains bromothymol blue (BTB) indicator: it is blue when CO₂ is low and turns yellow when CO₂ is high. Change the conditions and see who survives.

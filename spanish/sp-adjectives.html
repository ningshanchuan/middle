Make the endings agree

In a simple noun phrase, the adjective usually goes after the noun. To describe someone in a sentence, use ser: es with singular subjects like la chica, and son with plural subjects like las chicas. The adjective agrees with the noun or subject.

Adjective patternMasculine singularFeminine singularMasculine pluralFeminine plural
-o / -aalto alta altos altas

Many consonant endingsjoven joven jóvenes jóvenes
Some consonant endingstrabajador trabajadora trabajadores trabajadoras
Una chica alta. La chica es alta.A tall girl. The girl is tall.
Es un poco alto. Es bastante alto. Es muy alto.He’s a little tall. He’s quite tall. He’s very tall.

Memory tipFind the noun first. For many adjectives, the ending shows gender and a final s signals plural.

Common mix-upMuy, un poco and bastante stay the same here. Change the adjective in muy alta → muy altas.

Culture note · Describe a basilica’s towers

Look at the towers of the Sagrada Família in Barcelona. Torres is feminine plural, so the adjective is altas.

Las torres son altas. The towers are tall.

An adjective can change meaning with position

Un amigo viejo usually describes an older friend. Un viejo amigo usually means a longtime friend. Look at which side of amigo the adjective is on.

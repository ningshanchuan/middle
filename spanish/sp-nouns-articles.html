Nouns and gender

Learn each noun with its article

Spanish nouns have grammatical gender. Words for things can be masculine or feminine, like el libro (book) and la mesa (table). Endings give clues.

EndingUsual genderExamples
Common mix-up Remember el día (day), el mapa (map) and la mano (hand). Their endings can mislead you.

Build a noun phrase

Why do we say el águila for “the eagle” and las águilas in the plural?

Show the answer

Águila is feminine. Many feminine nouns that start with a stressed a sound take el right before the singular noun. Listen to el águila blanca and las águilas blancas. The adjective stays feminine.

Make nouns plural

Singular ends in…ChangeSingular → plural
Memory tip Check two things when making a plural. Change the noun ending and switch the article to its plural form.

Watch the accent in lápiz → lápices. Both forms stress the first syllable. The singular needs an accent because it stresses the next-to-last syllable and ends in z. The plural needs one because it stresses the third syllable from the end.

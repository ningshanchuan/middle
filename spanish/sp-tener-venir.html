The Verbs tener and venir

Two patterns in the present tense

Both verbs have a yo form in go. With tú, él / ella / usted, and ellos / ellas / ustedes, the stressed stem changes from e to ie. It keeps e with nosotros and vosotros.

Subjecttener · to havevenir · to come
yo Itengo vengo
tú you (informal singular)tienes vienes
él / ella / usted he / she / you (formal singular)tiene viene
nosotros / nosotras wetenemos venimos
vosotros / vosotras you all (informal, Spain)tenéis venís
ellos / ellas / ustedes they / you alltienen vienen

In Latin America, use ustedes for “you all.” In most of Spain, vosotros / vosotras is the usual informal plural.

Memory tipPair tengo and vengo. They rhyme.

Common mix-upFor age, say Tengo doce años. Spanish uses tener to give someone’s age.

Where are they coming from?

Use venir de for an origin and venir a for a destination. When the next word is the article el, a + el becomes al and de + el becomes del.

Vengo de casa. Dani viene al parque.I’m coming from home. Dani is coming to the park.

A walk in the park

Luz sends a voice message from the park. Find out where she came from and what she has to do.

Hola, soy Luz. Tengo doce años. Ya estoy en el parque. Vengo de casa. Mi primo Dani viene al parque también. Tiene sed y yo tengo hambre.

Ahora tengo que preparar una merienda. Tengo agua para Dani.

See the translation

Hi, I’m Luz. I’m twelve. I’m at the park now. I came from home. My cousin Dani is coming to the park too. He’s thirsty and I’m hungry. Now I have to prepare a snack. I have water for Dani.

Culture note · Machu Picchu, Peru

At Machu Picchu, Inca buildings and terraces trace the shape of a ridge in the Andes. Try saying you’re coming from Peru.

Vengo de Perú. I’m coming from Peru.

Coming here and going there

At the park, Luz says Dani viene al parque. From home, Dani can say Voy al parque. The speaker’s point of view helps you choose venir or ir.

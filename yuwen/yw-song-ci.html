Song lyrics and the rules of classical verse

“Shui Diao Ge Tou” isn’t the title of Su Shi’s moon poem — it’s the name of a tune. Song-dynasty poets “filled in words” (填词) to existing melodies, a bit like writing new lyrics to a song you know. Each tune fixes how long every line is and where the rhymes fall.

Your mission: open up the “boxes” of a ci tune, hear the rise and fall of level and oblique tones (平仄 píngzè), catch every rhyme, and get to know Su Shi, Fan Zhongyan, Li Qingzhao and Xin Qiji.

Mission 1: ci tunes — each tune is a set of boxes

Pick a tune. Each square is one character; orange squares are rhymes. Ci lines are long and short — that’s why ci are also called 长短句 (“long-and-short lines”). A regulated poem (律诗) has the same number of characters in every line.

Ci in a nutshell

  • Ci began in the Sui–Tang era and flowered in the Song (960–1279). They were song lyrics, sung to music — hence 曲子词 (“song words”).
  • The tune name (词牌) fixes the number of characters, line lengths, tones and rhymes. A topic title may follow it: 《渔家傲·秋思》 = tune “The Fisherman’s Pride”, topic “Autumn Thoughts”.
  • Most ci have two stanzas (双调 shuāngdiào): 上阕 (upper) and 下阕 (lower).
  • Two styles are often named: bold and unrestrained (豪放 háofàng: Su Shi, Xin Qiji) and delicate and graceful (婉约 wǎnyuē: Liu Yong, Li Qingzhao).
⚠️ Don’t mix up:The tune is not the topic. Fan Zhongyan and Li Qingzhao both wrote to 渔家傲 — one about a cold frontier, the other about a dream voyage across the sky.

Mission 2: see the tones and the rhymes

Blue = level tone (平 píng), orange = oblique tone (仄 zè). Press “Guess from modern tones”: tones 1–2 count as level, 3–4 as oblique — but some characters come out wrong! Most are old “entering-tone” characters (入声 rùshēng), a short clipped sound Mandarin has lost. Tap every character the modern guess gets wrong.

Mission 3: read the ci — rhyme detective

Choose a ci and listen (each sentence lights up as it is read). Then switch on “Rhyme detective” and tap every rhyming character. Find them all with no wrong taps to clear that ci.

What does “bold” (豪放) ci sound like? ▸

Big subjects — war, hunting, the frontier, life itself — in strong, sweeping language. Su Shi: “I’ll draw my carved bow like a full moon, look northwest and shoot the Wolf Star.”

What does “graceful” (婉约) ci sound like? ▸

Personal feelings and small moments in light, flowing language — Li Qingzhao rowing into the lotus flowers. But her 渔家傲 is bold and soaring: style labels are only a guide.

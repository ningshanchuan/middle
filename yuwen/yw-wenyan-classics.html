Classical masterpieces: a hidden valley, a tower, a pavilion

A fisherman squeezes through a cave and finds a village nobody has disturbed for about 600 years. An official writes that a leader should “worry before everyone else worries, and be happy only after everyone else is happy.” Nearly every middle-schooler in China can recite these five texts. Can you crack them?

⚠️ Don’t mix up:记 (jì, “a record”) describes a place or event and then reflects on it; 说 (shuō, “a discourse”) uses a thing to make a point — like 《爱莲说》. A 记 isn’t always a travel diary: 《岳阳楼记》 was written to mark the tower’s rebuilding.

Mission 2: walk into Peach Blossom Spring

Explore: tap the circles to see how the text describes each spot. Find it: read a sentence and tap the place it describes — you retrace the fisherman’s route in the order of the story.

Why could nobody find it again? ▸

Peach Blossom Spring is Tao Yuanming’s imagined ideal society — no war, no oppression, everyone living in peace. “They got lost and never found the road again” tells us such a place doesn’t exist in real life. Tao (365?–427) lived through civil wars; the story shows his dream of a better life and his criticism of his own times. English borrowed a word from a similar idea: utopia (Thomas More, 1516) — and Chinese still says 世外桃源 for a perfect hidden paradise.

Tip 1: look for matching halves ▸

Classical writers love balance: two halves with the same shape split in the middle.

Tip 2: find 也, 矣, 乎, 焉 ▸

These particles usually end a sentence. The many 也 in 《醉翁亭记》 work like built-in full stops!

Standard: 《义务教育语文课程标准（2022年版）》 stage 4 (grades 7–9): read simple classical Chinese with notes and reference tools; task groups “literary reading and creative expression” and “accumulating and organising language”. All five texts are on the standard’s recommended recitation list.

Textbook (统编版): 爱莲说 — Grade 7 vol. 2, unit 4 (短文二篇); 桃花源记 and 小石潭记 — Grade 8 vol. 2, unit 3; 岳阳楼记 and 醉翁亭记 — Grade 9 vol. 1, unit 3. 《论语》十二章 and 《世说新语》二则 are in the lesson “Classical Chinese: First Steps”.

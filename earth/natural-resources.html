⚠️ Common mix-up:“Renewable” does not mean “no impact”. Wind farms use land and dams change rivers and fish habitats. “Nonrenewable” means it takes millions of years to form, so we use it up far faster than nature can replace it.

How do fossil fuels form?

Coal, oil and natural gas store sunlight captured by living things millions of years ago. Look at the rock layers on the front of the 3D model: a black coal seam, plus oil and gas trapped under a cap rock.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E6a (renewable/sustainable energy resources such as hydro, solar, wind, geothermal, tidal and biomass vs. nonrenewable resources such as nuclear (uranium) and fossil fuels (oil, coal, natural gas), and how they are used in everyday life) and S6E6b (design and evaluate solutions for sustaining energy resources: conservation, reduce, reuse, recycle). Try: use the “Follow the energy” story to discuss which sources trace back to the Sun; then have students compare an all-fossil plan with a renewables-plus-storage plan in the planner, arguing from evidence about emissions, cost and reliability.

Note: planner numbers are simplified approximations for comparison (e.g. coal ≈ 1 t CO₂/MWh, natural gas ≈ 0.45 t).

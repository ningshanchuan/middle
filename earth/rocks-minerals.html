The Rock Cycle: A Grain’s Great Journey

Could the rock in your driveway once have been red-hot lava, or the sand of an ancient beach? Minerals are the building blocks of rocks. And rocks don’t stay the same forever: they melt, cool, crumble into sediment, get pressed together, or change under heat and pressure. Follow one grain around the rock cycle, then become a mineral detective in the lab!

Three rock families, sorted by how they form

Geologists don’t classify rocks by color. They classify them by how they formed.

🌋

Igneous

Form when magma or lava cools and hardens. Slow cooling underground → big crystals (granite). Fast cooling at the surface → tiny or no crystals (basalt, obsidian).

🏖️

Sedimentary

Form when bits of rock, mud or shells pile up in layers and get compacted and cemented. Often layered, and the rock where fossils are found (sandstone, limestone, shale).

🔥

Metamorphic

Form when existing rock is changed by heat and pressure without melting. Minerals rearrange, often into bands (limestone → marble, shale → slate, granite → gneiss).

⚠️ Common mix-up:The rock cycle has no fixed order! Any rock type can become either of the other two, or be weathered or melted and start over.
💡 Georgia rocksStone Mountain is a huge body of granite-like igneous rock that cooled slowly underground. White marble from Tate in north Georgia is metamorphic; the statue of Lincoln in the Lincoln Memorial is carved from it. Georgia’s state mineral is staurolite and its state gem is quartz.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E5b (plan and carry out an investigation of the characteristics of minerals and how minerals contribute to rock composition) and S6E5c (explain how to classify rocks by their formation and how rocks change through geologic processes in the rock cycle). Ideas: ① use the 3D “grain’s journey” slider and have students name the process at each stage; ② in the mineral lab, require tests before an ID and have students justify it with the hardness range; ③ after the rock-cycle game, students sketch their route and compare with classmates, stressing that the cycle has no fixed order.

Safety: in a real lab never taste minerals; use dilute acid or vinegar for the fizz test and wear goggles.

Climate: What Controls It?

Why is Singapore hot all year while Moscow freezes every winter? Why is one side of a mountain a forest and the other side a desert? Move the sunbeam on the 3D globe, raise a mountain range, build climate graphs and turn up the greenhouse gases to discover what controls climate.

🌦️Weather

The condition of the atmosphere at one place over a short time (hours to days): today’s temperature, rain and wind.

📅Climate

The average weather pattern of a region over many years (usually 30 or more).

⚠️ Common mix-up:The equator is not hotter because it is closer to the Sun. Every place on Earth is almost the same distance from the Sun. What matters is the angle of sunlight: direct rays concentrate energy on a small area, while slanted rays spread the same energy over a bigger area.

Simulation: the greenhouse effect

Sunlight (yellow) passes through the air and warms the ground. The ground gives off heat as infrared light (red). Greenhouse gases absorb that infrared and send it back out in all directions, some of it back down to the ground. Change the amount of CO₂ and watch Earth’s long-term average temperature.

💡 Memory hintGreenhouse gases act like a blanket: sunlight gets in, but heat has a harder time getting out. A thicker blanket means a warmer Earth.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E4 (how uneven heating of Earth by the Sun and interactions of the atmosphere and oceans produce weather and climate patterns), connecting to S6E4a (greenhouse gases) and S6E4b (solar energy heating land, water and air). Try: compare the spot area and energy per m² at 0°, 40° and 70°; switch between June and December to discuss the cause of seasons; in rain-shadow mode, change the mountain height and have students use evidence to explain the dry leeward side. In the climograph section, ask which city is controlled by altitude, latitude, ocean or hemisphere.

Data notes: city data are approximate long-term averages; the greenhouse model uses a simplified “about 3 °C of long-term warming per doubling of CO₂” relationship to show the trend only.

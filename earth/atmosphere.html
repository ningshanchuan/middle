The Atmosphere: Earth’s Blanket of Air

Why does a bag of chips puff up on a mountain road, and why do your ears pop in an airplane? We live at the bottom of an ocean of air. Slice open the atmosphere below to see its five layers, ride a probe all the way up to space to watch temperature and pressure change, then discover how uneven heating by the Sun makes the wind.

⚠️ Common mix-up:“Higher means colder” is only true in the troposphere and mesosphere. In the stratosphere (ozone absorbs UV) and the thermosphere (thin gas absorbs high-energy sunlight), temperature actually rises with height. Look how the graph above zig-zags!

💡 Greenhouse gasesCarbon dioxide, water vapor and methane are tiny parts of air, but they absorb heat (infrared) given off by the ground and keep Earth warm. Adding more of them warms the planet.

Simulation: sea breeze & land breeze

The Sun shines on land and sea alike, but land heats up fast and cools down fast, while water heats and cools slowly. The temperature difference makes a pressure difference, and air moves: that is wind. Drag the time slider or press Play to watch a whole day.

💡 Memory hintWind blows from High to Low pressure. Warm air rises → Low (L); cool air sinks → High (H). Winds are named for where they come FROM: a sea breeze comes from the sea.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E4a (composition and characteristics of atmospheric layers, including the ozone layer and greenhouse gases), S6E4b (the Sun heats air, land and water at different rates) and S6E4c (unequal heating plus Earth’s rotation cause local and global winds). Try: have students predict “Is it always colder higher up?”, then test with the probe slider and temperature graph; use the True-thickness button to discuss how the model exaggerates; in the breeze sim, ask why the wind reverses between day and night.

Data notes: the temperature curve uses approximate standard-atmosphere values; thermosphere temperatures vary from about 500 to 2,000 °C with solar activity. Pressure is estimated as halving roughly every 5.5 km.

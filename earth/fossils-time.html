What kinds of fossils are there?

A fossil is the preserved remains or traces of an ancient living thing. Different ways of forming give different kinds of fossils.

🫙

Mold

An organism is pressed into mud, then dissolves away, leaving a hollow imprint in the rock.

e.g. the hollow print of a shell
🗿

Cast

Minerals fill a mold, making a solid rock copy of the organism’s outside shape.

e.g. a rock in the shape of a trilobite
🪵

Petrified (mineralized)

Minerals from groundwater fill the pores of bone or wood, or replace it entirely, turning it to stone.

e.g. petrified wood, dinosaur bones
🌿

Carbon film

Soft things like leaves are squashed flat, leaving only a thin black layer of carbon, like a photo in stone.

e.g. fern leaves in shale
🐾

Trace fossil

Evidence of what an organism did, not of its body.

e.g. footprints, burrows, fossil dung
🍯

Preserved remains

The actual organism is kept almost unchanged, usually trapped in amber, ice or tar.

e.g. insects in amber, frozen mammoths

Fossils are evidence that Earth has changed

🏔️

Sea fossils on mountaintops

The limestone at the top of Mount Everest contains fossils of sea creatures: the rock formed on a sea floor and was later lifted up by plate movement.

🧊

Coal and ferns in Antarctica

Frozen Antarctica holds coal and fossils of warm-climate plants: its climate was once warm, and the continent was once in a different place.

🦴

Same fossils, different continents

Fossils of Mesosaurus, a freshwater reptile, are found in both South America and Africa, showing these continents were once joined.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Most living things never become fossils! They usually rot or get eaten. Only organisms with hard parts that are buried quickly have a good chance, so the fossil record is incomplete.

🚀 Advanced: dating with half-lives (absolute age)

Radioactive “parent” atoms decay into “daughter” atoms at a steady rate. In each half-life, half of the parent atoms that are left decay.

🔢

Relative dating

Compares positions of layers to tell which is older or younger, without exact ages. Like knowing you are older than your brother without knowing either age.

⚛️

Absolute dating

Uses radioactive elements that decay at a steady rate (half-life) to find a rock’s age in years. Works best on igneous rock.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E5 (especially S6E5f): explain how fossils show evidence of Earth’s changing surface and climate, and use the law of superposition to find the relative ages of rock layers and fossils. Suggested use: have students dig layer by layer with the slider and name the environment each layer’s fossils point to (sea → swamp → desert → sea). Then click the dike and ask what “the dike is 50 million years old” tells us about layers 6 and 7 (combining relative and absolute dating).

Note: the 3D block is a teaching composite; real places rarely show every age in one spot, and ages are approximate. Common misconceptions: humans lived alongside dinosaurs; every dead organism becomes a fossil; a fossil is the original bone.

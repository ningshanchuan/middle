    Where is Earth’s water?

    Earth is called the “blue planet”, yet the fresh water we can use is surprisingly scarce. Click the bar or the list to zoom in, level by level.

    🍼

    If all of Earth’s water fit in a 1-liter bottle…

    🌊 Oceans and other salt water≈ 975 mL
    🧊 Ice caps and glaciers≈ 17 mL
    💧 Fresh groundwater≈ 7.5 mL
    🏞️ All lakes and rivers≈ 1 drop!
    💡 Memory hint“97 salty, 3 fresh, and most of the fresh is frozen”: about 97% of water is salt water, about 3% is fresh, and about two-thirds of that fresh water is ice.
    ⚠️ Common mix-up:The water cycle does not make new water; Earth’s total amount of water stays about the same. That is why polluting and wasting fresh water is a real problem.

    👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

    Supports GSE S6E3: the significant role of water in Earth processes, including where water is found on Earth (oceans, glaciers, lakes, rivers, groundwater, atmosphere) and a model of the water cycle powered by the Sun’s energy and gravity. Suggested use: switch the Sun off and have students notice that evaporation nearly stops and clouds fade; drop the temperature below 0 °C to see snowfall and snowpack (ice storage) grow; then use the live water counts under the stage to discuss which reservoir is biggest and where water stays longest.

    Note: the model’s “drops” are simplified to show pathways, not true proportions. Common misconceptions: clouds are water vapor; the cycle has a start and an end; groundwater is an underground river.

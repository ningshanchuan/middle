Asteroids, Comets & Meteors

66 million years ago, a rock about 10 km wide ended the age of the dinosaurs. In 2022, NASA crashed a spacecraft into an asteroid on purpose and changed its orbit. Where are the solar system’s leftovers, could one hit us, and what could we do about it?

🎯 What you’ll be able to do: tell asteroids, comets and meteoroids apart and say where they live, explain why a comet’s tail points away from the Sun, estimate an impact’s energy, and push a dangerous asteroid off course the smart way.

A comet’s journey along a long, thin ellipse

Scrub through time: far from the Sun the comet is a frozen “dirty snowball”; close to the Sun it grows two tails. Watch: the tails always point away from the Sun, even when the comet is heading away from it.

Meteor shower calendar: Earth crosses comet dust trails

🛰️ Planetary defense: nudge the asteroid!

An asteroid will hit Earth when the warning time runs out. Like NASA’s DART mission, you will ram it with impactor spacecraft. Choose when to hit, which way to push and how many impactors to launch. It must miss Earth’s centre by at least 10,000 km (more than Earth’s radius, because Earth’s gravity pulls it in).

Impactors:1

Simplified physics: each impactor is 600 kg at 6 km/s with momentum boost β = 3 (DART measured about 3.6); asteroid density 2,000 kg/m³. Pushing along the direction of motion changes the orbital period, so the shift keeps growing (about 3 × Δv × t); sideways or sunward pushes do not keep adding up.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E1e (compare and contrast the characteristics, composition and location of comets, asteroids and meteoroids), with links to S6E1d (gravity and inertia govern motion) and S6E1c (interpreting data). Suggested flow: ① in the 3D map, have students order the asteroid belt, Trojans, Kuiper Belt and Oort Cloud by distance; ② use the comet simulator to prove that tails point away from the Sun and compare speeds at perihelion and aphelion (Kepler’s second law); ③ impact lab: keep the size fixed and double the speed: how many times more energy? ④ meteor calendar: why the same dates every year? ⑤ planetary defense game: why does early warning matter so much?

Beyond grade 6: Kepler’s equation and the vis-viva equation, kinetic energy E = ½mv², crater scaling laws, the momentum-transfer factor β and the “3 Δv t” drift from orbital mechanics. Students can explore the trends without doing the algebra.

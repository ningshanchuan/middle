Four big ideas

Day, night and seasons all come from Earth’s motion. Tell rotation from revolution, add tilt and direct sunlight, and you can explain it all.

🌍

Rotation → day & night

Earth spins west to east on its axis once every 24 hours. The half facing the Sun has day; the half facing away has night.

🔄

Revolution → a year

Earth orbits the Sun once every 365¼ days. The extra ¼ day adds up to a leap day (Feb 29) every 4 years.

📐

Tilt of 23.5°

The axis tilts about 23.5° and always points toward Polaris. So for half the year the north leans toward the Sun, and for the other half the south does.

🔦

Direct vs. slanted rays

A high Sun concentrates light on a small area, so the ground heats quickly. A low Sun spreads the same light over a big area. Add longer or shorter days, and you get seasons.

Closest to the Sun in early January!

Around January 3 Earth is closest to the Sun (perihelion, about 147 million km); around July 4 it is farthest (aphelion, about 152 million km). Yet January is winter in the north! Distance changes only about 3%, a tiny effect.

⇅

Opposite seasons in each hemisphere

When Georgia has summer, Australia has winter. If distance caused seasons, the whole planet would have summer at the same time, and it doesn’t.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Seasons are NOT caused by Earth getting closer to or farther from the Sun. They happen because of the tilt: the hemisphere leaning toward the Sun gets more direct sunlight and longer days.

🚀 Advanced: the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation
🎯 Challenge
Rocket equation Δv = vₑ × ln(m₀ ÷ m_f): across is the mass ratio (full ÷ empty), up is the change in speed you can get. The curve flattens to the right: doubling the fuel adds only a little Δv. That is why rockets use stages. Dashed: about 9.4 km/s to reach low orbit (including losses); 11.2 km/s to escape Earth.

📡 How long does a radio signal take?

Radio waves travel at the speed of light, about 300,000 km every second. That is why nobody can steer a Mars rover with a joystick: rovers have to avoid rocks on their own.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E1 (current scientific views of the universe and how they evolved; S6E1a: scientific theories change as new information is added). Telescopes and space probes keep adding new evidence — for example, Galileo’s view of Jupiter’s moons supported the Sun-centered model. Heights and times in the 3D launch are compressed, and the telescope models are diagrams. Good questions: Why can a rocket speed up in space where there is no air? (It pushes on its own exhaust gas, not on air.) Why must infrared and X-ray telescopes go to space? (The atmosphere absorbs those kinds of light.)

Links to S8P3 (Newton’s laws): use the rocket simulation to compare thrust-to-weight ratios below 1, just above 1 and well above 1, and discuss why staging helps (dropping empty tanks lowers the mass).

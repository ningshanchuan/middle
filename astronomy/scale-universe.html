The Scale of the Universe

If the Sun were a basketball, Earth would be a peppercorn 26 meters (85 feet) away. So where would the nearest star be? About 6,900 km (4,300 miles) away: roughly Atlanta to London! Here you will zoom by powers of ten, from a kid all the way out to the edge of the observable universe.

🎯 You’ll be able to: write huge distances as powers of ten, convert between km, astronomical units and light-years, and explain why space is mostly empty.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E1 (scientific views of the universe: the relative sizes and distances of the solar system, stars and galaxies; units such as the light-year). The powers-of-ten zoom, the light-speed race and the basketball solar system all use real data (NASA Planetary Fact Sheet, JPL orbital elements), and objects in the zoom are drawn at true scale.

This goes beyond grade 6: scientific notation and logarithmic scales; light-travel time (we always see the past); and cosmic expansion, which makes “how long the light travelled” different from “how far away it is now” (the oldest light travelled for 13.8 billion years, but the matter that emitted it is now about 46 billion light-years away).

Activities: build the basketball solar system outdoors with a real ball and peppercorns (Earth at 26 m, Jupiter at 134 m); have students record the view width at each zoom stop and rewrite it in scientific notation.

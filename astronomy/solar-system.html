☀️ How long does sunlight take, and how strong is it?

Light covers about 300,000 km every second. The farther a planet, the later sunlight arrives, and it fades by the inverse-square rule: twice as far means only 1/4 as bright.

Data: NASA Planetary Fact Sheet (mean distances, rounded). Light time = distance ÷ speed of light; sunlight strength = 1 ÷ distance² (Earth = 100%).

⚠️ Common mix-up:Outer planets don’t take longer only because their path is longer: they also move more slowly. Neptune (about 5.4 km/s) moves at roughly a fifth of Earth’s speed (about 29.8 km/s).

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E1c (analyze and interpret data to compare and contrast the planets by size relative to Earth, surface and atmospheric features, relative distance from the Sun, and ability to support life) and S6E1e (compare the characteristics, composition and location of comets, asteroids and meteoroids), and sets up S6E1d (gravity and inertia govern motion in the solar system). Suggested flow: in the easy view have students name the planets in order, then switch on True distances and ask why posters cannot be drawn to scale. Use the time slider to compare how fast Mercury and Neptune orbit (“farther from the Sun → longer year”). The scale-model calculator works well as a real walk across the school field.

Data: NASA planetary fact sheets (rounded). Moon counts change as new moons are discovered.

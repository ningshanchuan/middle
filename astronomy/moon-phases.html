Phases of the Moon

Why does the Moon look like a thin smile one week and a full circle two weeks later? The Moon makes no light of its own. It reflects sunlight, and half of it is always lit. 🌗

Half is always lit

The Sun always lights the half of the Moon facing it. A phase is just how much of that lit half we can see from Earth.

🔄

A 29.5-day cycle

From one new Moon to the next takes about 29.5 days, roughly one month. The word “month” comes from “Moon”.

↔️

Waxing, then waning

After new Moon the lit part we see grows each night (waxing). After full Moon it shrinks (waning).

⚠️ Common mix-up:Phases are NOT caused by Earth’s shadow! At first quarter, Earth’s shadow is nowhere near the Moon. Earth’s shadow falls on the Moon only during a lunar eclipse.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E2a: develop and use a model to demonstrate the phases of the Moon by showing the relative positions of the Sun, Earth, and Moon.

Target the misconception “phases are Earth’s shadow”: stop the slider at first quarter and ask where Earth’s shadow points (to the right, away from the Moon). Turn on “8 phases” and stress that every small Moon is half lit, with the lit side facing the Sun.

Extension: press the hemisphere button to discuss why people in Australia see the phase flipped. Remind students that sizes and distances are not to scale (the real Earth–Moon distance is about 30 Earth diameters).

How does the model take a step?

Each star feels the pull of the others. We choose model units for distance, mass and time so the gravity constant G has a value of 1. The clock counts model seconds. Velocity Verlet updates the motion every 0.0005 model seconds. In B and C, a softening length ε = 0.008 keeps acceleration finite during close passes. In A and the two-star preset, ε = 0 gives ordinary Newtonian gravity.

The line in the sky view marks the distance r between stars 1 and 2. Move the time slider to see how much acceleration star 2 alone gives star 1. The mass of star 2 is m₂.

Worked example · Double the distance

Given G = 1 and m₂ = 1 in the two-star preset, use ordinary gravity. Suppose the stars’ separation changes from 1 to 2 distance units. Find the acceleration that star 2 gives star 1 at each distance.

Set ε = 0 and substitute into the formula. r = 1: a = 1/1² = 1 distance units per model second² r = 2: a = 1/2² = 0.25 distance units per model second² The acceleration falls from 1 to 0.25 distance units per model second², one quarter as much.

From two stars to three

Two stars have a simple rule

In an ideal, isolated pair, two bound stars that do not collide keep tracing ellipses around their shared center of mass. A circle is a special ellipse. Try the two-star control and see whether the stars retrace their paths.

A third star changes the possibilities

As the three stars move, all their pulls change. There’s no simple general formula for their motion from arbitrary starting conditions. Henri Poincaré’s work revealed how complicated the motion can get. In chaos (混沌, hùndùn), tiny differences at the start can grow into big differences later, even under the same rules.

Why weather gets hard to predict

Measurements of temperature and wind always have some uncertainty. Like the 0.001 shift in the butterfly experiment, tiny starting differences can grow as the atmosphere moves. Research puts the limit for day-to-day forecasts in midlatitudes, where much of the United States lies, at about two weeks.

What if your home had three suns?

Liu Cixin’s The Three-Body Problem is a science-fiction novel for older readers. It won the 2015 Hugo Award for Best Novel. Its Trisolaran world has three suns. During Stable Eras, days and nights follow a pattern. During Chaotic Eras, sunrise and temperature become unpredictable.

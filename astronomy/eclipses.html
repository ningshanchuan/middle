Solar & Lunar Eclipses

How can the small Moon hide the giant Sun? Earth and the Moon both cast long shadows into space. When the Sun, Earth and Moon line up exactly, one body’s shadow falls on the other: that is an eclipse. Move the Moon and see where the shadows land!

Six kinds of eclipse

A solar eclipse happens at new Moon, when the Moon blocks the Sun. A lunar eclipse happens at full Moon, when Earth’s shadow falls on the Moon. Which part of the shadow you are in decides what you see.

Solar eclipse 🌑Lunar eclipse 🌕
Line-upSun – Moon – EarthSun – Earth – Moon
Moon phaseNew MoonFull Moon
Whose shadow on whomMoon’s shadow on EarthEarth’s shadow on the Moon

⚠️ Common mix-up:A lunar eclipse is not a Moon phase. Phases happen every month and come from how much of the lit half we see. A lunar eclipse happens only when Earth’s shadow actually falls on a full Moon. And phases are never caused by Earth’s shadow.

Why isn’t there an eclipse every month?

The Moon’s orbit is tilted about 5.1° to the plane of Earth’s orbit (the ecliptic). The Moon crosses that plane at only two points, called the nodes. An eclipse can happen only when a new or full Moon falls close to a node. Scrub through a real year and watch the “eclipse seasons” appear.

💡 Memory hintThe line of nodes has to point at the Sun to make an eclipse season. There are about two seasons a year, about 173 days apart. The line of nodes turns backward about 19° a year, so eclipse seasons come about 19 days earlier each year.

🔁 The saros: eclipses on repeat

Ancient Babylonian astronomers noticed that almost the same eclipse returns every 18 years 11 days 8 hours (one saros = 223 lunar months). The extra 8 hours let Earth turn one third of a turn, so the repeat happens about 120° farther west.

Example: 2027 → 2045

The total solar eclipse of 2 August 2027 belongs to saros series 136. One saros later comes the total eclipse of 12 August 2045, which crosses the USA from coast to coast.

Why 223 months?

223 lunar months (about 6,585.3 days) almost exactly equals 242 “draconic months” (the time for the Moon to return to the same node) and 239 “anomalistic months” (perigee to perigee). So the phase, the node and the Moon’s distance all repeat together.

Jump assumes the same take-off speed everywhere (a 0.5 m jump on Earth, about 3.1 m/s). Height = v²/(2g), hang time = 2v/g. Space-suit weight is ignored.

💡 Memory hintMass (kg) = how much stuff you are made of, never changes. Weight = the force of gravity on you (newtons), changes from world to world.

🏐 Drop a ball on four worlds at once

0.0 s

Distance = ½ g t². Four times the gravity only halves the fall time, because time goes as 1/√g.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Heavy things do not fall faster. In 1971 Apollo 15 astronaut David Scott dropped a hammer and a feather on the airless Moon: they hit the ground together.

🪐 Why do Saturn’s rings “disappear”?

2026

Saturn’s spin axis is tipped 26.7° and keeps pointing the same way in space. Saturn takes 29.5 years to orbit the Sun, so from Earth we sometimes see the north face of the rings, sometimes the south face, and about every 15 years we see them exactly edge-on, almost invisible.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E1c (analyze and interpret data to compare and contrast the planets by size, surface and atmospheric features, relative distance from the Sun, and ability to support life) and S6E1d (gravity and inertia govern motion in the solar system). Suggested flow: ① in “Compare worlds” have students predict first (is Ganymede or Mercury bigger?); ② in the Gravity lab separate mass from weight and use t = √(2h/g) to explain why 4× gravity only halves the fall time; ③ use the time slider to count the 4 : 2 : 1 laps; ④ read the charts: which planet breaks the “farther = colder” pattern (Venus, greenhouse effect)? ⑤ discuss the water map and the needs for life (liquid water, energy, chemistry).

Beyond grade 6: the jump formula h = v²/(2g), free fall d = ½gt², the Laplace resonance (λ₁ − 3λ₂ + 2λ₃ = 180°), tidal heating, and the simple “no-air temperature model”. Students can notice the patterns without doing the algebra.

Data: NASA Planetary Fact Sheet (nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov) and NASA Solar System Exploration. Moon counts change with new discoveries (Saturn’s 293 is the August 2026 count).

The zodiac: why do the constellations change with the seasons?

Earth takes a year to go around the Sun. Seen from Earth, the Sun seems to creep in front of a ring of constellations; its path is called the ecliptic, and the constellations along it are the zodiac. The constellation behind the Sun is up only in the daytime, lost in the glare; the one on the opposite side is high in the south at midnight. Drag the time bar to carry Earth around its year.

📊 When the Sun is really in front of each constellation

Data: constellation boundaries set by the International Astronomical Union (IAU) in 1930; dates are approximate and can shift by a day from year to year. Horoscope dates come from a system set up about 2,000 years ago and are not science.

Why don’t the dates match the horoscope?

Earth’s axis slowly wobbles like a spinning top, making one circle about every 26,000 years (precession). Since the sign dates were fixed about 2,000 years ago, the Sun’s position has slipped by almost a month, so for most people the Sun was really in a different constellation on their birthday.

A 13th zodiac constellation?

The ecliptic also crosses Ophiuchus, the Serpent Bearer. The Sun is in front of Ophiuchus from late November to mid-December, much longer than its week in Scorpius. Ancient astrologers left it out to keep 12 equal signs.

Why are the constellations different sizes?

Constellations are just areas of sky with agreed borders, like US states of different sizes. Virgo is huge (the Sun takes over 40 days to cross it), while the ecliptic clips only a corner of Scorpius, crossed in under a week.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S6E1 (current scientific views of the universe and how those views evolved). The 3D celestial sphere uses real right ascension and declination (J2000) for about 110 bright stars; the faint background stars are decoration only. Good questions: Why does Polaris barely move? (It lies almost exactly where Earth’s axis points.) Why can we see Orion in winter but not in summer? (As Earth orbits the Sun, on summer nights our night side faces toward Scorpius, while Orion is on the same side as the Sun and is lost in daylight.)

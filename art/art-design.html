Elements & principles: the rules behind every great poster

Why do some team logos stick in your head while some posters make you look away? Designers have a toolkit: 7 elements (line, shape, form, space, color, value, texture) are the ingredients, and 8 principles (balance, contrast, emphasis, rhythm, movement, unity, variety, proportion) are the recipe.

    👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

    US standards: Georgia Standards of Excellence for Visual Art (2017), grades 6–8: VA6–8.CR.4.a (organize the elements using the principles of design), VA6–8.CR.4.b (express concepts through specific elements or principles), VA6–8.CR.2.c (use technology to create), VA6–8.RE.2.b (use art vocabulary to explain how meaning is conveyed), VA6–8.RE.1.c (images we meet every day and how they shape us). NCAS: VA:Cr2.1.7a, VA:Cr3.1.7a, VA:Re8.1.7a, VA:Re9.1.7a.

    China: 《义务教育艺术课程标准（2022年版）》 Fine Arts, Stage 3 (grades 6–7) tasks “传递我们的创意” (design to communicate ideas: posters, logos) and “营造环境”; competencies 创意实践、艺术表现. Chinese textbooks call these the 形式美法则 — 对称与均衡 (symmetry & balance), 对比与调和 (contrast & harmony), 节奏与韵律 (rhythm), 变化与统一 (variety & unity), 比例 (proportion). Traditional 留白 corresponds to negative space.

    Notes: textbooks list 7–9 principles; we use a common set of 8. The poster’s “visual weight” is a simplified model (area × value difference from the background); contrast uses the WCAG 2.1 relative-luminance formula.

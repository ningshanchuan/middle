Color theory: light vs. paint

What do red light and green light make? Yellow! But red paint plus green paint makes mud-brown. Your screen and your paintbrush follow two opposite rulebooks — learn both and you can control color like an artist or a game designer.

Your mission: mix 7 colors in the light-vs-paint lab, fill 8 orders as Color Chef, build 4 color schemes on the wheel, then pass the final test.

Collect these 7 mixes:
    💡 Why does light get brighter but paint get darker?

    Light is additive: each beam adds more light to your eye, so red + green + blue = white. Paint and ink are subtractive: each pigment absorbs (subtracts) some light and reflects only the rest, so mixing more makes it darker.

    🧑‍🍳 Chef’s secrets

    Two primaries make a secondary (orange, green, violet). Complements (opposite on the wheel) mixed together make brown or gray — painters use this to tone a color down. Add white bit by bit; black is bossy, one drop goes a long way.

    Scheme challenges:

      Where is color? In the light — and in your eyes

      White light is a whole rainbow. Your eyes have three kinds of cone cells, most sensitive to short (blue), medium (green) and long (red) waves — that’s why a screen can fool you with just red, green and blue light.

      📊 The visible spectrum (wavelength, nm)

      Data: visible light ≈ 380–750 nm (textbooks vary slightly). Cone sensitivity peaks ≈ S 420 nm, M 534 nm, L 564 nm (approximate; measurements by Bowmaker & Dartnall, 1980). 1 nm = one billionth of a metre.

      👁️ Your eyes cheat: simultaneous contrast

      Which little square is lighter? Most people say the right one… they are exactly the same gray! Surrounding colors change the color you see.

      Painters use this: put orange next to blue and both look more intense — exactly what Monet did in Impression, Sunrise.

      👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

      US standards: Georgia Standards of Excellence for Visual Art (2017), grades 6–8: VA6–8.CR.4 (organize the elements using the principles; VA7.CR.4.c uses color theory to create visual effects and convey meaning); VA6–8.CR.2.c (use technology to create original art); VA6–8.CN.1.c (knowledge from other disciplines — here, the physics of light and vision). National Core Arts Standards: VA:Cr2.1.7a, VA:Re8.1.7a.

      China: 《义务教育艺术课程标准（2022年版）》 Fine Arts — core competencies 审美感知、艺术表现、创意实践、文化理解; Stage 3 (grades 6–7) learning task “传递我们的创意” (using color to communicate). Junior-high art textbooks (人教版/人美版) have a color unit (hue, value, saturation; warm/cool and feeling). Grade 8 physics (vol. 1: 光的色散, 物体的颜色) covers the primaries of light vs. pigment — a good cross-subject link.

      Notes: paint mixing uses the RYB-cube interpolation of Gossett & Chen (2004) — an approximation; real paints vary by brand and pigment. Printing uses cyan, magenta, yellow + black (CMYK). Traditional Chinese pigments (cinnabar 朱砂, azurite 石青, malachite 石绿, ochre 赭石, gamboge 藤黄) appear in the Chinese Painting lesson.

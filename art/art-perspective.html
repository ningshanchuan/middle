Perspective: depth on a flat page

Stand between straight railroad tracks and they seem to crash together at the horizon — yet they never meet. Around 1420 in Florence, Brunelleschi proved this eye-trick with a mirror. Chinese painters took a different road: the scroll unrolls, and the viewpoint walks along with you.

Your mission: drag vanishing points and the horizon to build a 3D city, find the vanishing point in 5 real artworks, master three tricks that push things far away, then compare the moving viewpoint of the Qingming scroll with Western one-point perspective.

Drawing challenges:
    📏 Three key words

    Horizon line: the level line at the height of your eyes. Vanishing point: where parallel lines going away from you meet on the horizon. Orthogonals: the diagonal lines that run toward a vanishing point. One-point: the object faces you flat; two-point: a corner points at you.

    Mission 2: vanishing-point detective (main game)

    Five real artworks. Find lines that run away from you — floor tiles, ceiling beams, wall edges — imagine extending them, tap where they meet, then press Check. The closer to the true vanishing point, the more stars.

    Mission 3: three tricks that push things far away

    Besides vanishing points, artists use: size shrinks with distance, atmospheric perspective (far things paler, bluer, blurrier) and foreshortening (things pointing at you look shorter). Try each experiment.

      Mission 4: a viewpoint that walks — Chinese “moving” perspective

      A Renaissance painting is like a window: you stand at one fixed spot, look with one eye, and every receding line meets at one vanishing point (focal, or linear perspective). Zhang Zeduan’s Along the River During the Qingming Festival (Northern Song) is over 5 m (17 ft) long and has no single vanishing point: the viewpoint glides along the Bian River like a bird, each section has its own angle, and the slanted lines of buildings often stay parallel (散点透视, “scattered-point” or moving perspective).

      👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

      US standards: Georgia Standards of Excellence for Visual Art (2017), grades 6–8: VA6–8.CR.3 (skills in 2D and 3D work), VA6–8.CR.4.a (organizing space and other elements with the principles), VA6–8.RE.1.a (how time, place and culture appear in artworks), VA6–8.CN.1.b (how history, culture and art influence one another). NCAS: VA:Cr2.1.7a, VA:Re7.2.7a, VA:Cn11.1.7a. Math link: similar triangles and proportion (apparent size ∝ 1/distance).

      China: 《义务教育艺术课程标准（2022年版）》 Fine Arts — 审美感知、艺术表现、文化理解; Stage 3 (grades 6–7) task “领略世界美术的多样性”. Junior-high art textbooks teach 平行透视 (one-point), 成角透视 (two-point) and 近大远小; Chinese painting’s 三远 (Guo Xi) and appreciation of the Qingming scroll fit here too.

      Facts: Brunelleschi’s mirror experiment is usually dated about 1415–1420 (sources differ); Alberti wrote the first theory of perspective, On Painting, in 1435. The Qingming scroll (Palace Museum, Beijing) measures 24.8 × 528.7 cm. The “three distances” come from Guo Xi’s Lofty Message of Forests and Streams (Northern Song). Vanishing-point targets are our own estimates from the images.

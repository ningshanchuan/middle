Peking opera: role types and four skills

A role type, or hángdang, groups characters who share ways of using makeup, voice, and movement. These choices help show who a character is as soon as they enter.

UNESCO inscribed Peking opera on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2010.

Hear the ensemble at work

The jīnghú, a bowed string instrument, has a high, bright sound that supports the singing. Short percussion strikes can cue a movement or mark a pause. Describe one sound you hear as sustained or short.

Watch for a moment when the actor stops moving. Does the accompaniment stop at the same moment?

Painted faces: where does color lead your eye?

Liǎnpǔ is Peking opera makeup painted directly on a performer’s face. The main color and pattern work together to suggest a character’s personality. Read these color conventions alongside the design and the story.

Color a character

Choose a color and tap a patch on the face. You can also paint using the region buttons.

Advanced: change one contrast

Choose Bao Zheng. Keep the black base and change only the crescent from white to black. When is it easier to see? Explain using light–dark contrast, then try a different base color.

Read the face from across the stage

Look for two uses of white. White dominates Cao Cao’s design. The small white patch over a chǒu performer’s nose is a separate makeup convention.

Look at the crescent on Judge Bao’s forehead. Then look back at your design. If the audience sat far away, which patch would be easiest to see?

Spot Peking opera chǒu performer Xiao Changhua in CCTV’s Drunken Concubine and look for the small white patch.

Quick check · painted faces (2 questions)

Stylized movement: what can you see on an empty stage?

A convention, or chéngshì, is a way of performing that actors and audiences recognize. Actors use rhythmic gestures to help the audience picture a door, a horse, or a journey.

Try slowly pushing open an imaginary heavy door. Let your eyes arrive first, then your hand and body.

Kunqu (kūnqǔ)

Kunshan, Jiangsu

Kunqu developed around Kunshan and influenced many other forms, earning the nickname “ancestor of a hundred operas.” UNESCO proclaimed it a Masterpiece in 2001 and inscribed it on its Representative List in 2008. In The Peony Pavilion, listen to how the voice and flute move together.

Cantonese opera and song

Cantonese-speaking Guangdong and Guangxi

New York’s Chinatown has a tradition of staging opera in Cantonese. Yuèqǔ is Cantonese narrative singing, and some of its songs come from opera. UNESCO inscribed Cantonese opera on its Representative List in 2009. Listen to the two performers sing to each other.

Yue opera (yuèjù)

Shengzhou, Zhejiang

Yue opera began in Shengzhou, Zhejiang, and developed further in Shanghai, becoming known for its lyrical singing. In the farewell scene from The Butterfly Lovers, listen to how the singers answer each other.

Huangmei opera (huángméixì)

Huangmei, Hubei, and Anqing, Anhui

Huangmei opera grew from folk traditions including tea-picking songs. It developed around Anqing in Anhui and is also performed in Huangmei, Hubei. In The Heavenly Match, listen to how the Seventh Fairy and Dong Yong converse in song.

Henan opera (yùjù)

Henan

Henan opera, also called Henan bangzi, can have a bright, forceful sound. In Hua Mulan, listen to “Who Says Women Are No Match for Men?” Which words carry the most punch?

Sichuan and Chongqing

World stages: faces, sound, and space

You’ve heard how different opera traditions use voice and movement. Now shift to the audience. How do different stages help people read an expression, hear a voice, or follow the story?

Four places · stage photos, masks, and a recording

Ancient Greece · the theatre at Epidaurus

Ancient Greek actors wore masks to portray characters. The Met’s small terracotta mask depicts a comic character type. Two holes suggest it was hung up. At open-air Epidaurus, stone seats rise around a circular performance area. Sound reflecting off the floor and scattering from the seats can help people hear speech clearly. Background noise also matters.

Turn an ancient Greek terracotta mask

Turn it sideways. How does the slight smile change? Compare it with the exaggerated mouth of the terracotta comic mask beside it.

Mask, Selinus, Sicily, 550 BCE, maker unrecorded · Cleveland Museum of Art, 1943.451 · CC0 · Open on Sketchfab

England · Shakespeare and the Globe

Shakespeare’s company performed at London’s original Globe. At the modern reconstruction, the stage reaches into the audience so spectators can watch from three sides.

If you stood to the left, how could an actor turn so you could see the gesture too?

Japan · Noh and Kabuki

Some Noh characters wear carved masks and perform with a chorus and instruments. Certain Kabuki roles use bold kumadori makeup to give the face force and definition.

Compare the Noh mask, Kabuki makeup, and a Peking opera painted face. Where does shadow do the work? Where do color and line do it?

United States · Broadway musicals

Musicals on New York’s Broadway often weave dialogue, singing, and dance into a story. A tune can reveal what a character wants. An ensemble can make a whole group’s mood visible.

Compare the stages through one lens

Choose two traditions. Name one similarity and one difference, and point to an image or sound as evidence.

Quick check · world stages (2 questions)

Be on stage: one line, two meanings

Read the lines, then perform them with the stage directions. A different look, pause, or gesture can give the same words a different feeling.

Footsteps at the door

  1. (The stage is bare. The keeper hears footsteps and freezes with a hand on an imaginary latch.) Keeper: You came.
  2. (The visitor stops and lets the keeper see their empty hands.) Visitor: I heard you call.
  3. (The keeper slides the latch and takes one step aside.) Keeper: Come in.

Change the intention

Perform alone or with a partner. Replay the line, changing one detail at a time. Try the pause first, then the gesture. Ask an observer to finish the sentence “I thought the character felt … because I saw or heard …”

Advanced: write a stage direction

Write a playable direction for the third line. Turn “feels uneasy” into something an audience can see or hear. Perform it and ask what the audience noticed.

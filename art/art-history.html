Art through history: 40,000 years in one time machine

17,000 years ago someone crawled into a cave in France with a torch and painted a horse on the rock. Over 2,000 years ago the Greeks built the Parthenon in marble; in 1889 Van Gogh painted a swirling night sky from his window. Today artists paint walls with spray cans. Art keeps changing — but why people make it never has.

Your mission: ride the time machine through 10 stops, crack 12 clues in 4 masterpieces with the zoom tool, put 8 artworks in time order, then pass the final test.

    👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

    US standards: Georgia Standards of Excellence for Visual Art (2017), grades 6–8: VA6–8.RE.1.a (time, place and culture in artworks), VA6–8.RE.2.c (describe and analyze aesthetic qualities of specific works), VA6–8.RE.3 (art criticism to build meaning), VA6–8.CN.1.a/b (universal themes across cultures; why art is made and how history, culture and art shape each other). NCAS: VA:Re7.2.7a, VA:Re8.1.7a, VA:Cn11.1.7a. Links to Georgia Social Studies (SS6/SS7 world regions) and China’s Grade 9 World History (统编版《世界历史》: ancient civilizations, the Renaissance).

    China: 《义务教育艺术课程标准（2022年版）》 Fine Arts, Stage 3 (grades 6–7) task “领略世界美术的多样性”; competencies 审美感知、文化理解. Each Western era is paired with China at the same time (Shang–Zhou bronzes, Song landscapes, Ming–Qing porcelain) for a world view.

    Facts (common sources, not textbook text): Lascaux ≈ 17,000 years ago; tomb of Nebamun ≈ 1350 BCE; Parthenon 447–432 BCE; Wang Ximeng’s scroll 1113; Mona Lisa ≈ 1503–1519; Hokusai’s Great Wave ≈ 1831; Monet’s Impression, Sunrise 1872; Van Gogh’s Starry Night 1889; Seurat’s Grande Jatte 1884–86; Mondrian’s Composition II 1930; Berlin’s East Side Gallery 1990. The real Lascaux cave has been closed to the public since 1963; visitors see replicas. Many Lascaux photos on Commons are of replicas — captions say so.

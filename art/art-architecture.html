Listen for the start of each sound and how it fades. Imagine what the sound would tell you with your eyes closed.

Listen into the pause

Some sound reflects from walls, floors and ceilings. When many reflections overlap, sound can linger after its source stops. This is called reverberation.

Clap softly once in a quiet indoor space. Try a second space. Where is the tail easier to hear?

A ringing bell can keep vibrating, too. To compare two rooms, use the same brief clap in both.

Design a facade that guides the eye

Use shapes and repetition to draw attention to an entrance. Change one opening and see where your eye lands first.

Pick a piece and tap a slot to place it. Put a door in the center and make the three slots on either side mirror each other. You may leave spaces blank. Use at least one pair of openings.

Break the mirror pattern with one change. Try moving a window to a different slot or replacing a round window with a tall one. What draws your eye first? Do the two sides still feel balanced?

Teaching notes and sources

Ask students to support a reaction with a visible or audible clue. For example, “I saw the door first because pairs of windows lead to it.” Different students can respond differently to the same detail.

Worked example: enlarging a sprite

Given: The sprite has 16 columns, and each cell is drawn 8 pixels wide in the preview image. Find its width. Multiply 16 columns by 8 pixels per column to get 128 pixels. The height is the same.

In-class check

Turn an impossible triangle

From the aligned view, the centers of the two end cubes land on the same spot on screen. The ends look joined. Turn the 3D model to see where they come apart.

0°

In the projected view, let h be the horizontal distance and v the vertical distance. The Pythagorean theorem gives the straight-line distance d = √(h² + v²).

Worked example: measuring the projection

Suppose the horizontal and vertical distances are 3 and 4 units. Then d = √(3² + 4²) = 5 units. Move the slider and find the view where the distance reaches zero.

In-class check

Make a tiny chiptune

Pick a note, then tap a step to place it. Leave a rest for a little breathing room. Play the same notes with a square wave and a triangle wave. How does the sound quality, or timbre, change?

Each box is one beat. Seconds per beat = 60 ÷ beats per minute.

The ideal wave is shown over 10 milliseconds. One cycle takes T seconds, where T = 1 / f and f is the frequency in hertz.

Worked example: timing one cycle

Given: The standard tuning note A4 has a frequency of 440 hertz, or 440 cycles per second. Find the time for one cycle. Divide 1 second by 440 to get about 0.00227 seconds, or 2.27 milliseconds.

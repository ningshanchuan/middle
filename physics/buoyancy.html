Archimedes’ principle: what changes buoyancy?

Fᵦ = W₍disp₎ = ρ₍fluid₎ gV₍disp₎

Buoyancy equals the weight of the liquid or gas pushed aside. In the same uniform liquid, displacing more liquid gives more buoyancy. For the same displaced volume, a denser liquid gives more buoyancy.

Once the block is fully submerged, lowering it farther still increases the pressure. The displaced volume stays fixed, so buoyancy stays the same in a uniform liquid.

Challenge: Find the plateau in fresh water, then try 1250 kg/m³. Why is the plateau 25% higher, while the bend stays at 8 cm?

Pressure explanation: when the block is fully submerged, the depth difference between its top and bottom is its height H. Lower it and both pressures increase equally, leaving a pressure difference of ρgH. The horizontal side forces cancel.

Calculations and chapter quiz

Worked example · Find a block’s density

Given
A spring scale reads 6.0 N with the block hanging still in air and 2.0 N with the block held still, fully submerged in fresh water.
Find
Buoyant force, block volume and density.
Solve
Scale difference = (6.0 − 2.0) N = 4.0 N ρg = (1000 kg/m³)(10 N/kg) = 10⁴ N/m³ Volume = 4.0 N ÷ 10⁴ N/m³ = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³ Mass = 6.0 N ÷ 10 N/kg = 0.60 kg ρ = m/V = 0.60 kg ÷ (4.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³) = 1500 kg/m³
Answer
The buoyant force is 4.0 N. The block’s volume is 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³, and its density is 1500 kg/m³.
Full submersion makes the displaced volume equal to the block’s volume. Set the lab to fresh water and 8 cm to check the readings.

Worked example · How much wood is above water?

Given
A 100 cm³ block of wood with an average density of 600 kg/m³ floats in fresh water.
Find
Volume above the surface.
Solve
While floating, buoyancy equals weight. Expand both sides and cancel g.
ρ₍water₎ gV₍disp₎ = ρ₍wood₎ gV Displaced volume = (600 kg/m³ ÷ 1000 kg/m³) × 100 cm³ = 60 cm³
Answer
Volume above water = (100 − 60) cm³ = 40 cm³
Release the wood block in fresh water in the sandbox to see the portion above the surface.

Multi-step practice

Use g = 10 N/kg and fresh-water density 1000 kg/m³ on this page. 1 mL = 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³. Use 9.8 N/kg when a problem specifies it.

What does the scale read in oil? (Show steps)

The scale reads 2.0 N when a 6.0 N block is held still, fully submerged in fresh water. Hold it the same way in oil of density 800 kg/m³. Find the buoyant force and the new scale reading.

  1. Find the force in fresh water. (6.0 − 2.0) N = 4.0 N
  2. At the same displaced volume, the force scales with liquid density. (800 kg/m³ ÷ 1000 kg/m³) × 4.0 N = 3.2 N
  3. Subtract buoyancy from weight. (6.0 − 3.2) N = 2.8 N
How much extra water does the cargo ship displace? (Show steps)

A 600 t ship takes on 200 t of cargo and stays afloat in fresh water. Find the new displaced mass, the new displaced volume, and the increase in displaced volume.

  1. At equilibrium, displaced mass equals the ship plus cargo mass. (600 + 200) t = 800 t = 800,000 kg
  2. Divide by water density. 800,000 kg ÷ 1000 kg/m³ = 800 m³
  3. The extra displaced mass is the 200 t of added cargo. 200,000 kg ÷ 1000 kg/m³ = 200 m³
What does the egg do in salt water? (Show steps)

Release a 106 g model egg of volume 100 cm³ from rest while it is fully submerged in brine of density 1120 kg/m³. Predict its initial motion and find its final displaced volume.

  1. Find weight and the fully submerged buoyant force. Weight = 0.106 kg × 10 N/kg = 1.06 N Buoyant force = 1120 kg/m³ × 10 N/kg × 10⁻⁴ m³ = 1.12 N
  2. 1.12 N exceeds 1.06 N, so the egg rises and then floats at the surface.
  3. While floating, the displaced liquid has the same mass as the egg. Find its volume in m³, then convert to cm³. 0.106 kg ÷ 1120 kg/m³ ≈ 9.46 × 10⁻⁵ m³ 9.46 × 10⁻⁵ m³ = 94.6 cm³

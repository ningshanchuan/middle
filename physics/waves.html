Waves: Energy on the Move

When a stadium crowd does “the wave”, the wave races around the stands. But does anyone leave their seat? A wave is a vibration that carries energy from place to place — without carrying the material along with it. Shake a 3D rope, push a slinky and make ripples. Change the amplitude, wavelength and frequency and test speed = frequency × wavelength for yourself.

Four things waves do

🪞

Reflection

A wave bounces off a surface, like an echo or your image in a mirror. Angle in = angle out.

🥢

Refraction

A wave bends when it enters a new medium and changes speed — that is why a straw looks “broken” in water.

🚪

Diffraction

A wave spreads out around an obstacle or through a gap. It is strongest when the gap is about one wavelength wide — that is why you can hear around a doorway.

🎧

Interference

Two waves overlap and add: in step they build up (constructive), half a wave out of step they cancel (destructive). Noise-cancelling headphones use this.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P4: obtain, evaluate and communicate information to support the claim that electromagnetic (light) waves behave differently than mechanical (sound) waves. This page covers similarities and differences between mechanical and electromagnetic waves (S8P4a); reflection, refraction, diffraction and interference; and the mathematical relationship among amplitude, wavelength, frequency and speed (v = f × λ).

Classroom ideas: (1) With “Same medium” ticked, have students raise the frequency and predict what happens to the wavelength (it shrinks; speed stays the same). (2) Untick it and discuss: in real life, what sets a wave’s speed? (The medium.) (3) Follow the red bead to correct the idea that water flows along with a wave. (4) In the ripple tank, click bright and calm bands and compare path differences: whole wavelengths → constructive; odd half-wavelengths → destructive. Frequencies in 3D are slowed down so they can be seen.

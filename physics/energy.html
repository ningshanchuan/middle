Energy formulas and graphs

Kinetic energy depends on mass and speed; gravitational potential energy depends on mass and height. Move the sliders and watch the dots: KE vs. speed is a curve, PE vs. height is a straight line.

Forms of energy and transformation chains

Energy comes in many forms. It is never created or destroyed — it only changes from one form to another. Click an example to see its transformation chain.

🏔️

Gravitational PE

Stored because of an object’s height.

🏹

Elastic PE

Stored in stretched or squashed objects: springs, rubber bands, bows.

🔋

Chemical energy

Stored in chemical bonds: food, fuel, batteries.

🏃

Kinetic energy

The energy of anything that moves.

🔥

Thermal energy

Energy of moving particles inside matter; friction produces it.

⚡

Electrical energy

Carried by moving electric charges (current).

💡

Light (radiant) energy

Travels as electromagnetic waves, like sunlight.

🔊

Sound energy

Carried by vibrations travelling through matter.

⚠️ Common mix-up:“Used up” energy has not disappeared. When a flashlight battery is “dead”, its chemical energy has become light and thermal energy that spread into the surroundings, where it is hard to collect and use again.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P2: the law of conservation of energy — energy transforms from one form to another within a system. S8P2a: graph how kinetic energy depends on mass and speed and how potential energy depends on mass and height (see the two graphs in “Energy math”). S8P2b: investigate KE ↔ PE transformations in a system (roller coaster, pendulum, rubber band). S8P2c: argue for the type of energy transformation (see the transformation chains).

Classroom ideas: (1) With friction off, ask students to predict what happens if hill 2 is taller than the start (the cart cannot get over and rolls back). (2) Change the mass: the speed at the bottom stays the same, but every energy value scales with mass. (3) Turn friction on and ask “Where did the mechanical energy go?” — it became thermal energy; the total is unchanged. The model ignores air resistance and wheel rotation; g = 9.8 N/kg.

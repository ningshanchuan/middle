Wave speed in vacuum

c = λf

All electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light in vacuum, about 300 million meters per second. Wavelength is the distance between adjacent crests, measured in meters. Frequency is the number of cycles per second, measured in hertz.

At fixed wave speed, doubling the frequency halves the wavelength.

Phones, base stations and satellites

Mobile networks

A phone encodes sound as digital data and exchanges it with a nearby base station by radio. The base station connects to a wider network, often by fiber. A cell is an area served by a base station. As you move, the network can hand the connection over to another cell.

Satellite communication

A communications satellite relays signals. A geostationary satellite follows a circular orbit about 35,786 km above the equator, matching Earth’s spin in direction and period. It appears fixed from the ground. Three spaced 120° apart can cover most of Earth, with gaps near the poles.

In-class check · Communication (3 questions)

Where does the electricity come from?

Choose an energy source, follow its route to electricity, and compare what each source needs.

Two ways to classify energy sources

Primary energy comes directly from nature. Examples include coal, sunlight, wind, moving water and the energy stored in uranium nuclei. Grid electricity and refined gasoline are examples of secondary energy.

Sunlight, wind and water power are renewable because nature replenishes them. Coal, oil, natural gas and uranium are nonrenewable. Nature would take far longer to replace these resources than we take to use them.

Ask two questions. Did it come directly from nature? Can nature replenish the source on a human timescale?
Fission, fusion and the “artificial sun”

In a nuclear power plant, a heavy nucleus can split and release energy and neutrons. Some of those neutrons cause further fissions in a chain reaction. The plant controls the reaction and uses its heat to make steam that spins a turbine connected to a generator.

Control rods absorb neutrons to regulate the chain reaction. Cooling systems carry heat away. Containment helps prevent radioactive releases. Even after shutdown, radioactive material in the fuel continues to give off heat as it decays, so cooling is still needed. Used fuel needs careful management.

Fusion joins light nuclei into heavier ones. It powers the Sun. China’s EAST experiment, nicknamed an “artificial sun,” uses magnetic fields to confine plasma, a hot gas containing charged particles. The experiment helps researchers learn how future fusion power plants might work.

Efficiency and the direction of energy transfer

Give a model lamp 100 J of electrical energy. Move the slider and see how much leaves as visible light and how much warms the surroundings.

Electrical energy → visible light + heat to the surroundings

Visible light Heat transferred
The numbers are model settings. For a lamp running steadily, we track energy as it leaves the lamp. Some leaves as visible light, and the rest warms the surroundings. Visible light can also warm objects that absorb it later.

Useful energy depends on the job

Efficiency is useful energy output divided by energy input, usually expressed as a percentage. For lighting, visible light is useful. Some energy spreads into the surroundings and increases their internal energy. Counting that energy, the total is conserved.

Energy is conserved even when useful output decreases. Energy spread through the surroundings is usually hard to collect and reuse for the original job.
Worked example · Find the model lamp’s efficiency
  1. Given — The lamp takes in 100 J of electrical energy and gives out 25 J of visible light. Set the slider back to 25% to check your answer.
  2. Find — Its efficiency for lighting.
  3. Solve — η = (Eᵤ / Eᵢ) × 100% = (25 J / 100 J) × 100% = 25%
  4. Answer — The efficiency is 25%. The other 75 J warms the surroundings.
Heat flow and choices that save energy

Which cup gives up heat?

A metal rod connects two cups of water at different temperatures. Swap the cups and watch which way heat flows along the rod at first.

When objects at different temperatures are in contact, heat flows spontaneously from the warmer object to the cooler object. A refrigerator uses electrical energy to move heat from its cold interior into the warmer room.

For the same lighting, choose a lamp that uses less electricity and switch off lights you do not need. For a power supply, consider emissions, local sun and wind, river ecosystems and how reliably the supply meets demand.

For a communications station far from the grid, first estimate how much electricity it needs by day and by night. In a sunny location, solar panels could generate electricity during the day and a battery could store energy for night use.

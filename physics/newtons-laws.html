Newton’s Laws of Motion

Why do you lurch forward when a bus brakes? And how does a rocket push itself along in empty space? Three laws explain every motion from skateboards to rockets. Lab: force, mass and acceleration

Choose a push force and a cart mass, then run a trial. The lab times how long the cart takes to travel 2 m, calculates its acceleration and plots the point. Collect several trials and find the pattern!

💡 Memory hint“More force, more acceleration. More mass, less acceleration.” a = F ÷ m.

Mass is not weight

Mass is the amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms (kg). It is the same everywhere. Weight is the pull of gravity on that mass, measured in newtons (N): W = m × g.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P3b: use Newton’s laws to explain how balanced and unbalanced forces affect an object’s motion; also S8P3 (force, mass and motion) and S8P5 (gravity). Have students predict the slow-pull vs. fast-yank results before running them. Follow-up: “Would heavier dishes change the result?” (No: friction and inertia both scale with mass, so the dish’s acceleration μg does not depend on mass.)

Physics note: all three setups are integrated with a fixed time step (1/500 s). Tablecloth: μs = 0.5, μk = 0.3 (dish–cloth), μ = 0.4 (dish–table). The cart track is frictionless and the push lasts 0.5 s. The spring pushes each cart with 40 N for 0.15 s (impulse 6 N·s). Balloon rocket: 0.12 N thrust for 1.5 s, with air drag.

How to trace the three principal rays

A convex lens is thicker in the middle and brings parallel rays together. A concave lens is thinner in the middle and spreads parallel rays apart. The principal axis runs through the optical center. A ray parallel to the axis leaves a convex lens through the far focus F. After a concave lens, it appears to come from the near focus F. Focal length is the center-to-focus distance.

Two boundaries to remember. One focal length separates real and virtual images. Two focal lengths separate enlarged and reduced real images. When a lens is forming a real image, moving the object closer to F makes the image farther away and larger.

Common mix-up. A convex lens can form a virtual image. A screen at that position cannot catch a sharp image because only the backward extensions meet there.

Example 1 · Where should the screen go?

Given a convex lens with a focal length of 12 cm and a 4 cm tall object 18 cm from the lens.

Find the image distance, height, and type.

Solution
  1. The object lies between F and 2F. Expect a real, inverted, enlarged image. 12 cm < 18 cm < 24 cm
  2. Use the thin-lens equation to find the screen distance. 1/v = 1/f − 1/u 1/(12 cm) − 1/(18 cm) = 1/(36 cm) → v = 36 cm
  3. Use magnification to find the image height. Its negative sign means the image is inverted. hᵢ = −(36 cm / 18 cm)(4 cm) = −8 cm

The real, inverted, enlarged image is 8 cm tall and forms 36 cm past the lens.

Example 2 · Where is the magnified image?

Given a magnifier with a focal length of 15 cm and a mark 10 cm from the lens.

Find the image distance and magnification.

Solution
  1. The object is inside the focal length. Expect a virtual, upright, enlarged image. 10 cm < 15 cm
  2. Substitute into the thin-lens equation. 1/v = 1/(15 cm) − 1/(10 cm) = −1/(30 cm) → v = −30 cm
  3. Find the magnification. Its positive sign means the image is upright. m = −v/u = −(−30 cm)/(10 cm) = +3

The virtual image is 30 cm from the lens on the object side. It is upright and three times as tall as the object.

How the eye changes focus

When the eye is in focus, the cornea and crystalline lens bend light to form a real, inverted image on the retina. For near objects, the healthy eye’s lens becomes rounder and bends light more strongly. This is accommodation. With nearsightedness (myopia), distant light focuses in front of the retina. A diverging lens spreads the rays first. With farsightedness (hyperopia), light from a nearby object would focus behind the retina if the eye cannot adjust enough. A converging lens helps bring the rays together sooner.

Challenge problems

1. A convex lens with a focal length of 10 cm forms an image on a screen three times the object’s height. Find u and v.

Show steps for problem 1

The real image is inverted, so m = −3 and v = 3u. Substitute to find an object distance of about 13.33 cm and an image distance of 40 cm.

1/(10 cm) = 1/u + 1/(3u) = 4/(3u) u = (40/3) cm, v = 40 cm

2. An object is 24 cm from a concave lens with |f| = 12 cm. Where is the image? How tall is it compared with the object?

Show steps for problem 2

A diverging lens has a negative f. The image is 8 cm from the lens on the object side, upright and one third as tall.

1/v = 1/(−12 cm) − 1/(24 cm) = −1/(8 cm) m = −(−8 cm)/(24 cm) = 1/3

🎯 Challenge

Three shapes, three motions

On a distance–time graph, a steeper line means faster motion. On a speed–time graph, a steeper line means the speed is changing faster (bigger acceleration).

⚠️ Common mix-up:A flat line on a distance–time graph means the object is STOPPED. A flat line on a speed–time graph means it is moving at a STEADY speed. Always read the y-axis first!

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P3a: analyze and interpret data to identify patterns in the relationships between speed and distance, and velocity and acceleration (including motion graphs). Try: ask students to predict whether Car B (from rest, a = 2.5 m/s²) catches Car A (steady 10 m/s) and where; then run it (t = 8 s at 80 m). Next, turn on “Allow reversing” and compare distance with displacement. The GSE clarification does not require calculating velocity or acceleration, so treat the calculation questions as enrichment.

Physics note: the race uses a fixed time step (1/200 s) with the constant-acceleration update x = x₀ + vt + ½at², accurate to under 1 mm. The speed–time graph plots signed velocity (negative = backwards).

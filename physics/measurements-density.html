Mass, Density & Measurement

How do you measure the volume of an irregularly shaped stone? Try a balance and a graduated cylinder, then use measurements to identify a material and check whether a metal ball is hollow.

Mass and weight

Mass measures how much matter an object contains. Changing its shape, state, or location keeps its mass the same if no matter is gained or lost. Here weight means the force of gravity on the sample. The sample weighs more on Earth’s surface because g, the force per kilogram, is larger there.

The SI unit of mass is the kilogram (kg). Grams (g) are also common. 1 kg = 1,000 g. Measure weight in newtons (N).

Two-pan balance

Setup, weighing, and common mistakes

Set the balance level and slide the rider (游码, yóumǎ) to zero. With empty pans, move the balancing nuts away from the side the pointer leans toward. Put the object on the left. Use tweezers to try weights on the right, largest first, then move the rider. Once balanced, add the weights’ masses and the rider reading.

During weighing, adjust only the weights and rider. Read the rider’s left edge. If you zero the balance with the rider at 1.2 g and later add the full rider reading, the result will be 1.2 g too high. Use a container for liquids and stay within the balance’s capacity.

Graduated cylinder and parallax

Set the cylinder on a flat surface. Read water at the bottom of its curved surface (the meniscus), with your eye at the same height. Find the value of one division first. Each small mark is 2 mL here and the parallax effect is exaggerated.

For water, line up your eye with the lowest point. 1 mL = 1 cm³.

Ounces and fluid ounces on U.S. packages

On U.S. food packages, an ounce (oz) measures mass. One ounce is about 28.35 g. On drink bottles, a U.S. fluid ounce (fl oz) measures volume. One U.S. fluid ounce is about 29.57 mL.

Slope = density

Density (密度, mìdù) is mass per unit volume. With volume on the horizontal axis and mass on the vertical axis, the slope is density. For a uniform substance at the same temperature and pressure, a larger sample has proportionally more mass and the same density.

At the same volume, look for the larger mass. The denser material plots higher.

How do the density units convert?

The SI unit is kilograms per cubic meter. Grams per cubic centimeter are useful in the lab. Cube the length conversion to get the volume conversion.

1 cm³ = (10⁻² m)³ = 10⁻⁶ m³ 1 g/cm³ = 10⁻³ kg ÷ 10⁻⁶ m³ 1 g/cm³ = 1,000 kg/m³

Liquid density is often given in g/mL. Its numerical value is the same in g/cm³ because 1 mL = 1 cm³.

Error analysis · Find which measurement changed
  • Weighing the stone after immersion can leave extra water on the stone and make the mass too high. Air bubbles on a submerged stone make its displaced volume too high and the density too low.
  • A freely floating block displaces only the volume of its submerged part. With a sinker, compare the fully submerged pair with the sinker alone. You can take the readings in either order if the amount of water stays the same.
  • Weigh the beaker with liquid before and after pouring some into the cylinder. If none spills, the difference is the mass that entered the cylinder. Subtracting the mass of the dry beaker instead includes leftover liquid, so the density comes out too high.
  • If you read the cylinder first and pour into a weighed empty cup, liquid left in the cylinder makes the transferred mass too low for the original volume, so the calculated density is too low.

Worked example · Identify a metal
Given
m = 54.0 g, V = 20.0 cm³
Find
Find the density and the closest metal in the table.
Solve
ρ = m/V = 54.0 g ÷ 20.0 cm³ρ = 2.70 g/cm³ = 2,700 kg/m³
Answer
The density is 2.70 g/cm³. Aluminum is the closest match in the table.
Practice · Find the volume of the cavity

A sealed aluminum ball has an outer volume of 100 cm³ and a mass of 216 g. Ignoring the mass of the air inside, how much space is hollow?

Show steps
Given
Outer volume 100 cm³, mass 216 g, aluminum density 2.70 g/cm³.
Find
Volume of the cavity.
Solve
Vₐₗ = 216 g ÷ 2.70 g/cm³ = 80 cm³ 100 cm³ − 80 cm³ = 20 cm³
Answer
The cavity occupies 20 cm³.

Average density of a mixture

ρ̄ = (m₁ + m₂) ÷ V

The model uses 1.00 g/mL for water and 0.90 g/mL for oil. The liquids stay in layers, so their volumes add. At equal volumes, the average density is halfway between their densities. If volume changes on mixing, use the measured final volume.

Challenge · Make a sample of 0.96 g/mL

Try 60 mL of water and 40 mL of oil. The total mass is 60 g + 36 g = 96 g. Divide by 100 mL to get 0.96 g/mL.

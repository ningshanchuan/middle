⚖️ Ohm’s law: I = V ÷ R

Voltage (V) is the push on the charges, resistance (R) is how much the path resists, and current (I) is the rate of flow.

2.00 A
💡 Water-pipe modelVoltage is like the pump’s pressure, current is like how much water flows, and resistance is like a narrow section of pipe. More pressure → more flow; narrower pipe → less flow.
🎈

Static electricity

Rubbing a balloon moves electrons from your hair to the balloon. The charge builds up and stays put. Opposite charges attract; like charges repel. On an insulator, charge stays where it was rubbed; on a conductor, it spreads over the whole surface.

🔌

Current electricity

In a closed circuit, a battery pushes charges to flow steadily through wires. Current turns electrical energy into light, heat and motion, which is how our devices work. Lightning is a giant static discharge.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Bulbs do not “use up” current. The current flowing out of a bulb equals the current flowing in. What gets used is energy, which turns into light and heat.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P5 (electricity, magnetism and gravity as major forces in nature), especially S8P5b (investigate the distribution of charge in conductors and insulators) and S8P5c (factors such as the number of dry cells that affect electric and magnetic forces).

Have students predict, then test in the 3D circuit: (1) how brightness changes when bulbs are added in series; (2) what happens to the others when one parallel bulb is unscrewed; (3) how the current changes with more cells. Use ammeter readings as evidence. The model treats the battery as ideal (no internal resistance) and each filament as a constant 3 Ω.

Misconceptions to probe: current is “used up” by bulbs; bulbs nearer the battery are brighter; batteries store electrons; electrons race from the battery to the bulb.

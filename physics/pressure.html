Pressure: Solids, Liquids & Air

Which sinks farther into sand, a brick lying flat or the same brick standing on end? Turn it over, then use pressure to explain water jets, canal locks, and boiling water on a mountain.

Pressure from a solid

One brick, three contact areas

Average pressure (压强, yāqiáng) is perpendicular force F divided by contact area S. The force acts at right angles to the surface. Keep the force fixed and compare the marks made by the three faces.

Average pressure
24 N
35°

Drag the brick sideways to change your view. The dents show the trend. Each turn starts on a fresh patch.

How can you change pressure?

For the same force, snowshoes spread the load over a larger area and lower the pressure on snow. A sharp knife concentrates force over a smaller area and raises the pressure on the material.

To increase pressure, push harder or use a smaller contact area. To decrease pressure, push more lightly or use a larger area. Change one variable at a time. 1 Pa = 1 N/m².

Worked example · A brick on the floor

Given
F = 24 N, S = 240 cm²
Find
Average pressure p on the floor.
Solve
S = 240 × 10⁻⁴ m² = 0.024 m² p = F/S = 24 N ÷ 0.024 m²
Answer
p = 1000 N/m² = 1000 Pa

Pressure in a liquid

Lower the probe

A liquid at rest exerts pressure. In the same liquid, deeper means higher pressure, and points at the same depth have equal pressure. At any one point, pressure is the same in every direction. Measure depth h vertically down from the surface.

1.2 m
Pressure due to the liquid (above the pressure at the surface)

ρ is liquid density and g is 9.8 N/kg. The tank keeps a steady liquid level. In the ideal model, deeper holes give faster jets. Changing the density changes pressure inside the tank at a fixed depth, while the ideal exit speed at each hole stays the same.

The equation below uses the hole depth h to find the exit speed. A faster jet can land closer because it has less time to fall from a lower hole.

v = √(2gh)

U-tube manometer

Water pushes on the probe’s flexible membrane and raises the air pressure in the hose. The water falls on the connected side and rises on the open side. Read the vertical difference ΔH. In this ideal model, both liquids are water, so ΔH equals the probe depth.

Connected vessels (连通器, liántōngqì)

Left and right levels above the same base

These vessels are open at the top and connected at the bottom. With the same liquid and equal air pressure above both surfaces, the liquid settles at the same height in both arms, even when the widths differ.

Quick check · Liquids

Atmospheric pressure

Torricelli’s experiment

Air has weight and exerts pressure. Torricelli inverted a mercury-filled tube with a sealed end into a mercury reservoir. The top of the tube is nearly a vacuum, and air pressure supports the mercury column above the reservoir surface.

0 km
Air pressure and mercury column height

At higher elevation there is less air above you, so pressure generally falls. At sea level the model uses 1 standard atmosphere, 101,325 Pa (about 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa). That supports a mercury column about 760 mm (29.92 in) high. The model holds mercury density and g fixed. Weather also affects actual pressure.

Magdeburg hemispheres

After most of the air is pumped out, the higher outside pressure holds the halves together. Let air back in to equalize the pressure and the halves are easy to pull apart.

Air pressure and boiling point

0 km
Approximate boiling point of pure water

Water boils when its vapor pressure matches the surrounding pressure. Lower air pressure lowers the boiling point. A pressure cooker raises the pressure and the boiling point.

Compare wide and narrow flow

Follow the same path through a steady flow at the same height. Where the air moves faster, its static pressure is lower, as long as friction and density changes are small. A pressure gauge connected to the pipe wall reads the static pressure there.

Static pressure drop into the narrow section

Air density is set to 1.2 kg/m³. The subscripts 1 and 2 mark the wide and narrow sections. Challenge: set the area ratio to 50%. Double the wide-section speed from 5 m/s to 10 m/s. What happens to the pressure drop?

On a typical lifting wing, the air often moves faster above the wing, where the pressure is lower. The pressure difference produces lift, and the wing turns the airflow downward overall. Air passing above and below can reach the back edge at different times.

Practice and review

Practice problems

A ski and a boot each press on the snow with 300 N of force. The ski covers 0.15 m² and the boot sole covers 0.025 m². Find the average pressure each exerts on the snow.

Pressure on snow from the ski

p₁ = 300 N ÷ 0.15 m² = 2000 Pa

Pressure on snow from the boot

p₂ = 300 N ÷ 0.025 m² = 12,000 Pa

At the same force the boot sole has one-sixth of the area, so its pressure is six times as large.

In oil of density 850 kg/m³, the pressure due to the oil is 4.165 kPa. Use g = 9.8 N/kg. How deep is that point?

4.165 kPa = 4165 Pa h = p/(ρg) = 4165 Pa ÷ [(850 kg/m³)(9.8 N/kg)] h = 0.50 m

The same mercury barometer reads 760 mm at the coast and 600 mm on a mountain. Approximately what is the mountain pressure in kPa?

With mercury density and g treated as constant, air pressure is proportional to the column height. A 760 mm column corresponds to about 101.325 kPa.

p = 101.325 kPa × 600 mm ÷ 760 mm ≈ 80.0 kPa

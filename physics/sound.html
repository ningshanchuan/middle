🚑 The Doppler effect

An ambulance siren sounds higher as it comes toward you and lower as it drives away. When the source moves toward you the waves are squeezed together (shorter wavelength, higher frequency); when it moves away they are stretched out (lower frequency).

💡 Memory hint“Coming = higher, going = lower.” Humans hear about 20–20,000 Hz. Below 20 Hz is infrasound; above 20,000 Hz is ultrasound.

Echo distance calculator

An echo is a sound wave reflected back from a surface. The sound travels there AND back, so: distance = speed × time ÷ 2. Bats, dolphins and ship sonar all measure distance this way.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P4: sound is a mechanical wave that needs a medium and behaves differently from electromagnetic waves such as light. This page covers how different media affect wave speed (solids > liquids > gases; no sound in a vacuum), the link between frequency and pitch and between amplitude and loudness, and how the human ear perceives sound (outer ear, ear canal, eardrum, ossicles, cochlea, auditory nerve).

Classroom ideas: (1) Remind students to start with a low volume; audio only starts after a button click. (2) Keep the frequency fixed and switch media to see how the wavelength changes (λ = v ÷ f). (3) Use the echo calculator for a sonar problem: a ship hears its echo after 2 s — how deep is the sea? (1480 × 2 ÷ 2 = 1480 m.) (4) Discuss hearing protection: long exposure above 85 dB damages the hair cells in the cochlea, and they do not grow back. Vibrations in the 3D animation are slowed down enormously.

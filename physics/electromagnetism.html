The Ørsted experiment

Change the current direction

Memory tip⊙ looks like an arrow tip coming toward you. ⊗ looks like the tail moving away.

In 1820, Hans Christian Ørsted found that a nearby compass needle turned when a wire carried current. The wire's field combines with Earth's field. Switch the current off and the needle returns to magnetic north. Reverse the current and the wire's field reverses.

Solenoids and electromagnets

Turn the right hand and find N

Drag the model sideways or use the viewing angle slider. Hide the hand to see the field, then show it and compare the fingers with the thumb.

A solenoid is a coil of wire. Its field looks like a bar magnet's when current flows. Use the right-hand grip rule (安培定则, ānpéi dìngzé). Curl your right fingers with the current around the turns. Your thumb points toward the N pole. Field lines go from N to S outside the coil.

Electromagnetic relay

In an electromagnetic relay (电磁继电器, diàncí jìdiànqì), the energized coil pulls a moving iron arm called the armature. The contacts close, and the load's own source powers the lamp. A spring opens the contacts again when the coil is off. Some alarms and automatic doors use relays to switch a siren or a door motor.

The DC motor

Catch the current reversal

26°

Play the animation or use the slider to see when the coil current reverses.

The battery supplies direct current (DC). The magnetic field pushes the two current-carrying sides of the coil in opposite directions, turning it. Every half-turn, the split-ring commutator reverses the coil current to keep the turning effect in the same direction. Reverse just the battery or just the field to reverse the spin. Reverse both to keep the original spin.

Common mix-upThe commutator reverses the rotor coil's current. The source still supplies DC. A moving rotor coasts across the gap in this model. A single-coil motor resting at that gap cannot start by itself.

Michael Faraday discovered electromagnetic induction in 1831. Moving the magnet here changes the magnetic flux, a measure of how much magnetic field passes through the coil. That produces a voltage. A closed circuit allows an induced current. When the magnet stops and the flux stays steady, the needle returns to zero.

Common mix-upIn the textbook setup, sliding one side of a closed loop across field lines makes the galvanometer deflect. To tell whether a whole loop has an induced emf, look for a change in magnetic flux. An open circuit can have an induced voltage across the gap while its galvanometer reads zero.

Spin a coil to generate AC

0°

Press play for steady rotation. Use the slider to preview the voltage at any angle during that turn. If the coil actually stops, the flux stays steady and the induced voltage is zero.

A turning coil changes the magnetic flux through it. Each slip ring stays connected to one end of the coil. Many large generators spin the magnet and keep the output coils fixed. Flowing water, wind or steam can turn a turbine. Its shaft supplies the mechanical energy that the generator converts into electrical energy.

Memory tipSupply electricity to a motor to get motion (electrical → mechanical). Supply motion to a generator to get electricity (mechanical → electrical). Real devices also transfer some energy to their surroundings, including as heat.

US-style · Voltage across a coil

A 100-turn coil in the model experiences a flux change of 0.0020 Wb (webers) per turn in 2.0 s. What is the magnitude of its average induced voltage?

Find the average rate of flux change per turn, then multiply by the number of turns. One weber per second equals one volt.

0.0020 Wb / 2.0 s = 0.0010 Wb/s
100 × 0.0010 Wb/s = 0.10 V

Chinese-style · Current in the loop

Given (已知) · In one full insertion, the magnitude of the average induced voltage is 0.10 V. The closed circuit has 10 Ω total resistance.

Find (求) · The magnitude of the average induced current in that interval.

Solution (解) · The current keeps the same direction during this insertion. With constant resistance, apply Ohm's law to the averages.

0.10 V / 10 Ω = 0.010 A = 10 mA

Answer (答) · The average current has a magnitude of 10 mA.

The primary has 100 turns and a 6.0 V AC input (RMS).

Alternating current in the primary changes the flux in the core and induces a voltage in the secondary. With the same input voltage and primary turns, more secondary turns raise the output voltage in this ideal model. Once a DC input is steady, the flux stays steady and the secondary has no sustained induced voltage.

Simulation: compasses around a current-carrying wire

Simulation: spin a magnet to make electricity

Electricity can make magnetism, and magnetism can make electricity! Spinning a magnet inside a coil produces a voltage that lights a bulb. This is how a generator works.

⚠️ Common mix-up:A magnet sitting still inside a coil makes no electricity. Current is produced only while the magnetic field through the coil is changing (the magnet or coil is moving).
🌍

Earth is a giant magnet

Moving liquid iron in Earth’s outer core makes its magnetic field. A compass’s N end points toward the geographic North Pole because a magnetic SOUTH pole sits near there. The field also shields us from the solar wind and causes auroras near the poles.

⚙️

Electric motors

A motor changes electrical energy into motion: current turns a coil into an electromagnet that is pushed and pulled by permanent magnets, so it spins. Fans, electric cars and washing machines all use motors.

💡

Generators

A generator changes motion into electrical energy, the reverse of a motor. Hydroelectric dams, wind turbines and power plants all use water, wind or steam to spin magnets and coils past each other.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P5a (argue from evidence that fields, such as magnetic fields, exist between objects exerting forces on each other even when they are not in contact) and S8P5c (investigate the factors that affect the strength of an electromagnet: number of wire turns, number of dry cells / current, and the iron core).

Have students change one variable at a time (a fair test), record the number of clips lifted and graph the results. In the model, electromagnet strength is computed as turns × current × core factor; the clip count is qualitative. Field lines are numerically traced from a two-pole (magnetic charge) model.

Misconceptions to probe: all metals are magnetic; field lines are real objects; the geographic North Pole is a magnetic north pole (it is actually a magnetic south pole); cutting a magnet separates N from S.

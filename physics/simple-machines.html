Simple Machines

How could one person lift something as heavy as a piano, using only a long bar or a few pulleys? Simple machines can’t give you free work, but they let you use a smaller force to do the same work, by moving that force over a longer distance. Slide a lever’s fulcrum and add pulleys to see where mechanical advantage comes from.

The six simple machines

⚠️ Common mix-up:Machines do NOT reduce the work you do! If a machine cuts your force to ¼, you must move 4 times as far. Work = force × distance stays the same (ideally); in real life friction makes it a little more.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P3c: construct an argument from evidence about how simple machines (lever, pulley, wedge, inclined plane, wheel and axle, screw) affect work. Have students find the smallest effort that lifts the load, record the effort arm and load arm, and discover effort × effort arm = load × load arm. Compare the three lever classes and discuss why third-class levers (tweezers, fishing rods) are useful even though they need MORE force (they gain distance and speed).

Physics note: the lever is ideal (plank weight ignored for the force calculation) and its motion is integrated from torques and moment of inertia; the pulley system’s ideal MA = number of supporting strands n, with a little damping. Ramp: F = W·h/L without friction, F = W(sin θ + μ cos θ) with friction. g = 9.8 N/kg.

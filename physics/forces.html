Forces, Friction & Gravity

Gravity is pulling on a parked car right now. So why doesn’t it move? A force is a push or a pull. When the forces on an object are balanced, its motion doesn’t change; when they are unbalanced, it speeds up, slows down or turns. Run a tug-of-war, then put a block on ramps with different surfaces and watch the force arrows change.

Drop race: with and without air

Drop two objects from 20 m at the same moment. With air, air resistance grows as the object speeds up until it equals gravity, and the object falls at a steady “terminal velocity”. Switch the air off (like a vacuum chamber or the Moon) and everything falls together!

⚠️ Common mix-up:Heavy objects don’t fall faster just because they are heavy. In a vacuum, a bowling ball and a feather land together. With air, the difference comes from how big air resistance is compared with the object’s weight.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P3b (use Newton’s laws to explain the effect of balanced and unbalanced forces on motion) and S8P5a (fields such as gravity act between objects that are not touching). Use the tug-of-war to show that “net force zero ≠ no forces”. On the ramp, have students find the angle where the block just starts to slide (critical angle, tan θ = μs) and compare ice, wood and rubber.

Physics note: the tug-of-war system has a total mass of 300 kg, a = (F_right − F_left) ÷ 300. Ramp: g = 9.8 N/kg; at rest, static friction = mg sin θ; sliding, f = μk mg cos θ and a = g(sin θ − μk cos θ). The drop race uses a = g − (k/m)v² with a fixed 1/500 s step.

Follow the electrons

When two materials touch and separate, electrons can transfer between them. Rubbing brings the surfaces into contact repeatedly. If both objects start neutral, the one that loses electrons becomes positive and the one that gains electrons becomes negative.

Memory tip

Start neutral. Gain electrons and become negative. Lose electrons and become positive. The total charge stays the same when the pair exchanges no charge with its surroundings.

The triboelectric series and the names of the charges

A neutral object has equal amounts of positive and negative charge. Static effects can come from a buildup of net charge or from charges separating within an object.

A triboelectric series ranks materials by their tendency to gain or lose charge when paired. The model shows typical results. Real results can depend on factors such as the condition of the surfaces.

Benjamin Franklin used “positive” and “negative” for the two kinds of charge. By today’s convention, an electron is negative. Glass rubbed with silk typically becomes positive.

Worked examples · Find the total charge

Charge is written as Q. Its SI unit is the coulomb (C). A nanocoulomb (nC) is one billionth of a coulomb. A and B are the two objects. Each rub transfers 1 nC of negative charge in this model. The moving symbol represents a group of electrons.

US-style example · Glass and silk

Choose glass and silk, reset, and rub three times. The glass has a charge of +3 nC and the silk has a charge of −3 nC. What is their total charge?

Q(A + B) = Q(A) + Q(B)
(+3 nC) + (−3 nC) = 0 nC

Their total charge is 0 nC, the same as before rubbing.

Chinese-style example · Balloon and hair

Given · A balloon and hair start neutral and exchange no charge with their surroundings. After rubbing, the balloon has a charge of −4 nC.

Find · The charge on the hair.

Solution · The total charge is the same before and after rubbing.

Q(B) = 0 nC − (−4 nC) = +4 nC

Answer · The hair is positive at +4 nC. Choose balloon and hair, reset, and rub four times to check.

In-class check · Rubbing

Attraction and repulsion

Read the arrows

Like charges repel. Opposite charges attract. These forces act across a gap, evidence of an electric field. Near a negative balloon, charges in the paper shift slightly, making the near side a little positive. The closer attraction is stronger than the farther repulsion, so the net force pulls the paper toward the balloon.

Charge separates slightly within the paper (polarization). The whole scrap stays neutral.

Worked example · Double the distance

In Like or Unlike, the objects are modeled as point charges, and their charges stay fixed. Coulomb’s law gives an inverse-square relationship. The graph compares the force to its value at 4 cm. It shows the force ratio up the side and distance in centimeters across the bottom. Move from 4 cm to 8 cm and compare the arrows and graph points.

F(8 cm) / F(4 cm) = (4 cm / 8 cm)² = ¼

At twice the distance, the force is one-quarter as large.

Conductors and insulators

Compare where charge goes

Metals are conductors. Some of their electrons are free to move through the material. In insulators such as plastic, charge moves with difficulty, so excess charge tends to stay near where it was placed.

Silicon is a semiconductor. Its conductivity can be controlled, which makes it useful for making chips.

When you hear thunder, go indoors

Go straight into a sturdy enclosed building. If none is nearby, get into a car with a metal roof and close the windows. Wait at least 30 minutes after the last thunder before going back outside. Indoors, stay away from doors, windows, plumbing, and plugged-in equipment.

Apply what you learned · Reveal the reasoning

A negative rod approaches a neutral electroscope. With the rod still nearby, you ground the electroscope. Then you remove the rod while the ground connection is still in place. Finally, you disconnect ground. What is the final charge?

Show steps

While it is grounded, the negative rod repels some electrons into Earth. When the rod is removed first, electrons can flow back through the ground connection, restoring neutrality. Disconnecting ground then leaves it neutral.

A negative balloon attracts a small scrap of paper. Can you conclude the paper is positive? What observation could tell you that an object is negative?

Show reasoning

Neutral paper can be attracted through polarization. If a known negative object repels the test object and other causes are ruled out, the repulsion is evidence that the test object is negative too.

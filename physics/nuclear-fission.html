Inside the nucleus

A nucleus contains positively charged protons and usually uncharged neutrons. The proton count identifies the element. Add protons and neutrons to get its mass number.

Review atomic structure

Uranium-235 and uranium-238 are isotopes. Both have 92 protons, with 143 and 146 neutrons respectively. A powerful attraction inside the nucleus holds it together despite the protons’ electrical repulsion.

By atom count, natural uranium is about 0.72% U-235 and 99.27% U-238, with a trace of U-234. Uranium for most commercial power reactors is enriched, meaning its U-235 fraction is increased.

Check the mass and proton numbers

U-235 can absorb a slow neutron and form excited U-236. It can change shape and split into two smaller nuclei, releasing energy and usually two or three neutrons. Many pairs of fragments are possible.

Where did the missing mass go?

E = Δm c²

Add up the rest masses before and after fission. The products have slightly less rest mass. This difference, called the mass defect, corresponds to the energy released. The elegant equation uses the speed of light squared as its huge conversion factor.

A U-235 fission releases about 200 MeV on average, or about 3.2 × 10⁻¹¹ J. One split contributes very little energy. The sum for many nuclei can be enormous.

Mass number counts nucleons. The total rest mass can decrease even when that count stays the same.
Worked example · How much energy corresponds to 1 g of mass?

Problem. Imagine converting 1.00 g of rest mass entirely into other forms of energy. Find the energy, then express it as heat from standard coal and as a TNT energy equivalent.

  1. Convert grams to kilograms and use the mass–energy equation. 1.00 g = 1.00 × 10⁻³ kg E ≈ (1.00 × 10⁻³ kg)(3.00 × 10⁸ m/s)² E ≈ 9.00 × 10¹³ J
  2. Use 29.31 GJ per metric tonne of standard coal, or 2.931 × 10⁷ J/kg. Divide by the heat per tonne. 9.00 × 10¹³ J ÷ (2.931 × 10¹⁰ J/t) ≈ 3.07 × 10³ t
  3. For the TNT energy unit, use 4.184 × 10¹² J per kiloton. 9.00 × 10¹³ J ÷ (4.184 × 10¹² J/kt) ≈ 21.5 kt

Answer. About 9.00 × 10¹³ J, equal to the heat from about 3,070 metric tonnes of standard coal or about 21.5 kilotons of TNT as an energy unit. This is a hypothetical full conversion of rest mass.

Worked example · All the nuclei in 1 kg of U-235

Given There are about 6.022 × 10²³ atoms in 235 g of U-235. Start with 1,000 g and use about 3.2 × 10⁻¹¹ J per fission.

Find Total energy E if every nucleus splits, and the amount of standard coal that would give the same heat.

Solve Count the nuclei and multiply by the energy per split.

N ≈ (1,000 g ÷ 235 g/mol) × 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ ≈ 2.56 × 10²⁴ E ≈ (2.56 × 10²⁴)(3.2 × 10⁻¹¹ J) ≈ 8.2 × 10¹³ J 8.2 × 10¹³ J ÷ (2.931 × 10¹⁰ J/t) ≈ 2.8 × 10³ t

Answer About 8.2 × 10¹³ J, equivalent to the heat from about 2,800 metric tonnes of standard coal.

Advanced · Read the binding-energy curve

The points show selected nuclei and the line traces the overall trend.

Binding energy is the energy needed to separate a nucleus into its individual protons and neutrons. Divide it by the number of nucleons to get binding energy per nucleon. The nuclear force holds nucleons together over very short distances.

Iron-56 and nickel-62 sit near the peak. Heavy nuclei can release energy by splitting into more tightly bound, medium-sized nuclei. Some light nuclei release energy by joining.

The average across generations

The vertical axis is logarithmic. Each grid line represents ten times the value of the one below it.

The box shows one fission and possible paths for its neutrons. If a neutron triggers another fission, you’ll see another nucleus split. The graph starts with one effective neutron at generation 0 and shows the average trend. Even at k = 1, a chain that starts with one neutron can end.

With k fixed, each generation’s expected count is the previous one multiplied by k. Below 1 the chain dies out. At 1 it stays steady on average. Above 1 it multiplies from one generation to the next. Each step along the horizontal axis is one generation.

How does a reactor stay steady?

In a typical thermal reactor, a moderator slows neutrons so they are more likely to split U-235. Inserting control rods absorbs more neutrons. Operators and automatic systems adjust the chain reaction. At steady power, k equals 1.

What differs between a bomb and a reactor?

A fission bomb releases energy in an extremely fast, uncontrolled chain reaction. Most power reactors use low-enriched uranium fuel. A commercial power reactor cannot detonate like an atomic bomb, although a reactor accident can still have serious consequences.

What does critical mass mean?

Critical mass means the amount of material that just sustains a chain reaction under given conditions. A small piece has more surface relative to its volume, so a larger fraction of neutrons can escape and too few may remain to sustain the chain.

Nuclear power in China

In 1991, Qinshan in Zhejiang became the first nuclear plant in mainland China to connect to the power grid. Guangdong is home to the Daya Bay plant. The first Chinese-designed Hualong One pressurized water reactor entered commercial service at Fuqing, Fujian, in 2021.

China’s National Energy Administration reports that nuclear power supplied about 4.5% of the country’s electricity generation in 2024.

Two nuclear power plant accidents

In 1986, a power surge during a test destroyed a reactor at Chernobyl, in present-day Ukraine, releasing radioactive material. In 2011, an earthquake-triggered tsunami flooded electrical equipment at Japan’s Fukushima Daiichi plant, disabling cooling and leading to core meltdowns and radioactive releases.

Radioactivity and shielding

Put a shield in the beam

The number of tracks shows which beam is weaker. It gives no measured percentage. Shielding depends on the material, its thickness, and the radiation’s energy and type.

☢Recognize the radiation warning sign

Unstable nuclei can decay on their own and emit radiation. An alpha particle is a helium-4 nucleus. A beta-minus particle is a fast electron. Gamma rays are high-energy electromagnetic waves.

The trefoil sign warns of ionizing radiation or radioactive material. Three basics of protection are less time near the source, more distance from it, and suitable shielding.

Paper stops alpha particles. Alpha-emitting material can still harm the body if inhaled or swallowed.

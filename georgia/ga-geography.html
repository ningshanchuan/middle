Why is the north cool and the south hot?

Georgia has a humid subtropical climate: hot, sticky summers and mild winters. But the higher and farther north you go, the cooler it gets — a January day averages about 37°F (3°C) in the mountains and about 50°F (10°C) in Savannah. The south also has a longer growing season, which is why the Coastal Plain became the big farming region.

Where does the most rain fall?

Most of Georgia gets about 45–55 inches (1,100–1,400 mm) of rain a year. The northeast mountains catch moist air and get the most — up to about 70–80 inches. Summer thunderstorms, hurricanes in late summer and fall, and occasional droughts all affect farming and water supply.

How big is Georgia?

About 59,425 square miles (about 154,000 km²) — by land area the largest state east of the Mississippi River, bigger than all of England. It has 159 counties, second only to Texas.

Drinking water

Atlanta isn’t on a big river. Most of its water comes from the Chattahoochee and Lake Lanier, made by Buford Dam in the 1950s. In dry years Georgia, Alabama and Florida have argued for decades over sharing that water — the “tri-state water wars.”

🚢

Transportation & trade

Savannah, near the mouth of the Savannah River, has been a port since 1733; today it is one of the busiest container ports in the US. Brunswick ships huge numbers of cars. Before railroads, Fall Line cities marked the end of river travel.

⚡

Power

The rapids of the Fall Line once turned water wheels for grist mills and cotton mills — Columbus and Augusta grew on water power. Today dams on Georgia’s rivers make electricity, and power plants use river water for cooling.

🌾

Farming & fishing

Farmers in southwest Georgia irrigate peanuts, cotton and corn with groundwater and river water. The coastal salt marshes are nurseries for shrimp, crabs and fish — Georgia has about a third of the salt marsh on the US East Coast.

⚠️ Don’t mix up:The Fall Line is not a river and not a region — it is the boundary between the Piedmont and the Coastal Plain. Its three big cities: Augusta (Savannah River), Macon (Ocmulgee River), Columbus (Chattahoochee River). Memory hook: “A-M-C — Augusta, Macon, Columbus, in a line.”

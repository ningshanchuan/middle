Jimmy Carter: from peanut farm to Nobel Prize

Maynard Jackson (1938–2003)

In 1973, at 35, Jackson was elected mayor of Atlanta (taking office in 1974) — the first Black mayor of a major Southern city. He insisted that minority-owned firms get a fair share of city contracts — up from under 1% to more than 35% — and oversaw a huge new airport terminal, finished ahead of schedule and under budget. He won a third term in 1990.

Andrew Young (born 1932)

Young worked beside Martin Luther King Jr. In 1972 he became the first Black Georgian elected to Congress since Reconstruction; from 1977 to 1979 he was President Carter’s ambassador to the United Nations. Elected mayor in 1981 (served 1982–1990), he courted foreign investment and co-chaired the committee for the 1996 Olympics.

The 1996 Centennial Olympics

In September 1990 Atlanta beat Athens and four other cities to host. From July 19 to August 4, 1996, 10,318 athletes from 197 nations competed in 26 sports, and more than 2 million visitors came. On July 27 a bomb in Centennial Olympic Park killed two people and injured more than 100; the Games went on.

⏱️

Short-term impacts

An estimated $5.14 billion economic impact; about 7,500 new hotel rooms built between 1990 and the Games; construction and tourism jobs; an estimated 3.5 billion people watched part of the Games on TV.

🏙️

Long-term impacts

The Olympic Village became college dorms and mixed-income housing; the stadium became the Braves’ Turner Field and later Georgia State University’s Center Parc Stadium; Centennial Olympic Park revived downtown, and conventions and international tourism grew for years.

Mission 2: can this ship get in?

The Port of Savannah sits up the Savannah River, miles from the Atlantic, so ships must follow a dredged channel. The Savannah Harbor Expansion Project (2015–2022, about $973 million) deepened that channel from 42 to 47 feet. Pick a ship, set its load and the tide, and see if it can pass safely.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Standard: Georgia Standards of Excellence, Grade 8 Georgia Studies — SS8H12, “Explain the importance of development in Georgia since the late 20th century”: a Atlanta under mayors Maynard Jackson and Andrew Young; b Jimmy Carter as state senator, governor, president and past president; c short- and long-term impacts of the 1996 Olympics on Georgia’s economic and population growth; d Georgia’s role in the 21st-century national and global economy — tourism, Savannah port expansion and the film industry. (The site catalog labels this lesson SS8H11; under the current GSE it is SS8H12.) Connects to the economics standards SS8E.

Link to China’s curriculum: People’s Education Press Geography, Grade 8 Volume 1, Chapter 4 “China’s Economic Development,” Section 1 “Transportation” covers ports, air travel and choosing transport. A useful comparison: Shanghai, the world’s busiest container port, handles roughly nine times as many containers a year as Savannah (approximate).

Notes: ship drafts, tide and safety margin are simplified, approximate values; the game’s points are designed to start a discussion about trade-offs, not an economic forecast. The “what really happened” panels come from the New Georgia Encyclopedia and news reports.

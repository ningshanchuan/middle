Georgia Government & Citizenship

You can’t vote yet — but Georgia’s government already decides when school starts, which bus you ride and how old you must be for a driver’s license. Who makes those rules?

📜 What’s in the Georgia Constitution?

The current constitution took effect on 1 July 1983 — Georgia’s 10th (1777, 1789, 1798, 1861, 1865, 1868, 1877, 1945, 1976, 1983). It opens with a preamble; Article I is the Bill of Rights; there are 11 articles in all. Like the US Constitution it sets up three branches and protects rights — but the US Constitution is the supreme law, so Georgia’s may not conflict with it.

✍️ How is it amended?

An amendment must first win a two-thirds vote in both the Senate and the House, then a majority of voters at the next general election. That’s why amendments show up on Georgia ballots so often.

🚩 The flag pledge’s three principles

“I pledge allegiance to the Georgia flag and to the principles for which it stands: Wisdom, Justice and Moderation.” The same three words are on the three pillars of the state seal. Memory hook: W-J-M.

Rights and responsibilities

Rights (Article I): free speech, press and religion; due process; trial by jury; no unreasonable searches. Responsibilities: obey laws, pay taxes, serve on a jury, vote, stay informed and respect others’ rights.

Who can vote in Georgia?

US citizen; resident of Georgia and of the county; 18 by Election Day (you may register at 17½); registered by the deadline; not serving a sentence for a felony; not ruled mentally incompetent by a judge. Voters show a photo ID.

159 counties, 500+ cities — and special districts

Georgia has 159 counties — more than any state except Texas (254). Click any county on the map, or take the county hunt.

🏛️

Counties

Counties began as arms of the state: run courts, keep land and marriage records, build roads, keep the peace. A board of commissioners governs; the sheriff, probate judge, clerk of superior court and tax commissioner are elected.

🏙️

Cities

A city is created when residents ask and the General Assembly grants a charter. Cities provide police, fire, water and parks. Three forms: mayor–council (strong or weak mayor), council–manager (a hired professional manager) and commission.

🚇

Special-purpose districts

Local governments with one job: 180 school districts, MARTA transit in metro Atlanta, water and airport authorities, development authorities. They can cross county lines.

💰 Where does local money come from?Property taxes on homes and land are the biggest source. A SPLOST (special purpose local option sales tax) adds a penny per dollar — only after voters approve it in a referendum — and pays for projects like schools and roads. Add user fees (water, trash) and state and federal grants. Commissioners or council members adopt a budget each year after public hearings.

Mission 4: courts and juvenile justice

The judicial branch has a dual purpose: to interpret the laws and to administer justice. Adults and young people go through different systems.

🚔

Criminal law

A crime harms society, so the state (a prosecutor) charges the defendant. Outcomes: fines, probation, prison. Felonies (like burglary) are serious; misdemeanors (like minor shoplifting) less so. Example: “State of Georgia v. Smith.”

🤝

Civil law

Disputes between people or businesses: contracts, damaged property, divorce, rent. A plaintiff sues a defendant, usually for money; nobody goes to prison. Example: a neighbor’s tree crushes your car.

Delinquent — or a child in need of services?

Delinquent act: would be a crime if an adult did it. Status offense (once called “unruly”; since Georgia’s 2014 juvenile code, a “child in need of services,” CHINS): only an offense because of the person’s age.

What rights do juveniles have?

Notice of the charges; a lawyer (the court appoints one if the family can’t pay); to remain silent; to confront and question witnesses; a transcript of the hearing; to appeal. A judge hears the case — usually no jury.

What are the consequences?

CHINS: counseling, family services and community programs — the goal is help, not punishment. Delinquent acts: probation, community service, paying the victim back (restitution), fines and, for serious cases, a youth detention center or Youth Development Campus (YDC).

When is a teen tried as an adult?

If a 13- to 17-year-old is charged with one of seven of the most serious violent crimes (such as murder or armed robbery with a gun), the case can go to superior court as an adult case — from a 1994 state law (SB 440). In Georgia, 17-year-olds are usually already treated as adults for crimes.

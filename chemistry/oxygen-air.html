Worked example: Turn a reading into a percentage

A bottle initially holds 300 mL of air. After cooling and opening the clamp, 60 mL of water enters. Estimate the oxygen fraction.

  1. Use the incoming water to estimate the volume of gas removed, about 60 mL.
  2. Divide by the starting air volume.
    60 mL ÷ 300 mL × 100% = 20%

The measured value is about 20%, or one fifth. The reference value for dry air is about 21%.

What does a catalyst change?

Manganese dioxide is a catalyst for this reaction. It speeds up the breakdown of hydrogen peroxide, and its mass and chemical properties are the same before and after the reaction. Equal starting amounts of peroxide yield the same amount of oxygen if both samples break down completely.

Lab safety

  • Use these demonstrations only in a supervised lab. Wear goggles and point heated test tubes away from people.
  • Check for leaks before generating gas. Keep the delivery path open so pressure cannot build up.
  • When collecting oxygen over water from a heated tube, lift the delivery tube out of the water before stopping the heat to prevent suck-back into hot glass.

Use the evidence

Practice with explanations

How much oxygen is in 500 mL of dry air?

A dry air sample has a volume of 500 mL and is about 21% oxygen. Find the volume of oxygen.

At the same temperature and pressure, multiply the air volume by the oxygen fraction.

500 mL × 0.21 = 105 mL

About 105 mL. A rough one-fifth estimate gives about 100 mL.

Why would charcoal give an unreliable reading in the phosphorus setup?

Burning charcoal consumes oxygen and makes gaseous carbon dioxide. Some carbon dioxide can also dissolve into the water, so the water rise does not directly measure the oxygen used. The phosphorus oxide in the red-phosphorus demonstration is present as solid particles after cooling.

How would you fairly compare equal amounts of peroxide with and without MnO₂?

Use solutions of the same concentration, volume, and temperature. Under the same collection conditions, time how long each takes to reach a fixed volume of oxygen. The catalyzed sample reaches it sooner. Complete decomposition gives both the same final amount.

Teacher and parent notes

Ask for an observation before an inference. The phosphorus model neglects effects such as the pressure of the water column. After cooling to room temperature, the incoming water approximates the gas volume consumed. The 21% value is a dry-air reference and 20% is a sample experimental reading.

Textbook terms: 红磷 (hónglín, red phosphorus), 排水法 (páishuǐfǎ, collection over water), 催化剂 (cuīhuàjì, catalyst), 化合反应 (huàhé fǎnyìng, combination or synthesis), and 分解反应 (fēnjiě fǎnyìng, decomposition).

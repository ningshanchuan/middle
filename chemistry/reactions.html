Chemical Reactions & Conservation of Mass

When a log burns down to a small pile of ash, where did the rest of it go? In a chemical reaction, old bonds break and atoms regroup into new molecules. But atoms are never created or destroyed. Reading a chemical equation

A chemical equation is the “sentence” for a reaction: reactants on the left, products on the right, and the arrow means “yields” or “makes”.

🧪

Reactants

The starting substances, written to the left of the arrow.

➡️

Arrow

Read as “yields”. Old bonds break and new bonds form.

✨

Products

The new substances made, written to the right of the arrow.

⚖️

Conservation of mass

Total mass of reactants = total mass of products, because the atoms are just rearranged.

🔢 Coefficients vs subscripts

In 2H₂O, the big 2 is a coefficient (2 water molecules) and the small ₂ is a subscript (2 H atoms in each molecule). When balancing, change ONLY coefficients, never subscripts. Changing a subscript makes a different substance!

🫙 Closed vs open systems

In a closed container nothing gets in or out, so the scale reads the same before and after. In an open container, gases escape into the air and the scale reads less, but the atoms still exist; they just left the scale.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Ash weighs less than the log, and a candle gets shorter, so mass seems to “disappear”. Really, the CO₂ and water vapor escaped into the air. Collect every gas and weigh it, and the total mass hasn’t changed at all.

3-step method

  1. Count: list how many of each atom are on each side.
  2. Start with the most complicated substance; leave single elements (O₂, H₂) for last.
  3. Re-count after every change, and use the smallest whole numbers.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P1f: construct an explanation based on evidence to describe conservation of matter in a chemical reaction, including the resulting differences between products and reactants. Suggested flow: drag the progress slider and have students count each kind of atom before and after; switch on “Unbalanced equation” and ask why the scale tips (atoms vanishing or appearing breaks conservation of mass); then switch on “Open system” and discuss why burning things seem to lose mass.

Model notes: masses are in atomic mass units (H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16). Each reaction shows only the smallest whole-number set of molecules from its equation.

The fire triangle

In this lesson, combustion means a fuel reacts with oxygen, releasing heat and often light. The ignition temperature (着火点, zháohuǒdiǎn) is the lowest temperature at which a fuel starts to burn under the given conditions. White and red phosphorus are two forms of the same element. Both can burn. Red phosphorus ignites at a higher temperature.

Remember all three together: fuel, oxygen and a temperature high enough for that fuel to ignite.

Putting out fires and getting to safety

Which side of the fire triangle is lost?

If a pan catches fire, get to safety. Never pour water on a grease fire or move the pan. Burning oil can splash and spread the fire.

Why does the type of extinguisher matter?

Water extinguishers mainly cool ordinary burning solids such as wood. Some dry chemical extinguishers interrupt the chemical reactions that keep flames going. Trained adults select an extinguisher for the material that is burning.

Cooling lowers the fuel’s temperature. Its ignition temperature under the same conditions stays the same.

Get out and call for help

If there’s a fire, alert people nearby and leave by a safe route. Use the stairs and avoid elevators. Once outside, stay out. Call from a safe place: 119 in mainland China or 911 in the US. Give the address and say if anyone is trapped.

Plan two ways out

With your household, plan two ways out of each room and agree on an outdoor meeting place. If smoke or fire blocks the usual exit, find a clear route. If no route is safe, keep a closed door between you and the fire, call for help immediately and give your location.

Why do flour mills and coal mines control dust?

Flour and coal dust can burn. When dust hangs in the air, more particle surface is exposed to oxygen. If it catches fire, the fire can spread rapidly. In a confined space, rapid burning can make pressure rise suddenly and cause a dust explosion.

Flour mills and coal mines protect workers through professional controls that limit airborne dust and dust buildup and reduce ignition hazards.

Fossil fuels and chemical energy

Coal formed mainly from ancient land plants. Oil and much natural gas formed from ancient organisms buried in sediment. Over geologic time, heat and pressure helped turn their remains into fossil fuels. These fuels form far more slowly than we use them, so they are nonrenewable.

Coal, petroleum and natural gas are mixtures. Methane is the main component of natural gas. Combustion is exothermic. Some of the chemical energy in the reactants transfers to the surroundings as heat and sometimes as light.

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a poisonous gas you can’t see or smell. Incomplete combustion can produce it. A home CO alarm can warn you early. Use charcoal grills and portable fuel-powered generators outdoors. Keep generators more than 20 feet (about 6 meters) from doors, windows and vents.

Worked examples

US-style example · Check conservation of atoms

Problem: One methane molecule reacts with two oxygen molecules in complete combustion. How many oxygen atoms end up in the products? The complete-combustion model above shows twice these amounts. Count its oxygen atoms, then halve that number.

CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O
  1. Two O₂ molecules start with two oxygen atoms each.
    2 × 2 = 4
  2. In the products, one CO₂ molecule has two oxygen atoms, and two H₂O molecules have one each.
    1 × 2 + 2 × 1 = 4

Answer: four oxygen atoms. Carbon and hydrogen atoms are conserved as well.

Chinese-style example · Find the mass of carbon dioxide
8 g

Find: the mass of carbon dioxide produced.

Solve: in complete combustion one CO₂ molecule forms for each CH₄ molecule. Their rounded relative molecular masses, 16 and 44, give a mass ratio of 16 to 44.

The CO₂ has more mass than the methane consumed because oxygen also reacts. To check conservation of mass, add up the masses of all the reactants and all the products separately.

Challenge · How much methane releases the same heat?

Given 0.50 kg of hydrogen burning completely, find the mass of methane that releases the same heat. Set the slider to 0.50 kg first and read the heat released by hydrogen.

Solve: find the heat from hydrogen, then divide by methane’s lower heating value.

0.50 kg × 120 MJ/kg = 60 MJ
60 MJ ÷ 50 MJ/kg = 1.2 kg

Answer: about 1.2 kg of methane. The chart compares fuel mass alone. Storage tank mass and equipment efficiency also matter in practice.

Cleaner fuels and new energy

For the same heat released, burning natural gas generally emits less CO₂ than burning coal. It also tends to emit lower amounts of several other air pollutants. Burning natural gas still releases CO₂, and methane that leaks from the supply system is a greenhouse gas.

Hydrogen, solar and wind
Hydrogen
Hydrogen is an energy carrier. Making it requires energy. A hydrogen fuel cell uses hydrogen and oxygen to generate electricity and makes water. The emissions from making hydrogen depend on the materials and energy source used.
Solar
Photovoltaic panels turn light into electricity without burning fuel to generate it.
Wind
Wind turns turbine blades, and a generator converts that motion into electricity.

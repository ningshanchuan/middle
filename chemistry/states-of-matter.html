States of Matter: Dancing Particles

Why does ice melt into a puddle, and why does the puddle vanish on a sunny day? Ice, water and steam are the same substance: water molecules! The only difference is how fast the molecules move and how close together they stay. Heat up or cool down this box of water molecules and watch what happens.

The particle model: four states side by side

All matter is made of tiny particles that never stop moving. The attraction between particles and how fast they move decide whether a substance is a solid, liquid or gas.

Change-of-state map (click an arrow to watch it in the 3D box above)
⚠️ Common mix-up:Particles do NOT get bigger when a substance turns into a gas. The SPACE between them grows. A water molecule in steam is exactly the same as one in ice.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P1: use the particle model to describe the arrangement and motion of particles in solids, liquids, gases (and plasma), and explain changes of state caused by heating or cooling (melting, freezing, vaporization, condensation, sublimation, deposition). The 3D heat slider uses real proportions: ask students to notice that boiling needs about 7× more energy than melting (2260 J/g vs 334 J/g). Ask: why does the temperature stay flat on a plateau? (The energy overcomes attractions between particles instead of raising their average kinetic energy.) You can also point out that ice is less dense than water, which is why ice floats. Note: real water also evaporates below 100 °C (only at the surface); the model leaves this out for simplicity.

Properties and changes

Properties describe what a substance is like; changes describe what happens to it. The key question is always: was a NEW substance made?

📏

Physical properties & changes

Properties you can observe or measure without changing what the substance is.

  • Properties: color, state, density, melting & boiling point, hardness, solubility, magnetism
  • Changes: shape, size or state changes (cut, crush, melt, freeze, boil, dissolve)
  • Same substance before and after; the particles themselves don’t change
🔥

Chemical properties & changes

Properties you can only observe when the substance turns into something else.

  • Properties: flammability, reacting with oxygen (rusting), reacting with acids, stability
  • Changes: burning, rusting, cooking, rotting, fermenting, reacting with acid
  • Atoms rearrange into new substances with new properties
⚠️ Common mix-up:Bubbles don’t always mean a chemical change! Bubbles in boiling water are just water vapor, still H₂O. And “can’t be undone” doesn’t always mean chemical: cut paper can’t be put back together, but it is still paper.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P1, especially S8P1c (plan and carry out an investigation to compare chemical properties such as reactivity and combustibility with physical properties such as density, melting point and boiling point) and S8P1d (construct an argument from observational evidence that a change is chemical or physical). Have students use “predict, then observe” in the info panel, then discuss: which evidence is most convincing? Why can the time slider refreeze the ice but never un-burn the wood?

Science notes: baking soda + vinegar absorbs heat, so the mixture gets colder; a temperature change can be a drop, not only a rise. Dissolving is treated as a physical change at this level.

Indicator color chart

Different indicators change color over different parts of the pH scale. Drag the slider to see what color each indicator shows in the same solution.

🍋

Acids (pH < 7)

Release hydrogen ions (H⁺) in water; taste sour; turn blue litmus red; many react with metals to make hydrogen gas.

💧

Neutral (pH = 7)

Equal amounts of H⁺ and OH⁻. Pure water, salt water and sugar water are neutral.

🧼

Bases (pH > 7)

Release hydroxide ions (OH⁻) in water; taste bitter and feel slippery; turn red litmus blue and phenolphthalein pink.

Simulation: neutralization titration

The flask holds 25.0 mL of dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl, 0.10 mol/L). The burette holds sodium hydroxide (NaOH) of the same concentration. Open the tap slowly and stop the moment the indicator changes color — that’s the end point.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports GSE S8P1 (structure and properties of matter): acidity is a chemical property that can be tested with indicators, and neutralization makes new substances (a salt and water) — a clear example of a chemical change. The 3D beaker uses 1 mol/L vinegar (acetic acid) and ammonia, 0.05 mL per drop, with pH computed exactly from charge balance; household pH values are typical approximations. Suggested activities: (1) make red-cabbage indicator at home and test kitchen items; (2) have students explain why each pH step is a 10× change; (3) in the titration, compare phenolphthalein with litmus as the indicator. Safety: never mix bleach and ammonia; never taste or sniff lab chemicals.

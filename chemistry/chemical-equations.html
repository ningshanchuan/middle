Account for the gas

The substances that react and the products they form have the same total mass. Solid calcium carbonate gets used up, and carbon dioxide bubbles form. Atoms rearrange during the reaction, and the count of each kind stays the same. The balance under the open vessel weighs what remains. Add the escaped carbon dioxide to get the original total mass.

Each balance was zeroed with its empty vessel in place. Each mixture contains 10.0 g of calcium carbonate and makes 4.4 g of carbon dioxide when it reacts completely, using rounded atomic masses. How do coefficients give a mass ratio?

For substances made of molecules, such as hydrogen, oxygen and water, the coefficients give the molecule count ratio. Multiply each coefficient by its substance’s relative molecular mass to get the mass ratio of reactants and products.

Use rounded relative atomic masses H = 1 and O = 16. Relative molecular mass has no unit. The models show molecule counts with simplified sizes and spacing. The bars show sample masses in grams that follow the same reaction proportions.

Common mix-up: the coefficient 2 in front of H₂ counts two copies of H₂. To find hydrogen’s term in the mass ratio, multiply 2 × 2 = 4.

Match the terms

In the table, line up the known and unknown amounts under their formulas. For the top row, multiply each coefficient by the relative formula mass. For the bottom row, use the actual masses. Keep the same order when you write the proportion.

Relative formula mass is the sum of the relative atomic masses in a formula. For a substance made of molecules, this is its relative molecular mass.

Use the mass of the substance that actually reacts. For example, the H₂O₂ term uses the mass of the hydrogen peroxide itself.

A 25 g limestone sample reacts with excess hydrochloric acid. Assume its impurities do not react. Change the mass percentage of CaCO₃ to see how much CO₂ forms.

Challenge: what mass percentage of CaCO₃ gives 6.6 g of CO₂?

First find the required calcium carbonate: 6.6 g × 100/44 = 15 g. Then divide by the sample mass: 15 g ÷ 25 g = 60%.

Most metals are shiny and conduct heat and electricity well. They can usually be pressed into sheets (malleability) or drawn into wires (ductility). Hardness, density, and melting point vary. Mercury is liquid at room temperature.

An alloy (héjīn, 合金) is a metallic material made by combining a metal with other elements. Many alloys are harder than their main pure metal. Many also start melting at a lower temperature than that metal. Copper is common in wires because it conducts electricity well and is easy to draw into wire.

Metals with oxygen and acid

The same metal can show very different changes in oxygen and in acid.

Watch for an oxide

Many metals react with oxygen to form metal oxides. The metal and the reaction conditions determine what you observe.

Magnesium, zinc, and iron react with dilute HCl to form magnesium chloride, zinc chloride, and iron(II) chloride, respectively. All three release hydrogen. Copper gives no hydrogen. The activity series helps predict whether a reaction can happen. The actual rate depends on factors such as temperature, surface area, and the condition of the metal surface.

Challenge: What does surface area change?

Change the area from 0.5× to 2×. Compare the zinc curve at 4 seconds. What happens to its final hydrogen yield?

The larger exposed area has produced more hydrogen at 4 seconds. Both strips start with the same number of zinc atoms and have excess acid, so they produce the same amount once they react completely.

Worked example: theoretical iron yield

Given: 500 kg of ore contains the selected mass percentage of iron(III) oxide (Fe₂O₃). Assume this oxide is the only source of iron in the ore and all its iron is recovered as metal. Use relative atomic masses Fe = 56 and O = 16.

Find: What is the maximum mass of iron that can be recovered?

Solution: Find the mass of iron oxide. Then multiply by the fraction of the oxide’s mass that comes from iron.

Block water and oxygen

Keep steel dry, or add a barrier such as paint or grease. Inspect and repair damaged coatings.

Coatings and alloys

Galvanizing adds a zinc barrier. At a small scratch, zinc can also oxidize before the iron does. Chromium in stainless steel helps form a protective surface film.

Sort and recycle scrap

A magnet can help separate ordinary steel from aluminum. Sorted steel scrap can be remelted, reducing demand for newly mined ore. Keeping products in use longer also saves resources.

Practice with the evidence

Same metal, different bubbling speeds

Equal masses of zinc granules and zinc strip are put in separate portions of identical excess dilute HCl. The granules bubble faster at first. Do the two samples eventually make the same mass of hydrogen?

Yes. The greater exposed surface area can speed up the reaction. Both samples contain the same amount of zinc and react completely, so the final hydrogen masses are equal.

Rank three metals from two observations

X displaces Y from a solution of Y’s salt. Z displaces X from a solution of X’s salt. Rank the three metals from most active to least active. Explain what each observation tells you.

The first observation puts X above Y. The second puts Z above X. Together the order is Z, X, Y.

Recover silver from silver nitrate solution

A copper strip is placed in silver nitrate solution. Which solid forms on the strip, and why does the solution turn blue? Predict before checking the equation.

Copper ranks above silver, so metallic silver forms. Dissolved copper(II) ions give the solution its blue color.

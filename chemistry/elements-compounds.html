Elements, Compounds & Mixtures

Sodium is a metal that reacts violently with water. Chlorine is a poisonous gas. So why is it safe to eat them joined together as table salt? Everything is made of atoms. HOW those atoms are put together decides whether a substance is an element, a compound or a mixture. Four kinds of matter

First ask: is it pure (only one kind of particle)? If yes, ask how many elements it contains.

⚪

Element

A pure substance with only one kind of atom. It cannot be broken down into simpler substances.

O₂ · Fe · Au · He
🔗

Compound

A pure substance of two or more elements chemically bonded in a fixed ratio. It has a formula.

H₂O · CO₂ · NaCl
🥛

Homogeneous mixture

Substances mixed but not bonded, the same all the way through. You can’t see the parts.

air · salt water · vinegar
🥗

Heterogeneous mixture

Not the same everywhere. You can see or pick out the different parts.

sand + iron · salad · trail mix
Matter→One kind of particle? Yes → pure substance→1 element → element · 2+ elements → compound·No → mixture (homogeneous / heterogeneous)
⚠️ Common mix-up:“Molecule” and “compound” are not the same thing. O₂ is a molecule but not a compound; NaCl is a compound but is not made of molecules.

How to read a formula

0formulas solved
0total atoms in this formula
  • A capital letter starts a new element (Na, Cl, O).
  • A subscript (small low number) counts the atom right before it. No subscript means 1.
  • A coefficient (big number in front) multiplies the whole formula. 2H₂O has 4 H and 2 O.
  • A subscript after parentheses multiplies everything inside: Ca(OH)₂ has 2 O and 2 H.
💡 Memory hintA subscript is shy (only the atom before it); a coefficient is bossy (the whole formula).

    👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

    Supports GSE S8P1a: develop and use a model to compare and contrast pure substances (elements and compounds) and mixtures. Suggested flow: use “Show a sample” to compare pure water with salt water so students state “a pure substance has only one kind of particle”; use the ball-and-stick / space-filling slider to discuss that every model is an approximation. The separation lab connects the definition of a mixture (parts can be separated physically) to properties like magnetism, solubility and boiling point.

    Language note for bilingual classes: Chinese textbooks distinguish 元素 (a kind of atom) from 单质 (a pure substance made of one element); English uses “element” for both.

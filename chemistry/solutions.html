⚠️ Common mix-up:Dissolving is not melting! Melting needs heat to turn a solid into a liquid; dissolving is when solute particles spread out between solvent particles. The sugar’s mass doesn’t vanish: 100 g water + 20 g sugar = 120 g of sugar water.

Experiment: the dissolving race (a fair test)

Three things make a solid dissolve faster: higher temperature, stirring, and smaller pieces. Set up two cups, then race to see which cup dissolves its 10 g of sugar first. Remember: change only ONE variable at a time!

Why faster?

—cup A time
—cup B time

🌡 Hotter: water particles move faster and hit the solid harder and more often.
🥄 Stirring: carries dissolved particles away and brings fresh water to the surface.
🧂 Smaller pieces: the same mass has much more surface area touching the water.

💡 Memory hint“Heat, stir, crush” = dissolve faster. But only temperature changes HOW MUCH can dissolve (the solubility).

Supports GSE S8P1a (solutions are homogeneous mixtures; pure substances vs. mixtures) and S8P1b (solubility as a physical property). In the 3D model each dot stands for a cluster of solute particles, not a single molecule.

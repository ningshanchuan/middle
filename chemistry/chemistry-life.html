Six classes of nutrients

Sugars and starches are both carbohydrates. A blue-black result shows that the sample contains starch. When you add iodine solution to sugar water, the mixture looks amber.

What makes a balanced diet? The Food Pagoda and MyPlate

The 2022 Chinese Food Guide Pagoda uses five levels to show relative amounts. From bottom to top they show grains and tubers, vegetables and fruit, fish, poultry, meat and eggs, dairy alongside soybeans and nuts, and finally cooking oil and salt. The school-age version gives guidance by age.

The former USDA MyPlate graphic put fruits and vegetables on half the plate, grains and protein foods on the other half, and dairy on the side. The 2025–2030 U.S. Dietary Guidelines use a new food pyramid graphic.

  • Chinese-style meal: mixed-grain rice, greens, steamed fish, a mandarin, and plain yogurt.
  • U.S.-style meal: a whole-wheat sandwich with roast chicken, lettuce and tomato, plus an apple and plain yogurt.

Aim for variety across meals. Most foods supply more than one nutrient.

Chemical elements and health

Choose an element to see its role in the body

Major and trace refer to amount

Calcium is a major mineral. Iron, zinc, and iodine are trace minerals. Food supplies these elements in chemical forms the body can use.

The body needs trace minerals in small amounts. Getting too little or too much can affect health. Iodized salt supplies iodine without adding more salt to meals.

Mold, nitrite, and misuse of formaldehyde
  • Mold. Some molds produce toxins. Mold can grow out of sight in the tiny holes in bread. If you find mold in a loaf of bread, discard the whole loaf.
  • Nitrite. In regulated meat curing, nitrite can slow the growth of some harmful bacteria. Too much nitrite can reduce the blood’s ability to carry oxygen. Sodium nitrite is unsafe as a substitute for table salt.
  • Formaldehyde. Some foods naturally contain small amounts. Adding formaldehyde directly to preserve food is prohibited. Determining whether someone added it takes more evidence than appearance alone.

Four material families · Properties and uses
  • Metals and alloys. Steel is an iron-based alloy in which carbon is an important ingredient. Copper conducts electricity well, so it is used for wiring.
  • Inorganic nonmetallic materials. This class includes glass and ceramics. Window glass lets light through. Porcelain is hard and brittle. On Jingdezhen blue-and-white porcelain, cobalt-blue brushwork shows clearly under a transparent glaze.
  • Organic polymer materials. Plastics, synthetic fibers, and synthetic rubber are common synthetic polymer materials. Polyethylene (PE) is a thermoplastic that can soften and be reshaped. Cured epoxy is a cross-linked thermoset.
  • Composites. A resin reinforced with carbon fibers can make lightweight parts that resist bending. In reinforced concrete, concrete handles compression well and steel bars can carry tension.

A thermoplastic softens with heat and hardens on cooling. In a cured thermoset, cross-links join the chains into a network that resists flowing into a new shape.

White pollution, degradable plastics, and green chemistry

The Chinese term “white pollution” refers to litter from discarded plastics, such as bags, films, and foam containers. Cutting single-use waste, reusing items, and following local disposal rules help keep plastics out of the environment.

“Biodegradable” means microbes can break down the plastic under certain conditions. “Commercially compostable” means it can break down within a set time under controlled composting conditions. Check whether your local compost site accepts it.

Green chemistry aims to reduce the amount of hazardous material a product or process uses or creates. For example, a redesigned process can start with safer ingredients and leave less hazardous waste.

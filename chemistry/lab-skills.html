Transfer and measure

Read the label first. Scoop powder with a spatula and guide it into a test tube with a folded paper trough. For a solid piece, remember three moves: hold the tube sideways, place the piece at the mouth with forceps, then slowly stand the tube up.

Handling notes and minimum amounts

For powder, hold the tube nearly horizontal, guide the paper trough to the bottom, then slowly bring the tube upright. For a solid piece, use forceps to place it at the mouth of a nearly horizontal tube and slowly tip the tube upright so the piece slides down.

Set the stopper upside down, with the clean inner face up. Hold the bottle with its label toward your palm and replace the stopper promptly. Dispense with the dropper vertical, bulb up and tip above the opening. Keep the tip clear of the vessel. Wash a shared dropper as directed, and return a dedicated dropper to its own bottle.

Use the specified amount. The mainland textbook convention for an approved activity with no amount specified is usually 1–2 mL of liquid or just enough solid to cover the test tube bottom. Follow the supervising adult’s directions in the lab.

Move your eye and read the cylinder

Same water level, different readings

Stand the cylinder upright on a level surface with the scale facing you. Water forms a curved surface called a meniscus. Read its lowest point at eye level. Looking down gives a reading that is too high. Looking up gives one that is too low.

This enlarged side view is a model. Raising the eye by one step increases the reading by 1 mL here. The error in a real lab depends on where you look from and the shape of the cylinder.

Worked examples · US reading and Chinese calculation

US style · Count the intervals

There are five equal spaces between 20 mL and 25 mL. The lowest point of the meniscus is two spaces above 20 mL. What does the scale read?

  1. Find the volume per interval.(25 mL − 20 mL) ÷ 5 = 1 mL
  2. Count up two intervals from 20 mL.20 mL + 2 × 1 mL = 22 mL

Chinese style · Find the volume poured out

  1. Given: a cylinder held 23 mL of water. After some is poured out, 17 mL remains.
  2. Find: the volume poured out.
  3. Solution: subtract the remaining volume from the starting volume.23 mL − 17 mL = 6 mL
  4. Answer: 6 mL of water was poured out.

These examples use whole-milliliter marks. Follow the instrument scale and reporting directions for decimal places in lab work.

In-class check · Transfer and measurement

Heat a liquid in a test tube

Check the fill and where the tube points, use the outer flame to warm the tube evenly, then heat the part holding liquid.

Before and after heating

Grip a dry, heat-resistant test tube with a holder near the open end. Fill to at most one-third of its capacity. For liquid heating, hold the tube at about 45° to the bench and point the open end away from everyone. Let hot glass cool in the designated heat-safe place. Never add fuel to a lit or hot lamp.

In-class check · Heating

Wash, connect, and check for leaks

For this hand-warming test, watch the submerged delivery-tube tip. If there are no bubbles, check the joints, look for a blocked tube, and consider whether the trapped air warmed enough.

Why does hand warming reveal a leak?

Immerse the delivery-tube tip first, then warm the stoppered empty test tube with a hand. With tight joints, some of the warming air is pushed out through the tip as bubbles. When you let go, the air cools, its pressure falls, and water may rise into the delivery tube.

In-class check · Care and airtightness

Read signs and respond safely

Find the eyewash and safety shower before lab work begins. For a bench spill, stop, warn people nearby, and tell the supervising adult. If a chemical gets in an eye, start rinsing immediately while someone alerts the adult.

Protective gear, signs, and waste

Wear chemical splash goggles and closed-toe shoes, tie back long hair, and use an apron and suitable gloves as directed. A red diamond on a label surrounds a chemical hazard pictogram. A goggles sign tells you what to wear. An eyewash sign shows where to find the equipment.

Put leftover reagents in the designated labeled waste container as directed. Never return leftovers to the stock bottle. Use the sink only when the lab procedure specifically allows it. Ask the supervising adult how to handle unknown waste and keep it separate. The adult handles broken glass according to lab procedure. Wash hands with soap and water at the end.

Extra practice · Reveal the reasoning

You look down at a cylinder and read 21 mL. At eye level, will the correct reading be above or below 21 mL? Try the model to test your prediction.

Show the reasoning

The correct reading is below 21 mL. With 20 mL in the model, move the eye up one step. The sightline crosses a higher mark, so the model reads 21 mL. The amount of error in a real lab depends on the viewing angle and instrument.

A leak test shows no bubbles and the joints look snug. What else should you consider before starting the experiment?

Show the reasoning

Check whether the tip is under water, the delivery tube is clear, and the trapped air warmed enough. Ask the supervising adult to check the setup before starting.

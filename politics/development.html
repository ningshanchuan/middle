Richer and Cleaner? How China Plans Its Development

Can a country get richer and cleaner and fairer at the same time — or does it have to choose? In about forty years China went from a poor country to the world’s second-largest economy, and paid a price in smoggy air and polluted rivers along the way. Today Chinese ninth-graders study three key words for what comes next: innovation (创新, chuàngxīn), sharing (共享, gòngxiǎng) and green (绿色, lǜsè). 👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

This lesson follows China’s national curriculum standard for Morality and Rule of Law (2022 edition; “national conditions” theme) and the wording of the national unified textbook 《道德与法治》 Grade 9 Volume 1: Unit 1 “Prosperity and Innovation” (Lesson 1 “On the Road to a Strong Country”; Lesson 2 “Innovation-driven Development”) and Unit 3 Lesson 6 “Building a Beautiful China”. Phrases such as “reform and opening up is the road to a strong country”, “innovation is the primary driving force of development”, “common prosperity is a fundamental principle of socialism with Chinese characteristics” and “lucid waters and lush mountains are invaluable assets” are the textbook’s own.

About the simulation: board 1 is a deliberately simple teaching model. Its curves illustrate the textbook’s points (resource-heavy growth cannot last; innovation takes time to pay off; green and growth can both win; sharing improves fairness). It is not real statistics or a forecast — please tell students so. For US classrooms: the lesson presents China’s development ideas in the terms of China’s own textbook; “Compare with the US” cards are factual only. Suggested flow: hook (3 min) → free play with the sim, then the challenge “finish with both environment and economy above the start” (12 min) → boards 2 and 3 (10 min) → low-carbon day (8 min) → quiz (10 min).

One Shared World: China on the World Stage

The phone in your pocket was probably designed in one country, built from parts made in a dozen others, and put together in China. What does that tell you about the world today? Chinese ninth-graders spend a semester on this question: how the world is tied together, which problems no country can solve alone, and what part China plays. 👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

This lesson follows China’s national curriculum standard for Morality and Rule of Law (2022 edition; “national conditions” theme: world trends, a community with a shared future for humanity, China’s role in international affairs) and the wording of the national unified textbook 《道德与法治》 Grade 9 Volume 2: Unit 1 “Our Shared World” (Lessons 1–2) and Unit 2 “China on the World Stage” (Lessons 3–4). Sentences such as “peace and development are the theme of our times” are the textbook’s own.

About the map: the world map is a schematic that shows only land outlines, not national borders; China is drawn from China’s standard map data (including Taiwan Province and the South China Sea islands). Belt and Road lines are schematic, not actual transport routes. For US classrooms: the lesson presents how China’s textbook sees the world; “Compare with the US” cards are factual and say plainly where views differ — a good chance to compare sources. Suggested flow: hook “where did your phone come from?” (3 min) → map and year slider (15 min) → idea web and matching (10 min) → case files (10 min) → quiz and sorting (7 min).

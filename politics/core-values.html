Twelve Words: Chinese Culture and Core Values

If you had to sum up what your country stands for in just twelve words, which would you pick? China did exactly that: 24 Chinese characters making 12 two-character words, called the Core Socialist Values (社会主义核心价值观, shèhuì zhǔyì héxīn jiàzhíguān). You will see them on posters in every Chinese city and school. Where do they come from, and what do they have to do with 5,000 years of culture? Build the chart, drag through history, and listen for yourself.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

This lesson follows China’s national curriculum standard for Morality and Rule of Law (2022 edition; themes “education in fine traditional Chinese culture” and “national conditions”) and the wording of the national unified textbook 《道德与法治》 Grade 9 Volume 1, Unit 3 “Civilisation and Homeland”, Lesson 5 “Guarding Our Spiritual Home”. The 24 characters, their three levels (value goals of the nation, value orientation of society, value standards for citizens) and the definition of the national spirit are given in the textbook’s own wording.

For US classrooms: the lesson presents values education in China in the terms of China’s own textbook; the “Compare with the US” cards are factual and do not rank either country. The same word (“democracy”, “freedom”) points to different institutions in the two countries — help students notice “same word, different system”. Suggested flow: hook “sum up your country in 12 words” (5 min) → build board 1 and open every tile (12 min) → time slider and the culture–virtue–spirit–confidence chain (8 min) → everyday choices (10 min) → listen to the guqin, browse photos, take the quiz (10 min).

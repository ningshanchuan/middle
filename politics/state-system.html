How do 1.4 billion people run one country?

Chinese textbooks answer with a set of “systems” (制度, zhìdù): one fundamental political system — the people’s congress system — three basic political systems, and a basic economic system. 🌏 How is this different from the US?

In both countries an elected body makes the laws. The links between state bodies, the role of parties and the shape of the economy are different.

ChinaUnited States
The elected bodyNational People’s Congress: one chamber, up to 3,000 deputies, one session a year; its Standing Committee works between sessionsCongress: two chambers (Senate 100, House 435), in session most of the year
How members are chosenVoters directly elect township and county congresses; each higher congress is elected by the congresses below itVoters directly elect both chambers
How state bodies relateThe people’s congress is the organ of state power; the government, supervisory commissions, courts and procuratorates are created by it, answer to it and are supervised by itThree separate branches check and balance each other; the President is elected separately
Political partiesThe Communist Party of China (CPC) is the governing party; eight other parties take part in state affairs and are consulted — they are not opposition partiesTwo major parties compete in elections, and the party in power can change
RegionsA unitary state; areas where minority ethnic groups live in concentrated communities have regional autonomy (for example, 5 autonomous regions)A federal system: 50 states, each with its own constitution and government
EconomyPublic ownership as the mainstay, with other kinds of ownership growing alongside; a “socialist market economy”A market economy based mainly on private ownership, with government regulation

Quick game: public sector or non-public sector?

In China’s basic economic system, the public sector and the non-public sector grow side by side. Decide where each example belongs.

⚠️ Common mix-up:“Mainstay” does not mean “everything”. Public ownership is the mainstay, but the non-public sector is also an important part of the socialist market economy. And keep two words apart: the public sector is the “mainstay”, while the state-owned economy inside it is the “leading force”.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

This lesson follows mainland China’s own course: the “national conditions” and “rule-of-law education” themes for Grades 7–9 in the 2022 national curriculum standard for Morality and Rule of Law (道德与法治), and Lesson 5 (China’s political and economic systems) in Unit 3 “The People Are Masters of the Country” of the national unified textbook, Grade 8 Volume 2. Definitions use the textbook’s wording; lesson titles and their order differ slightly between printings. The basic economic system is stated in its current three-part form (ownership, distribution, socialist market economy). The English version is written for American middle-school students; the US comparison is factual only and does not rank either system.

Suggestions: while building, stress the difference between the one “fundamental” political system and the three “basic” ones. At step 2 of the power flow, add that township and county deputies are elected directly, higher ones by the congress below. The examples in the ownership game are simplified; real companies can be more mixed.

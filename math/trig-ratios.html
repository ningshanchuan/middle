Two right triangles with the same acute angle are similar. All corresponding lengths scale by the same factor, so the ratios stay the same. Pick the reference angle first. Choosing the other acute angle swaps the roles of the two legs.

Solve a right triangle

To solve a right triangle, find its missing sides and angles. Given a side and an acute angle, choose a ratio linking the known side to the side you want. Given two sides, use their ratio to find an angle and the Pythagorean theorem to find the third side.

Use degree (DEG) mode. Inverse tangent, arctan (often labeled tan⁻¹), gives the angle from its tangent.

Worked example · A hypotenuse and an acute angle

Given ∠C = 90°, ∠A = 30°, and AB = 12 m. Find the lengths BC and AC and the measure of ∠B.

Solution

  1. BC is opposite ∠A. Use sine. BC = 12 m × sin 30° = 6 m
  2. AC is adjacent to ∠A. Use cosine. AC = 12 m × cos 30° = 6√3 m
  3. The acute angles are complementary. ∠B = 90° − 30° = 60°

Measure a building and a ramp

Measure up from the observer’s horizontal to the line of sight for the angle of elevation. Measure down for the angle of depression. Looking along the same line from opposite ends gives equal angles because the horizontals are parallel.

Worked example · Find the building height

On level ground, the observer is 24 m horizontally from the base. Their eyes are 1.6 m above the ground and the angle of elevation to the roof is 30°. Find the building height to the nearest 0.1 m.

Solution

  1. Draw a horizontal from eye level. The roof is Δh above this line and the horizontal distance is the adjacent leg. Δh = (24 m) tan 30° = 8√3 m
  2. Add the eye height, then round. H = (8√3 + 1.6) m ≈ 15.5 m

Worked example · From slope ratio to run and ramp length

A ramp has a slope ratio of 1:12 and rises 0.6 m. Find the horizontal run and the ramp length (ramp length to the nearest 0.01 m).

Solution

  1. The run is twelve times the rise. x = 12 × 0.6 m = 7.2 m
  2. The ramp is the hypotenuse. Use the Pythagorean theorem. L = √(0.6² + 7.2²) m ≈ 7.22 m
Common mix-up. Measure elevation and depression angles from the observer’s horizontal. Use the horizontal run as the denominator of the slope ratio. The distance along the ramp is the hypotenuse.

Two sighting points · Show steps

Two points on a horizontal line through the tower base lie on the same side of the tower and are 20 m apart. From 1.5 m above the ground at each point, the angles of elevation are 30° and 60°. Find the tower height to the nearest 0.1 m.

  1. Let d be the nearer distance and Δh the height above the points of observation. The nearer point has the 60° angle. Δh = d√3 = (d + 20 m)/√3
  2. Multiply by √3 and solve for the run, then include the observation height. 3d = d + 20 m ⇒ d = 10 m H = (10√3 + 1.5) m ≈ 18.8 m
A ramp in two sections · Show steps

A ramp rises in two sections. The first rises 0.4 m at a slope ratio of 1:10. The second has a horizontal run of 6 m and a slope ratio of 1:15. Find the ratio of total rise to total run.

  1. Find the first run and the second rise. x₁ = 10 × 0.4 m = 4 m　·　y₂ = 6 m / 15 = 0.4 m
  2. Divide the total rise by the total run. i = (0.4 + 0.4) m / (4 + 6) m = 0.08 = 8%
From a roof to a marker · Show steps

From a rooftop observation point, the angle of depression to the top of a marker is 45°. The marker is 16 m horizontally from the point directly below the observer. Its top is 2 m above level ground. Find the rooftop observation height.

  1. The horizontals are parallel, so the angle of elevation from the marker is also 45°. Δh = (16 m) tan 45° = 16 m
  2. Add the marker height to the difference. H = 16 m + 2 m = 18 m

Curriculum and study notes

This lesson follows Chapter 28 of PEP Grade 9, second semester. Hold the angle fixed while resizing, and use similarity to explain what stays constant. Use geometry for exact special-angle values and a calculator for approximate values at other angles. U.S. geometry classes often use SOH–CAH–TOA to remember the three ratios. The same methods work in feet (ft) when all lengths use that unit.

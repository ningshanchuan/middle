Exponents: count the multiplications

You share a video with 2 friends, each of them shares it with 2 more, and so on. After 20 rounds, how many new people see it? An exponent is just shorthand for “how many times we multiply”. Write the multiplications out and count, and every exponent rule appears by itself. Follow the pattern further and you will understand zero and negative exponents, and how powers of ten describe everything from atoms to galaxies.

⚠️ Common mix-up:3⁴ is not 3 × 4 = 12; it is 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81. When you multiply powers of the same base you ADD the exponents (2³ · 2⁴ = 2⁷), you don’t multiply them. And a negative exponent means a reciprocal (2⁻³ = 1/8), not a negative number.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 8.NR (properties of integer exponents; using scientific notation to represent, estimate, compare and compute with very large and very small numbers). Before steps 3–5, have students expand and count the factors themselves, then watch the animation to check. Step 6’s “divide by 10 each time” pattern is the most natural route to 10⁰ = 1 and negative exponents. In the Rule lab, set the quotient to m = n and then m < n and discuss a⁰ and a⁻ⁿ.

Integers: the number line keeps going left

In a video game your score is −30 and your friend’s is −50. Who is winning? Temperatures below freezing, depths below sea level, money you owe: all of them need numbers less than 0. Starting from a thermometer, the animation extends the number line to the left of 0, then shows opposites, absolute value, and how to compare negative numbers.

⚠️ Common mix-up:−6 looks like it has the “bigger number”, but it is LESS than −2: on a number line, farther left means smaller. −6 has the larger absolute value, which only means it is farther from 0, not that it is greater.

Explore: the elevator number line

Ground level is floor 0: floors above are positive, basements are negative. Drag the elevator (or use the slider). The purple dashed car sits at the opposite floor, and the green bracket shows the distance from the ground, the absolute value.

Try it: which two floors have the same absolute value? What is the opposite of floor 0?

Order challenge: least to greatest

Click the numbers in order from least to greatest. Each correct click drops the number onto the number line. Remember: farther left means smaller!

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 6.NR (positive and negative numbers describe quantities in opposite directions; rational numbers on the number line; opposites; absolute value as distance from 0; comparing and ordering rational numbers and interpreting them in context). The lesson builds “below zero” with a vertical thermometer and sea level before turning the line horizontal. Step 7 targets the misconception “bigger absolute value means bigger number”; pause and ask which is greater, −6 or −2. In the elevator explorer, students can discover |n| = |−n| and that 0 is its own opposite.

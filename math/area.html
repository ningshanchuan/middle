Area of polygons: cut it, move it

Which gives you more pizza: one 12-inch square pan, or two 6-inch square pans? Area is the number of unit squares that cover a shape. Rectangles are easy to count. For everything else, cut, slide and turn the pieces into a shape you already know. Every area formula comes from a rearrangement like that.

⚠️ Common mix-up:The height is measured straight up, perpendicular to the base, not along the slanted side! And the triangle and trapezoid formulas both have a ½ because each is half of a parallelogram.
Memory hint“If one won’t work, use two”: two copies of a triangle or a trapezoid always make a parallelogram, so take half.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 6.GSR (finding the area of triangles, special quadrilaterals and polygons by composing, decomposing and rearranging, and applying this to real problems). The lesson uses one idea throughout: turn the new shape into a known one (parallelogram → rectangle; two triangles or two trapezoids → a parallelogram). Step 6 (same base, same height, same area) is the key “aha”: have students predict whether the area changes before playing it. In Explore, slanting changes the perimeter but not the area, a good prompt for discussing the difference between the two.

Similar triangles have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides. Perimeter is the sum of the sides, so it scales by k. The base and the height each scale by k, so area scales by k². When k = 1, the triangles are congruent and similar.
Worked example · Double a 3–4–5 triangle

A right triangle has legs of 3 cm and 4 cm. A dilation has scale factor k = 2. Find the image perimeter and area.

  1. Use the Pythagorean theorem to find the hypotenuse.
  2. Add the original sides.
  3. Multiply the perimeter by 2.
  4. Use the perpendicular legs to find the original area, then multiply the area by 2².

Quick check · Dilation and ratios

Which clues prove similarity?

AA · Angle–Angle
Two pairs of corresponding angles match. The angle sum makes the third pair match too.
SAS · Side–Angle–Side
Two corresponding side ratios match, and so do the angles between those sides.
SSS · Side–Side–Side
All three corresponding side ratios match.
Common mix-up · SAS requires the angle between the two compared sides. Two side ratios and an equal non-included angle do not generally guarantee similarity.
Worked example · Prove similarity, then find a third side

Given AB = 6 cm, AC = 8 cm, BC = 10 cm, DE = 9 cm, DF = 12 cm, and ∠A = ∠D, find EF.

  1. Compare the sides around the angle, in second-to-first order.
  2. SAS gives △ABC ∼ △DEF, so EF corresponds to BC.

Quick check · Parallel cuts

How can you measure the tree from the ground?

Correspondence and tree height
Worked example · Use shadow lengths

On level ground at the same time, a vertical 1.8 m pole casts a 1.2 m shadow and an upright tree casts a 7 m shadow. Find the tree height H.

  1. The verticals make right angles with the ground, and the parallel sun rays make equal ground angles. The triangles are similar by AA.
  2. Multiply the tree’s shadow length by the height-to-shadow ratio.
Worked example · Use a flat mirror

A flat mirror lies on level ground between you and an upright tree. When you see the treetop, your eye is 1.6 m above the ground. The horizontal distances from your eye and the tree to the reflection point are 2.4 m and 9 m, respectively. Find the tree height.

  1. The angles of incidence and reflection are equal, so the angles with the mirror surface are equal too. The right angles give AA similarity.
  2. Solve for the height.

Spot the shadow

Find where the shadow begins. On level ground, measure from the base of the trunk to the shadow tip cast by the treetop. If you can’t identify that tip, use a thin pole to practice the shadow method, or use a mirror to measure the tree.

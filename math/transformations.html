Transformations: slide, flip, turn, resize

When a video-game character slides, flips or spins on the screen, what happens to its coordinates? Put a triangle on a coordinate grid and watch it slide, flip, turn and grow. Every motion has a coordinate rule, and some motions keep both size and shape (congruent) while others keep only the shape (similar).

⚠️ Common mix-up:A 90° counterclockwise rotation about the origin is (x, y) → (−y, x), not (−x, y); that one is a reflection across the y-axis. And a dilated figure is similar to the original but not congruent, unless the scale factor k = 1.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 8.GSR (properties of translations, reflections and rotations; describing the effect of translations, reflections, rotations and dilations with coordinates; two figures are congruent if a sequence of rigid motions maps one onto the other, and similar if dilations are also allowed). Pause at step 2 and have students predict A's new coordinates. At step 4, students can verify (−y, x) by turning tracing paper about the origin. In the sandbox's Challenge mode, ask students to state their sequence and explain why the result is congruent.

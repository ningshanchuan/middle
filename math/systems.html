Systems of Equations: two clues, one meeting point

One gym charges $10 a visit. Another charges $30 a month plus $4 a visit. After how many visits do they cost the same? One clue usually allows endless possibilities. Add a second clue and often only one answer survives. Starting from a simple snack purchase, we will see what “making two equations true at once” looks like on a graph.

⚠️ Common mix-up:A solution has to make BOTH equations true. A point that sits on only one of the lines is not a solution. And write it as an ordered pair (x, y): the order matters.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 8.PAR (writing and solving systems of linear equations in context; understanding a solution as the intersection of two lines; recognizing one, none or infinitely many solutions). Before steps 2–3, have students list a few pairs that fit a single clue and predict the shape the points will make. Steps 7 and 8 show substitution and elimination; the bar model in step 8 explains why subtracting removes x. In Explore, ask: what happens with the same slope but different intercepts?

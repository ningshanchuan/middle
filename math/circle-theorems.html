Perpendicular chord theorem

Radius OA joins the center to the rim. Chord AB joins two points on the rim. Diameter UV is a chord through the center. An arc is part of the rim. A diameter perpendicular to a chord bisects the chord and both of its arcs.

Inscribed angle theorem

A central angle has its vertex at the center. The vertex P of inscribed angle APB is on the circle, and its sides pass through A and B. It intercepts the arc from A to B that does not contain P. The inscribed angle is half the turn at the center along that same arc.

Opposite angles are supplementary

Proof sketch · Start with two equal radii

If O and M are distinct, right triangles OMA and OMB have equal hypotenuses OA and OB and share leg OM. Hypotenuse-leg (HL) congruence gives AM = MB. If M is O, AM and MB are both radii.

Reflect across UV. A and B swap, so U and V are the midpoints of the two arcs. Conversely, a diameter that bisects a chord other than a diameter is perpendicular to that chord.

Worked example · Find the chord length

Given OA = 5 cm, OM = 3 cm and OM perpendicular to AB, find AB.

Use the right triangle OMA to find half the chord.

AM = √(5² − 3²) cm = 4 cm

The perpendicular diameter bisects the chord, so double the half-chord length.

AB = 2(4 cm) = 8 cm

Quick check · Chords & diameter

Proof sketch · Draw the diameter through P

Start with B directly opposite P, so PB is a diameter. Since OP = OA, triangle OPA has equal base angles. The exterior angle AOB is the sum of those two base angles, so it is twice angle APB.

Now draw diameter PQ. If Q is A or B, use the first case. Otherwise, the arcs AQ and QB that avoid P measure twice angles APQ and QPB, respectively. If Q is between A and B on the intercepted arc, add the two arc measures. Otherwise, take their difference. Do the same operation with the angles at P and the factor of two still holds.

Worked example · The angle facing a diameter

Given diameter AB and another point P on the circle, find ∠APB.

Angle APB intercepts a semicircle, which has a central angle of 180°.

∠APB = 180° ÷ 2 = 90°

Watch the arc when AB is not a diameter. If P lies on the minor arc AB, angle APB intercepts the major arc. Use the turn greater than 180° at the center.

Quick check · Inscribed angles

Proof sketch · Two arcs make one full turn

Angle ABC intercepts arc ADC and angle ADC intercepts arc ABC. Their arcs make a full turn. Each inscribed angle is half its arc measure, so these angles add to 180°. The same argument works for the other pair.

Worked example · Find the opposite angle

In cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, angle ABC measures 112°. Find ∠ADC.

Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

∠ADC = 180° − 112° = 68°

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius

A tangent line meets the circle at exactly one point, the point of tangency. It is perpendicular to the radius there. A line through the outer endpoint of a radius and perpendicular to it is tangent. The distance from an outside point along a tangent to its point of tangency is the tangent length. The two tangent lengths from the same outside point are equal.

Two right angles
∠OTP = ∠OSP = 90°
Equal tangent lengths
PT = PS = √(OP² − OT²)
5 cm

Move P and compare the angles on either side of OP. Use right-triangle congruence to explain why OP bisects angle TPS.

Proof sketch · Find the shared hypotenuse

Drop a perpendicular from O to the tangent line. Its foot is the closest point on that line to O. If the foot differed from T, it would lie inside the circle, and the line would cross the rim twice. So T is the foot of the perpendicular.

Right triangles OTP and OSP share hypotenuse OP and have equal legs OT and OS. Hypotenuse-leg congruence gives PT = PS.

Worked example · Calculate both tangent lengths

Given OP = 5 cm and radius OT = 4 cm, find the tangent lengths PT and PS.

Use the Pythagorean theorem in right triangle OTP to find PT. Then use the equal tangent lengths to find PS.

PT = √(5² − 4²) cm = 3 cm

PS = PT = 3 cm

One fraction, two measurements

Two radii and the arc between them bound a sector. Let n° be the turn at the center along that arc and r be the radius. Divide n° by 360° to find the fraction of a full turn. Multiply the circumference by that fraction for arc length, or multiply the full area by it for sector area.

Fraction of the full circle
Arc length
ℓ = (n/360) · 2πr
Sector area
S = (n/360) · πr²
120°
6 cm

Hold the angle fixed and double the radius from 4 cm to 8 cm. By what factor does each measurement grow? Eliminate the common fraction from the two formulas to check this shortcut.

S = ½rℓ
Derivation · Divide the full turn

Split the full turn into 360 equal 1° slices. Each tiny sector has 1/360 of the area and its arc has 1/360 of the circumference. Add n slices to get both formulas for whole-number angles. Fractions of a degree work by subdividing the slices further.

In US problems with radius in inches (in), report arc length in inches and sector area in square inches (in²).

Worked example · A 120° sector

Given a radius of 6 cm and a central angle of 120°, find the arc length and sector area.

Since 120° is one-third of a full turn, take one-third of the circumference and one-third of the full circle’s area.

ℓ = (1/3) · 2π(6 cm) = 4π cm

S = (1/3) · π(6 cm)² = 12π cm²

Two parallel chords

A circle of radius 13 cm has parallel chords of lengths 10 cm and 24 cm on opposite sides of the center. Find the distance between them.

Show steps

A single perpendicular through the center bisects both chords. Use the half-chord and radius in each right triangle to find the two distances.

√(13² − 5²) cm = 12 cm√(13² − 12²) cm = 5 cm

The chords lie on opposite sides of the center, so add the distances to get 17 cm.

Use an inscribed angle to find arc length

On a circle of radius 9 cm, inscribed angle APB measures 70°. Find its intercepted arc length and the area of the corresponding sector.

Show steps

Double the inscribed angle to get 140°, which is 7/18 of a full turn.

ℓ = (7/18) · 18π cm = 7π cm

S = (7/18) · 81π cm² = 63π/2 cm²

The angle between two tangents

Tangents from P touch the circle at T and S. The smaller central angle TOS measures 124°. Find angle TPS.

Show steps

In quadrilateral OTPS, the angles at T and S are right angles. Subtract the three known angles from the quadrilateral’s 360° angle sum.

∠TPS = 360° − 90° − 90° − 124° = 56°

Teaching notes and references

Ask learners to identify the vertex and trace the intercepted arc before writing an angle equation. For chord and tangent lengths, mark the right angle and radius first. Keep exact answers in terms of π and round only when needed.

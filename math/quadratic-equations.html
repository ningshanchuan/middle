Quadratic Equations

How many times can a parabola meet the x-axis? Move the curve to find out, then use four methods to calculate the x-coordinates of those points.

The discriminant and the x-intercepts

Move all terms to the left and combine like terms. A polynomial equation with one variable is quadratic when the highest power left is 2. Its real roots are the x-values that make the left side zero. On the parabola, those are the x-intercepts.

ax² + bx + c = 0 (a ≠ 0)

Here a is the x² coefficient, b is the x coefficient, and c is the constant. If the x term or constant is missing, use zero for it.

Start by moving only c. Watch the whole parabola shift up or down.

Read the sign of Δ. Positive gives two distinct real roots. Zero gives one distinct real root (a double root). Negative gives no real roots.

Roots and coefficients (Vieta’s formulas)

When real roots exist, write the polynomial as a times two linear factors. Expand and compare the x-coefficient and the constant term to find the sum and product. Count a double root twice.

ax² + bx + c = a(x − x₁)(x − x₂)
a(x − x₁)(x − x₂) = ax² − a(x₁ + x₂)x + ax₁x₂
x₁ + x₂ = −b/a, x₁x₂ = c/a

    Build a perfect square

    Scrub to split, move and fill the corner.

    Worked example by completing the square

    Move the constant to the right. Half the x-coefficient of 6 is 3, so add 9 to both sides.

    x² + 6x − 7 = 0 → x² + 6x = 7
    x² + 6x + 9 = 7 + 9 → (x + 3)² = 16
    x + 3 = ±4 → x ∈ {−7, 1}

    The diagram uses positive lengths. The algebraic identity holds for every real x, so −7 is also a root.

    Derive the quadratic formula by completing the square

    Multiply both sides by 4a and move the constant term to the right. Add b² to both sides to make the left side a perfect square.

    4a²x² + 4abx + b² = b² − 4ac → (2ax + b)² = Δ

    When Δ ≥ 0, take square roots and solve for x. When Δ is negative, the square of a real number cannot equal Δ.

    2ax + b = ±√Δ → x = (−b ± √Δ)/(2a)

Dividing fractions: why flip and multiply?

A cookie recipe needs ¾ cup of sugar, but the only scoop you can find is ¼ cup. How many scoops? “To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal” is usually memorized. But division only asks a simple question: how many pieces of this size fit? Watch the pieces being laid down one by one, and the rule will appear on its own.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Measure the leftover in PIECES, not in wholes. In 3 ÷ 2/3, four pieces fit and 1/3 is left; that 1/3 is half a piece, so the answer is 4½, not 4⅓. And flip only the divisor (the second fraction), never the first one!

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 6.NR (interpret and compute quotients of fractions with visual models and the standard algorithm). The lesson uses the measurement (“how many groups fit?”) meaning of division: whole-number division first, then fraction bars that show why dividing by a proper fraction makes the answer bigger. Step 4 is the key idea: measure the leftover in pieces. Steps 5–7 give two explanations of the algorithm: common denominators then divide numerators, and “multiply by the denominator, divide by the numerator”, which together are multiplying by the reciprocal. In Explore, have students predict whether the quotient is more or less than 1 before checking.

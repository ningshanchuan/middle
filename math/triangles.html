Classification rules and vocabulary

A scalene triangle has three different side lengths. An isosceles triangle has at least two equal sides, so equilateral triangles are a special case. If a problem asks for one label, use “equilateral” when all three sides are equal.

An acute triangle has three angles under 90°. A right triangle has a 90° angle. An obtuse triangle has an angle greater than 90°. The same triangle can be both isosceles and right.

Equal sides in a triangle face equal angles. In Shape B, the angles opposite the two equal legs are both 45°.

Can three sticks close?

Drag the round ends to resize the sticks, or use the sliders. With c as the base, watch whether the free tips can meet.

Memory tip Add the two shorter lengths. Their sum must be greater than the longest length. If they are equal, the sticks can only meet along a straight line. |a − b| < c < a + b
Why are there two bounds?

Each pair of sides adds to more than the third because the direct segment is shorter than a detour. With a and b fixed, c is less than a + b. The other two inequalities say c is greater than the absolute difference. With c too long the tips fall short. With c too short the shorter stick cannot reach the longer stick’s tip, even when the tips are as close as possible.

Altitudes, medians and angle bisectors

Memory tipThe altitude meets at a right angle, the median finds a midpoint, and the bisector splits the vertex angle in half. You can draw each one from any vertex.
A median divides the opposite side into equal lengths. An angle bisector divides the vertex angle into equal angles. Drag A away from a symmetric position and watch their endpoints separate.
Try it with cardboard

Cut a triangle from cardboard of uniform thickness and density, then draw its medians. Gently support it with one fingertip at their intersection, then move your finger a little away. Compare where it can rest level and where it starts to tip.

Why do trusses use triangles?

Push the frame, then add a brace that fits its current diagonal. The brace makes two triangles. Push again and see whether the frame changes shape.

Three fixed side lengths determine a triangle’s shape. That makes the triangular frame rigid in this model. Real trusses also need suitable materials, sound connections and supports to carry loads.

From triangles to polygons

Polygons, regular polygons and tiling

Diagonals from one vertex divide a convex n-gon into n − 2 triangles.

(n − 2) × 180°

Walk once around a convex polygon in a consistent direction, taking one turn (exterior angle) at each corner. You finish facing your original direction after a full 360° turn. A regular polygon has equal sides and equal angles, so divide each sum by the number of sides to get one angle.

In an edge-to-edge tiling made from one kind of regular polygon, the corners around each vertex must fill exactly 360°. Equilateral triangles, squares and regular hexagons can each tile the plane this way. A regular pentagon has 108° corners, so three leave a 36° gap and four overlap.

Enrichment · 课外拓展 The same interior angle sum holds for a concave polygon whose sides don’t cross. To split it into triangles, you may need to draw diagonals from different vertices.

Worked examples, practice and quiz

Open two worked examples

US-style example · A triangular bracket

A triangular bracket has bars of 6 cm and 10 cm (about 2.4 in. and 3.9 in.). The third bar is x cm long. What lengths are possible?

  1. Find the lower bound.
    10 cm − 6 cm = 4 cm
  2. Find the upper bound.
    10 cm + 6 cm = 16 cm

Answer 4 < x < 16 (x in centimeters). Both endpoints are excluded.

Chinese-style example · A triangle’s exterior angle

Given In △ABC, extend BC through C to D. ∠ACD = 125° and ∠A = 47°.

Find The measure of ∠B.

Solution By the exterior angle theorem, the exterior angle equals the sum of the two nonadjacent interior angles.

∠B = ∠ACD − ∠A
125° − 47° = 78°

Answer ∠B measures 78°.

Practice · Reveal the steps
An isosceles triangle has sides of 4 cm and 9 cm. Find its perimeter.

The third side must match either 4 cm or 9 cm. If it is 4 cm, the shorter sides fall short.

4 cm + 4 cm = 8 cm < 9 cm

A third side of 9 cm works because 4 cm plus 9 cm exceeds 9 cm. Add the sides:

4 cm + 9 cm + 9 cm = 22 cm
A regular polygon has a 150° interior angle. Find its number of sides and its interior angle sum.

Subtract the interior angle from a straight angle, then divide the total turn by one exterior angle.

180° − 150° = 30°
n = 360° ÷ 30° = 12
(12 − 2) × 180° = 1800°
AM is a median and G is the centroid. If AG = 8 cm, find AM and GM.

The vertex-to-centroid distance is two-thirds of the whole median. Find the whole median, then subtract.

AM = 8 cm ÷ (2/3) = 12 cm
GM = 12 cm − 8 cm = 4 cm
Sort triangles by their angles

Find the largest angle in each set. Below 90° means acute, exactly 90° means right, and above 90° means obtuse.

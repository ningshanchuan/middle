Parallel Lines & Quadrilaterals

Can one pair of angles prove two lines are parallel? Drag the lines to find out, then use diagonals to identify quadrilaterals.

Angle review ↗

Three angle pairs

Line t is a transversal (截线, jiéxiàn). It crosses lines ℓ and m at different points. Pick an angle pair and notice its position. Then drag ℓ or m to see how the angles change.

Choose an angle pair

Corresponding angles

同位角 · tóngwèijiǎo · Matching positions

Angles 1 and 5 are corresponding. Parallel lines give equal corresponding angles. One equal pair also proves the lines parallel.

Alternate interior angles

内错角 · nèicuòjiǎo · Inside, on opposite sides of t

Angles 3 and 5 are alternate interior angles. Parallel lines give equal alternate interior angles. One equal pair also proves the lines parallel.

Same-side interior angles

同旁内角 · tóngpáng nèijiǎo · Inside, on the same side of t

Angles 3 and 6 are same-side interior angles. Parallel lines make them supplementary. One pair adding to 180° also proves the lines parallel.

Vertical angles like ∠1 and ∠3 are always equal, whether or not ℓ and m are parallel. A parallel-line test uses a specific pair from different crossings.

Example 1 · Use parallel lines to find angles

Given ℓ ∥ m and ∠1 = 64°.

Find ∠3, ∠5, and ∠6.

StatementReason
∠3 = ∠1 = 64°Vertical angles are equal.
∠5 = ∠3 = 64°Alternate interior angles of parallel lines are equal.
∠6 = 180° − 64°= 116°∠5 and ∠6 form a linear pair.

∠3 and ∠5 are both 64°, and ∠6 is 116°.

Advanced · Test for parallel with slope

Slope is rise divided by run. You can also calculate it from the tilt using the tangent function, tan. For these distinct, nonvertical lines, equal slopes prove the lines parallel.

Set ℓ to 10° and m to −10°. Compare the slope signs, then move m until an alternate interior pair is equal.

What diagonals tell you about a parallelogram

A parallelogram (平行四边形, píngxíng sìbiānxíng) has two pairs of parallel opposite sides. Change one side length and one angle. Which diagonal properties hold for every setting?

Diagonal AC
Diagonal BD
Angle between the diagonals (up to 90°)

Properties · Start with a parallelogram

  • Opposite sides are parallel and equal in length.
  • Opposite angles are equal. Adjacent interior angles add to 180°.
  • The diagonals bisect each other. O is the midpoint of AC and of BD.

Tests · Prove a quadrilateral is a parallelogram

For a quadrilateral, any one of these conditions is enough.

  • Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel (definition).
  • Both pairs of opposite sides are equal in length.
  • The same pair of opposite sides is parallel and equal in length.
  • Both pairs of opposite angles are equal.
  • The diagonals bisect each other.
Compare rectangle, rhombus, and square diagonals

A rectangle (矩形, jǔxíng) is a parallelogram with a right angle. A rhombus (菱形, língxíng) is a parallelogram with two equal adjacent sides. A square (正方形, zhèngfāngxíng) meets both definitions.

Guaranteed property All parallelograms Rectangle Rhombus Square
Diagonals bisect each otherYesYesYesYes
Diagonals are equal in lengthSometimesYesSometimesYes
Diagonals are perpendicularSometimesSometimesYesYes
Each diagonal bisects the angles at its endpointsSometimesSometimesYesYes

Once you know it’s a parallelogram, equal diagonals prove it’s a rectangle, perpendicular diagonals prove it’s a rhombus, and both together prove it’s a square.

Example 2 · Prove a quadrilateral is a parallelogram

Given diagonals AC and BD of quadrilateral ABCD intersect at O, with AO = CO and BO = DO.

Prove ABCD is a parallelogram.

StatementReason
AO = CO, BO = DOGiven.
∠AOB = ∠CODVertical angles are equal.
△AOB ≅ △CODSide–angle–side (SAS).
AB = CD∠ABO = ∠CDOCorresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal.
AB ∥ CDEqual alternate interior angles prove parallel lines.
ABCD is a parallelogram.The same pair of opposite sides, AB and CD, is parallel and equal.

The triangle midsegment

In triangle ABC, D lies on AB and E lies on AC. When each point is halfway along its side from A, DE is a midsegment (中位线, zhōngwèixiàn).

Start from vertex A

Midsegment theorem

The segment joining two side midpoints is parallel to the third side and half as long. Set D and E to 50% to check the rule in the diagram.

DE ∥ BC　DE = ½ BC

Spot the difference · Midsegment and median

When D and E are both midpoints, DE is a midsegment. Whenever D is the midpoint of AB, CD is a median from vertex C.

Example 3 · Find the length and prove parallel

Given D and E are midpoints of AB and AC in △ABC, and BC = 14 cm.

Find DE and prove DE ∥ BC.

StatementReason
DE is a midsegment of △ABC.Its endpoints are midpoints of two sides.
DE ∥ BC, DE = ½ BCTriangle midsegment theorem.
DE = ½(14 cm)= 7 cmSubstitute BC = 14 cm.

DE is 7 cm long and parallel to BC.

Advanced · From scaling to proof

When AD/AB = AE/AC, the two triangles share the included angle at A. By SAS similarity, △ADE ∼ △ABC. Corresponding angles ∠ADE and ∠ABC are equal, so DE ∥ BC. The ratio DE/BC equals the scale factor.

Move E independently. Put D at 40% and E at 60% to compare the directions of DE and BC. Then match the percentages and compare the lengths.

Practice 1 · Use algebra to test for parallel lines

Use the numbering from the transversal diagram. Suppose ∠3 = (2x + 16)° and ∠6 = (3x + 4)°. Can you prove ℓ ∥ m when x = 32?

Show steps
  1. ∠3 = (2 × 32 + 16)° = 80°
  2. ∠6 = (3 × 32 + 4)° = 100°
  3. 80° + 100° = 180°The same-side interior angles are supplementary, so ℓ ∥ m.
Practice 2 · Prove it’s a square from the diagonals

The two diagonals of quadrilateral ABCD bisect each other at right angles and each is 10 cm long. Prove ABCD is a square and find its side length.

Show steps
  1. The diagonals bisect each other, so ABCD is a parallelogram.
  2. Its diagonals are equal, so it is a rectangle. They are perpendicular, so it is also a rhombus. Therefore, it is a square.
  3. Let O be the intersection. OA and OB are both 5 cm, and ∠AOB is 90°. Apply the Pythagorean theorem in right triangle AOB. AB = √[(5 cm)² + (5 cm)²] = 5√2 cm
Practice 3 · Use the midsegment to find x and both lengths

In △ABC, DE is the midsegment parallel to BC. Its length is (3x − 1) cm and BC is (2x + 10) cm. Find x and both lengths.

Show steps
  1. The midsegment theorem gives BC = 2DE. Equate the lengths in centimeters. 2x + 10 = 2(3x − 1)
  2. 2x + 10 = 6x − 2 ⇒ 4x = 12 ⇒ x = 3
  3. DE = (3 × 3 − 1) cm = 8 cmBC = (2 × 3 + 10) cm = 16 cm

Sort the evidence

Every card starts with parallelogram ABCD, with diagonals crossing at O. Use the given facts to choose the most specific shape they guarantee.

Check the sorting rules
  • A right angle or equal diagonals guarantees a rectangle.
  • Equal adjacent sides or perpendicular diagonals guarantee a rhombus.
  • Evidence for both a rectangle and a rhombus guarantees a square.
  • Mutually bisecting diagonals or two pairs of equal opposite angles guarantee only a parallelogram.

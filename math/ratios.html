Ratios: two amounts that grow together

A sports-drink mix says 2 scoops for every 3 cups of water. How do you fill the whole team cooler and keep the same taste? A paint recipe, a car at steady speed, a price tag at the store: behind all of them is one idea, two amounts that change together in a fixed way. The animation starts with a bucket of paint and builds up to tape diagrams, double number lines, ratio tables and unit rates.

⚠️ Common mix-up:To get an equivalent ratio you must MULTIPLY (or divide) both numbers by the same number. Adding the same amount to both (like 2:3 → 3:4) changes the relationship, and the paint changes colour.

Explore: stretch a ratio

Set a recipe (blue : yellow) with the first two sliders, then drag the batches slider. The tapes and the double number line stretch together, but the paint colour never changes, because the ratio stays the same.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 6.NR / 6.PAR (ratio and rate reasoning: ratio language, tape diagrams, double number lines, ratio tables, unit rates and unit price). The lesson moves concrete → visual → symbolic: paint blocks, then tapes, then the double number line, then the ratio table and graph. Pause before step 6 and ask students to predict whether adding 4 to both numbers keeps the same colour. The graph in step 7 sets up 7th-grade proportional relationships (y = kx). In “Best buy”, challenge students to find two different deals with the same unit price.

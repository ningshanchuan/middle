Scatter Plots: seeing the trend in pairs of numbers

Do students who study longer tend to score higher? Plot study time and score as one point for each student, then look for a pattern. If the dots roughly follow a straight line, use a line to describe the trend and make predictions.

⚠️ Common mix-up: Judge a fitted line by how closely it follows the whole cloud. A good line can miss every data point.

Collect your own data

Do people with longer arm spans tend to be taller? For 8–15 people, record height and arm span in centimeters. Arm span is the distance between the middle fingertips with arms stretched sideways. Or pair each person’s study time for the same test with their score on it.

Describe your plot. Read from left to right. Do the dots generally rise, fall, or show no clear trend? Do they follow a line or a curve? Look for clusters or unusual points. Identical pairs overlap on the plot.

The dashed gray reference is the least-squares line. It gives the smallest sum of squared vertical gaps and passes through the mean point (x̄, ȳ), marked by a white cross. The correlation coefficient r ranges from −1 to 1. The closer |r| is to 1, the stronger the linear association.

Interpolation and extrapolation

Move the slider across the observed range and beyond it. Interpolation uses an x-value between the ends of the observed data. Extrapolation goes outside that range. The farther you go, the less evidence you have that the trend will continue.

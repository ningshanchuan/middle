Scatter Plots: seeing the trend in pairs of numbers

Does studying longer lead to better scores? Turn each student’s two numbers into one point on a graph, and the cloud of points tells you a trend. Then draw one straight line to sum it up, and use it to make predictions.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Two things changing together does not mean one causes the other. Ice-cream sales and sunburns rise together, but the real cause is hot, sunny weather. Also, a line of best fit does not have to pass through any of the data points.

Explore: fit the line yourself

Pick a realistic data set. Drag the two orange handles to move the line so it follows the cloud. Tap empty space to add a point, or tap a point to remove it, and watch how the trend and the line respond.

The best line makes the total area of the squares as small as possible (least squares), and it always passes through the mean point (x̄, ȳ) (white ✚). r is between −1 and 1: the closer |r| is to 1, the tighter the points hug a straight line. r only measures straight-line association.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 8.FGR / 8.DSR (construct and interpret scatter plots for bivariate data; describe patterns such as clustering, outliers, and positive or negative association; informally fit a straight line, use it to make predictions, and interpret slope and intercept in context). After step 3, ask students how a trend differs from a rule (not every point follows it). In step 6, stress that balancing points above and below is not enough: the misses should also be small. In Explore, the “car age vs price” data bends slightly, which opens a discussion of nonlinear association. Finish by discussing why correlation is not causation.

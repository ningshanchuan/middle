Square roots: a number that never ends

On a baseball diamond the bases are 90 feet apart. So how long is the throw from home plate to second base? A square with area 25 has side 5. What about a square with area 2? Its side is perfectly real (it is the diagonal of a unit square) yet its decimal digits go on forever without repeating. Let’s zoom into the number line and see.

⚠️ Common mix-up:√2 is not 1.41 or even 1.414: those are only approximations, and its decimal never ends. Also, a square root is not “divide by 2”: √16 = 4, not 8. The equation x² = 16 has two solutions, x = ±4, but the symbol √16 means only the positive one.

🗂️ Rational or irrational?

If it can be written as a ratio of two integers (its decimal ends or repeats), it is rational.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 8.NR (rational vs irrational numbers; approximating irrational numbers with rational numbers and locating them on the number line; using square-root and cube-root symbols for solutions of x² = p and x³ = p). Pause on step 5 (two unit squares re-cut into one square of area 2) and let students verify with paper. At steps 7–8, discuss why a fraction’s decimal must eventually repeat (long division can only have finitely many remainders). In Explore, ask students to pin √n to three decimal places and check by squaring.

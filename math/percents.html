Percent: it just means “per hundred”

Sneakers cost $80 and the sign says 25% off. How much do you actually pay? Sales, taxes, test scores, phone batteries: percents are everywhere. The animation starts from a 10 × 10 grid, shows what a percent really is, how to find a percent of any number, and how discounts and increases become a single multiplication by (1 ± p).

⚠️ Common mix-up:A percent is always a percent OF some whole. Going up 20% and then down 20% does not bring you back: the second 20% is taken of the bigger new price (100 → 120 → 96).

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 6.NR / 7.PAR (percent as a rate per 100; finding a percent of a quantity and finding the whole from a part and percent; in grade 7, multi-step percent problems such as discounts, tax and percent increase/decrease). Build intuition with the 10 × 10 grid first, then use the percent bar (a double number line) to line any whole up with 100%. Before step 9, have students vote on whether up 20% then down 20% returns to the start. In the sale explorer, students can verify that discount-then-tax equals tax-then-discount (multiplication is commutative).

    Tools and keyboard controls

    Use the compass to draw circles or arcs and transfer distances. Use the straightedge to draw through two points. The compass tip marks the center, and the other handle changes the opening. Moving the center keeps the opening fixed.

    The board snaps to nearby points and intersections. To use the keyboard, focus the board, move the cursor with the arrow keys, and press Enter or Space to select. Hold Shift to move in smaller increments. Use Set opening to set the compass width with two points.

    Mainland Chinese format · Deductive proof

    Given: O, A, B and X as above, with OA = OB and AX = BX.

    To prove: Ray OX bisects ∠AOB.

    Proof: In triangles OAX and OBX, OA = OB, AX = BX and OX = OX.

    Therefore △OAX ≅ △OBX by SSS, so ∠AOX = ∠BOX. Since X lies inside the angle, ray OX bisects ∠AOB.

    Definitions, propositions, theorems, converses and counterexamples
    Definition
    Tells what a term means. An angle bisector is a ray from the vertex that divides an angle into two equal angles.
    Proposition / statement
    A claim that can be true or false. In an if–then statement, the “if” gives the hypothesis and the “then” gives the conclusion.
    Theorem
    A statement established by proof. For example, if a quadrilateral is a square, then its diagonals are equal in length.
    Converse
    Swap hypothesis and conclusion. The converse says that if a quadrilateral has equal diagonals, then it is a square.
    Counterexample
    An example that meets the hypothesis and fails the conclusion. A rectangle with unequal length and width has equal diagonals and is not a square. One such example disproves the converse.

    For each line of a proof, ask what justifies it. When testing a converse, look for a counterexample.

    Worked example · Circumcenter of a right triangle

    US format

    Villages A, B and C form a right triangle with ∠C = 90°, AC = 6 km and BC = 8 km. How far is the shared station from each village?

    1. Find the hypotenuse with the Pythagorean theorem.
      AB = √((6 km)² + (8 km)²) = 10 km
    2. The circle with diameter AB passes through C because the angle at C is a right angle. Its center O is the midpoint of AB.
      OA = OB = OC = 10 km ÷ 2 = 5 km

    Mainland Chinese format

    Given: In △ABC, ∠C = 90°, AC = 6 km and BC = 8 km.

    Find: The circumradius.

    Solution: Use the Pythagorean theorem for AB. Circumcenter O is the midpoint of AB.

    AB = √(36 km² + 64 km²) = 10 km
    R = AB ÷ 2 = 10 km ÷ 2 = 5 km

    Answer: The circumradius is 5 km.

    From hexagons to trisection

    Inscribe a regular hexagon

    Keep the compass open to one radius and step that chord length around the circle. Each pair of neighboring vertices forms an equilateral triangle with the center. Every step turns through a 60° central angle.

    Why is there no general trisection method? Enrichment · 课外拓展

    Some angles can be trisected. One third of a right angle is 30°, which you can construct by bisecting 60°. But a compass and unmarked straightedge cannot trisect 60° exactly. So no single straightedge-and-compass method trisects every angle.

    You can construct 60° from rational starting coordinates by building an equilateral triangle. Coordinates of intersections of lines and circles require only arithmetic and square roots. Each square root leaves the field degree unchanged or doubles it. This forces the minimal polynomial degree of a constructible number to be a power of two.

    x = 2 cos 20°, and x³ − 3x = 2 cos 60° = 1

    Constructing 20° would give its cosine as the horizontal projection of a unit segment, so twice that length would be constructible too. The triple-angle identity gives the equation above. The only possible rational roots are 1 and −1, and neither works. This makes the cubic irreducible over the rationals. Its degree 3 is not a power of two.

    Practice and quiz

    Two more construction challenges

    1. Divide segment AB into four equal parts by repeatedly constructing midpoints. How many perpendicular bisectors does this take?

    Show steps

    Bisect AB at M. Then bisect AM and MB. This method uses three perpendicular bisectors.

    2. Try an SSS construction with lengths 2 cm, 3 cm and 5 cm. What happens?

    Show the key idea

    Use 5 cm as the base and draw circles of radii 2 cm and 3 cm. The radii add to the distance between the centers. The circles touch at just one point, on the base, so there is no triangle.

The chaos game

Dots plotted0

Join the midpoints of an equilateral triangle’s sides, cut out the middle triangle, and repeat on each triangle left. Each round, or iteration, triples the number of small triangles and halves their side length. In the chaos game, start at a corner and choose one of the three corners at random, with an equal chance for each. Move halfway toward it, plot the new point, and repeat from there.

Build a tiny equilateral triangle outward on the middle third of each edge and erase its base. One edge becomes four edges a third as long, so the perimeter multiplies by 4/3 each round and grows without bound. After the first round, each round adds 4/9 as much area as the previous round added, and the total area approaches 1.6 times the starting area.

Worked example · How do the numbers grow?

Given an equilateral triangle with sides of 3 cm, find the perimeter after two iterations.

Solution — Multiply the perimeter by 4/3 each round.

  1. Starting perimeter 3 × 3 cm = 9 cm
  2. After one iteration 9 cm × 4/3 = 12 cm
  3. After two iterations 12 cm × 4/3 = 16 cm

The perimeter is 16 cm. Set the slider to step 2 to check the table.

The dimension of the Koch curve

Move the snowflake slider. Each round gives one original side four times as many segments, each a third as long. Continuing forever gives a Koch curve made of four copies at one-third scale. At that scale a line segment needs three copies and a square needs nine, so the Koch curve’s dimension is between 1 and 2.

Each point is a complex number c, which has two coordinates. Start at 0 and keep squaring the result and adding the same c. The values of c whose results stay within some fixed distance of 0 forever make up the Mandelbrot set. Stretch problem · How much area does the snowflake gain?

If the starting triangle has area 9 cm², how much area does each of the first two rounds add?

Show the steps

Round 1 adds three triangles. Each has one-third of the starting side length, so it has one-ninth of the starting area.

3 × (9 cm² ÷ 9) = 3 cm²

Round 2 adds 12 triangles, each with one-ninth the area of a triangle from round 1.

12 × (1 cm² ÷ 9) = 4/3 cm²

Stretch problem · What is the total length of the last layer?

Suppose a tree with three levels has a 40 cm trunk and a length ratio of 0.50. Find the total length of all the branches in level 3.

Show the steps

The trunk is level 1. Two rounds of splitting give four branches at level 3. Set the depth slider to 3 to check the tip count.

Length of each branch 40 cm × 0.50² = 10 cm

Total length of this level 4 × 10 cm = 40 cm

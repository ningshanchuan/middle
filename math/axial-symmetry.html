Fold angle

A figure has reflection symmetry if folding along a line makes its two sides coincide exactly. The crease is its line of symmetry. Points that meet in the fold are corresponding points.

Two figures are reflections of each other if folding along a line makes one overlap the other.

Look for symmetry in photos

Look for matching details in the outlines or patterns. Pose, lighting and perspective can affect the symmetry you see in a photo.

The perpendicular bisector

Drag P and find where PA and PB are equal. Change its height to test your finding.

Distances in coordinate units.

The line of reflection is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining any two distinct corresponding points. A point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from the endpoints. Conversely, any point equidistant from the endpoints lies on that line.

Proof · Both directions

Let \(M\) be the midpoint of \(AB\). If \(P\) is on the perpendicular bisector and \(P\ne M\), then \(AM=BM\), \(PM\) is shared, and both included angles are right angles. SAS gives \(\triangle AMP\cong\triangle BMP\), so \(PA=PB\).

Conversely, if \(PA=PB\) and \(P\) is off line \(AB\), SSS makes the same triangles congruent. The two adjacent angles at \(M\) form a straight angle and are equal, so each is \(90^\circ\). On line \(AB\), only the midpoint \(M\) is equidistant from both ends. The midpoint itself lies on the perpendicular bisector.

Worked example · Distance to the two endpoints

Given \(A(-3,0)\), \(B(3,0)\), \(P(0,4)\), and \(M(0,0)\) is the midpoint of \(AB\). Find \(PA\) and \(PB\).

Work \(AM\) is 3 units and \(PM\) is 4 units. The Pythagorean theorem gives \(PA=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\) units. Since \(P\) is on the perpendicular bisector of \(AB\), \(PB=PA\). Answer Both distances are 5 units.

Split an isosceles triangle

Change the base angle and watch both sides move. Make the triangle equilateral and look for a 30° angle.

An isosceles triangle has two equal legs, so its base angles are equal. The converse also holds. If two angles of a triangle are equal, their opposite sides are equal.

The altitude to the base, the median to the base and the bisector of the vertex angle are the same segment. Its line is the axis of symmetry. Chinese textbooks call this 三线合一 (sān xiàn hé yī, three lines in one).

Start at the vertex between the equal legs and go to the base. In an equilateral triangle, any vertex can play that role.
Proof · Base angles, the three-line property and the converse

Suppose \(AB=AC\) and let \(D\) be the midpoint of \(BC\). SSS gives \(\triangle ABD\cong\triangle ACD\). This makes the base angles equal and makes \(AD\) the vertex angle bisector. The congruent angles at \(D\) form a straight angle, so both are right angles.

Conversely, if \(\angle B=\angle C\), draw the bisector of \(\angle A\) to meet \(BC\) at \(D\). The two triangles have two pairs of equal angles and share side \(AD\). By AAS, \(\triangle ABD\cong\triangle ACD\), so \(AB=AC\).

Equilateral and 30°–60°–90° triangles

An equilateral triangle has three equal sides, three 60° angles and three lines of symmetry. Draw an altitude to split it into two right triangles. The leg opposite 30° is half the original base, which is also half the hypotenuse.

Start with any right triangle with a 30° angle. Reflect it across the leg between the 30° angle and the right angle. The two 30° angles make 60°, the right angles form a straight line, and the two outer angles are each 60°. The original hypotenuse is a side of this equilateral triangle, and its base is twice the leg opposite 30°.

Worked examples · A vertex angle and a side opposite 30°
  1. Given \(AB=AC\) and \(\angle B=52^\circ\). Find the vertex angle at \(A\).
  2. Work The base angles are equal, so \(\angle C=52^\circ\). The angles sum to \(180^\circ\), so \(\angle A=180^\circ-2\times52^\circ=76^\circ\).
  3. Answer The vertex angle is \(76^\circ\). Set the base-angle slider to \(52^\circ\) to check.

Given In right triangle \(ABC\), \(\angle C=90^\circ,\ \angle A=30^\circ\), and \(AB=14\,\mathrm{cm}\). Find \(BC\).

Work \(BC\) is opposite 30°, and \(AB\) is the hypotenuse. So \(BC=\tfrac12(14\,\mathrm{cm})=7\,\mathrm{cm}\). Answer \(BC\) is 7 cm.

Shortest path to the riverbank

Start at A, stop at P on the straight riverbank to water your horse, then go to B. Drag P and watch the total distance.

Reflect B across the bank to B′. Since P is on the mirror line, PB equals PB′. The route from A through P to B′ is shortest when P is where segment AB′ meets the bank. Then the two legs make one straight segment.

AP+PB=AP+PB'\ge AB'
Reflect one endpoint to turn a shortest broken-path problem into a straight-line distance.
Worked example · Find the minimum distance
  1. Given \(A(0,3)\), \(B(12,6)\), and the bank is \(y=0\). Each unit represents 1 m.
  2. Find the minimum of \(AP+PB\) and where to put \(P\).
  3. Work Reflect to get \(B'(12,-6)\). From \(A\) to \(B'\) the line drops 9 m and runs 12 m to the right. The bank is only 3 m down, one third of the drop, so the horizontal run to the bank is 4 m.
  4. By the Pythagorean theorem, \(AB'=\sqrt{12^2+9^2}\,\mathrm m=15\,\mathrm m\). Answer Use \(P(4,0)\) for a minimum distance of 15 m.

Challenge · Where do P and Q go when A moves?

Move A from 6 m above the horizontal bank to 3 m. Predict whether each stop moves toward or away from the corner, then check with the diagram.

Reflect \(A\) across the vertical bank to \(A'\) and \(B\) across the horizontal bank to \(B'\). Then \(AP+PQ+QB=A'P+PQ+QB'\ge A'B'\). If \(A'B'\) crosses both banks in order, its crossings attain the lower bound. Every height in this model meets that condition.

At the 3 m setting, \(A'(-4,3)\) and \(B'(8,-3)\). The line drops 1 m for each 2 m to the right, so \(P(0,1)\) and \(Q(2,0)\). Both stops move closer to the corner, and the minimum is \(\sqrt{12^2+6^2}\,\mathrm{m}=6\sqrt{5}\,\mathrm{m}\).

Practice and quiz

Practice 1 · Find the mirror line

Distinct points \(P(-2,4)\) and \(P'(8,4)\) are reflections. Find the mirror line and the image of \(Q(5,-3)\).

Show steps

The midpoint is \((3,4)\). The segment is horizontal, so its perpendicular bisector is \(x=3\). \(Q\) is two units to the right of the axis, so its image is two units to the left, at \(Q'(1,-3)\).

Practice 2 · Is 70° a base angle?

An isosceles triangle contains a 70° angle. Find every possible set of angle measures.

Show steps

If 70° is the vertex angle, each base angle is \((180^\circ-70^\circ)/2=55^\circ\). If it is a base angle, the other base angle is also 70° and the vertex angle is \(180^\circ-2\times70^\circ=40^\circ\).

Practice 3 · Only part of the bank is accessible

Use \(A(0,3)\) and \(B(12,6)\) from the river model. Can you still take the shortest route if you can stop only where \(0\le x\le4\)? What if you can stop only where \(5\le x\le12\)?

Show steps

A 15 m route requires \(P\) to be the unique crossing of segment \(AB'\) with the bank, \((4,0)\). The first interval includes that endpoint, so the minimum remains 15 m. The second interval excludes it, so every allowed route is longer than 15 m.

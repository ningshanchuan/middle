Mean, Median & Spread: where is the middle of the data?

Your favorite player scored 12, 15, 8, 40 and 10 points in five games. What is a fair way to say how many they “usually” score? A set of data has highs and lows. How can one number describe the “typical” value, and another describe how spread out the data are? We will level stacks of books and balance a number line to see where the mean and median come from.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Always put the data in order before finding the median! For 7, 2, 9 the median is 7 (in order: 2, 7, 9), not the 2 that happens to sit in the middle of the list. And the mean and median are not always equal: an outlier drags the mean toward it.
What was typical for the player at the top?

The five games total 85 points, so the mean is 17 points per game. Put the scores in order: 8, 10, 12, 15, 40. The median is 12 points. The 40-point game pulls up the mean. The median better describes the cluster from 8 to 15 points.

Weighted mean (jiāquán píngjūnshù)

Two scores are 70 and 90 points. What happens if the second score counts more? Use the sliders to change the weights. Multiply each score by its weight, then divide the sum of the products by the total weight.

In-class check and practice
Practice: work backward to find a weight

Two scores are 70 and 90 points. The weighted mean is 86 points. What percentage of the total weight goes to the score of 90?

Show steps

The gap from 70 to 90 is 20 points. Since 86 is 16 points above 70, the weight on the 90-point score is 16 ÷ 20 = 80%.

Grouped frequencies and an estimated mean

Group ten walking times into equal-width intervals. Each interval includes its lower boundary and excludes its upper boundary. Draw a bar between those boundaries, with height equal to the frequency. Use the midpoint of each class to estimate the mean when only the frequencies are known.

In-class check and practice
Practice: find a missing frequency

Walking times fall into [10, 20) and [20, 30) minutes. Four walks fall in the first interval. Using the midpoints gives a mean of exactly 21 minutes. How many walks fall in the second interval?

Show steps

The midpoints are 15 and 25 minutes. The estimate is 6 minutes above 15, or 6 ÷ 10 = 60% of the way to 25. The first class therefore accounts for 40% of the times. There are 4 ÷ 40% = 10 times in all, so the second frequency is 6.

Quartiles (sìfēnwèishù) in two textbooks

Sort the data and split them into two halves with the same number of values. If the count is odd, leave the middle value out of both halves. The median of the whole set is Q₂. The medians of the lower and upper halves are Q₁ and Q₃.

Compare two groups by mean and variance

Two groups can have the same mean score in a target game, with different spreads. Compare their averages and then their variances. For each group, subtract its own mean from each score, square the differences, and average them. A smaller variance means more consistent scores.

In-class check and practice
Practice: add two points to every score

If every score in group B increases by 2 points, what happens to its mean and variance?

Show steps

The mean increases by 2 points as well. Each difference from the new mean stays the same, so the variance stays the same. Check this by sliding group B’s mean from 8 to 10 points.

Advanced: verify with the sum of squared deviations

Each group has four scores. Divide its sum of squared deviations by 4 to get the variance. Slide group B’s inner distance from 2 points to 1 point. This halves every distance from the mean. What fraction of the old variance remains?

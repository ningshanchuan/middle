If y = k/x, where k is a fixed nonzero number and x ≠ 0, then y varies inversely with x. The graph is a hyperbola. Its two branches get closer to the axes without touching them. The perpendiculars from P to the axes form a rectangle with side lengths |x| and |y|, so its area is |xy| = |k|. To see whether y rises or falls as x increases, look at one branch at a time.

Example 1 · Find the inverse function and rectangle area

Given: P (−2, 3) lies on an inverse proportion graph. Find the equation and the area of the rectangle shown in the diagram.

  1. Substitute P’s coordinates into xy = k. k = xy = (−2) · 3 = −6
  2. Write the equation. y = −6/x (x ≠ 0)
  3. The sides are 2 and 3 units long, so the area is 6 square units. S = |k| = 6

Quick check

What stays constant?

Use the same positive x in both equations. Then double x and compare the two outputs.

In direct proportion, the ratio y/x is constant for nonzero x, and the full graph is a line through the origin. In inverse proportion, the product xy is constant and the graph is a hyperbola. Double a positive x and the direct output doubles while the inverse output halves.

Example 2 · Both pass through (2, 4). What is each y when x = 4?
  1. Find the direct ratio. 4/2 = 2, y = 2x
  2. Find the inverse product. 2 · 4 = 8, y = 8/x
  3. Substitute x = 4 into each expression. 2 · 4 = 8 and 8/4 = 2

Example 3 · A 120 km drive

Given a distance of 120 km and an average speed of 40 km/h, find the travel time.

  1. Divide distance by average speed to get 3 hours. t = (120 km)/(40 km/h) = 3 h
  2. At an average of 30 km/h, the same trip takes 4 hours. t = (120 km)/(30 km/h) = 4 h

Miles work the same way. For example, 60 mi ÷ 20 mph = 3 h.

Example 4 · Double the contact area

A perpendicular force of 600 N acts over areas of 0.03 m² and 0.06 m². Find the average pressure in each case.

  1. First divide the force by the smaller contact area. p = (600 N)/(0.03 m²) = 20,000 Pa
  2. Double the area and halve the average pressure. p = (600 N)/(0.06 m²) = 10,000 Pa

1 Pa = 1 N/m², and 1 kPa = 1,000 Pa. US tire gauges often use psi (pounds of force per square inch).

Example 5 · Intersect y = 6/x with y = x + 1
  1. Set the two expressions for y equal. 6/x = x + 1, x ≠ 0
  2. Clear the denominator and factor. x² + x − 6 = 0(x + 3)(x − 2) = 0
  3. Substitute both nonzero roots into y = x + 1. x = −3 → y = −2x = 2 → y = 3
  4. The intersections are (−3, −2) and (2, 3).

Point P on an inverse proportion graph is in quadrant II at x = −3. Its rectangle with the axes has area 15 square units. Find y.
  1. In quadrant II, x < 0 and y > 0, so k < 0.
  2. Use the area to find k, then substitute. |k| = 15, k = −15 y = (−15)/(−3) = 5
A perpendicular force of 600 N must produce at most 12 kPa of average pressure. What minimum area is needed?
  1. Convert the pressure limit to pascals. 12 kPa = 12,000 Pa
  2. With fixed force, greater area lowers average pressure. Find the area at exactly the pressure limit. A = (600 N)/(12,000 Pa) = 0.05 m²
  3. The area must be at least 0.05 m². Check it in the pressure model above.
For positive x, where is y = 6/x above y = x + 1?
  1. “Above” means the first y-value is larger. 6/x > x + 1
  2. Multiply by positive x to keep the inequality in the same direction. (x² + x − 6) < 0
  3. Factor. Since x + 3 is positive throughout the given domain, we need x − 2 < 0. The answer is 0 < x < 2. (x + 3)(x − 2) < 0

Teaching notes

This lesson covers sections 26.1 and 26.2 of the PEP Grade 9, second-semester text. First keep k fixed and move P. Then keep |k| fixed and change the sign of k.

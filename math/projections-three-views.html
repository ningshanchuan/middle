Compare the rays

Sun rays reaching the ground are nearly parallel, so they give a parallel projection. On level ground, a vertical pole casts a longer shadow when the Sun is lower. Rays from a point lamp all pass through one point, giving a central projection.

Sun mode models an equinox at 30° north. From morning to solar noon, the Sun gets higher above the horizon.

At the same moment, vertical poles on the same flat ground have sunlight shadows pointing the same way. Their lengths can differ. With a point lamp, the shadows can even point in different directions.
Worked example · Find the height from a shadow

A 1.2 m pole casts a 2.4 m shadow. A taller pole casts a 7.2 m shadow at the same moment. Both poles are vertical on level ground. How tall is the second pole?

The Sun’s rays meet the ground at the same angle. The pole, its shadow and the ray to its tip form a right triangle in each case. The triangles are similar, so the height-to-shadow ratios are equal.

h ÷ 7.2 m = 1.2 m ÷ 2.4 m
h = 7.2 m × 0.5 = 3.6 m

The second pole is 3.6 m tall.

In orthographic projection, rays are parallel and meet the drawing plane at right angles. Project from the front, left and above to make the front, left and top views.

Align length and height. Match depth. Front and top line up left to right (same length). Front and left line up vertically (same height). Left and top show the same front-to-back depth.

Read dimensions from the views

The solid is a rectangular prism. Read length and height from the front view and length and depth from the top view. Each view shows one face. Its opposite face has the same area. Add the three areas and double the sum. For volume, multiply base area by height.

Surface area
S = 2(lh + wh + lw)
Volume
V = lwh

This shortcut uses the opposite faces of a rectangular prism. For a stepped cube solid, faces can overlap in projection. Count the exposed unit squares instead.

Explore surface area and volume
Worked example and extra practice

The top view of an upright cylinder is a circle 6 cm across. Its front view is a rectangle 6 cm wide and 4 cm high. Find the volume and total surface area.

Read the radius from the top and the height from the front. Unroll the curved surface to get a rectangle whose sides are the circumference and height.

r = 6 cm ÷ 2 = 3 cm, h = 4 cm
V = πr²h = π(3 cm)²(4 cm)
V = 36π cm³
S = 2πr² + 2πrh
S = 18π cm² + 24π cm² = 42π cm²

The volume is 36π cm³ and the surface area is 42π cm². Try changing only the height and see which views change.

From drawings to shadow theatre

  • Engineering drawings use several views to specify a part’s width, height and hole locations for making and checking it.
  • Imagine slicing a building horizontally and looking down at it. A floor plan uses this view to show the rooms. An elevation shows the walls and window heights from outside.
  • In Chinese shadow puppetry (píyǐngxì), performers move figures between a light and a translucent screen. The light brings the figures’ outlines and colors into view on the screen.

Scale Drawings: stretching without distorting

A toy car says 1 : 64 on the box. How long is the real car? Maps, blueprints and model cars are all scale drawings of real things. Why do they keep the same shape? And when lengths double, why does the area become four times as big? Let the animation show you.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Lengths multiply by the scale factor k, but areas multiply by k². Triple the sides and the area becomes 9 times as big, not 3 times. And stretching in only one direction is not scaling: the shape changes.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 7.GSR (solve problems involving scale drawings: compute actual lengths and areas, reproduce a drawing at a different scale). The squashed house in step 3 is a great discussion point: ask students to explain why it is not a scale drawing. Before steps 6–7, have students predict how area changes when lengths double or triple; many will say 2× and 3×. Link the map section to ratios and unit rates: “1 cm represents 5 km” is a rate of 5 km per cm.

Solving equations: keep the balance

A phone plan costs $10 a month plus $5 for each gigabyte. Your bill is $35. How many gigabytes did you use? An equals sign is like a level balance. Adding or subtracting the same amount on both sides keeps the same solutions. So does multiplying or dividing both sides by the same nonzero number. When the scale tips, an inequality shows which side is heavier.

⚠️ Common mix-up:Changing only one side (say, subtracting 3 on the left only) breaks the balance. And when you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, you must flip the sign: −2x < 6 gives x > −3, not x < −3.
Memory hint“Unwrap in reverse”: the last operation done to x is the first one you undo.

Multi-step equations: x on both sides. How many solutions?

Grade 8 equations take several moves: distribute, combine like terms, get the x-terms on one side, then divide. Sometimes x disappears completely! Then the equation has either no solution or infinitely many.

🧩 Step-by-step solver: you choose the next move

📈 See it on a graph + 🎯 build an equation

Think of each side as a line: y = left side and y = right side. Where the lines meet, the two sides are equal, so the x-coordinate of the meeting point is the solution. Different slopes cross once. Same slope but different y-intercepts: parallel, no solution. Identical: the lines coincide, infinitely many solutions.

Multi-step inequalities and systems of inequalities

Distribute, move terms, and combine like terms as you would for an equation. When you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign. Then graph the solution set.

If collecting the x-terms on the left gives a negative coefficient, collecting them on the right lets you divide by a positive number. For example, 3 ≤ 2x gives 1.5 ≤ x, which reads as x ≥ 1.5.

📘 How China teaches it: 人教版 Grade 7 (second half), inequalities

Chinese textbooks start with three properties of inequalities: (1) adding or subtracting the same number keeps the sign; (2) multiplying or dividing by the same positive number keeps it; (3) multiplying or dividing by the same negative number reverses it. Solving 一元一次不等式 (yī yuán yī cì bùděngshì, a linear inequality) uses the same five steps as equations: 去分母, 去括号, 移项, 合并同类项, 系数化为1, with the flip in case (3).

All the solutions together are called the 解集 (jiě jí, “solution set”). For a 不等式组 (bùděngshì zǔ, “system of inequalities”), the 解集 is the common part. Chinese students memorize a four-line rhyme (see the four boxes above): 同大取大 tóng dà qǔ dà, 同小取小 tóng xiǎo qǔ xiǎo, 大小小大中间找 dà xiǎo xiǎo dà zhōngjiān zhǎo, 大大小小找不到 dà dà xiǎo xiǎo zhǎo bú dào.

👩‍🏫 Teacher notes

Supports Georgia 6.PAR and 7.PAR (solving one- and two-step equations using properties of equality; writing and solving simple inequalities and graphing their solutions; understanding why the sign flips when multiplying or dividing by a negative). The balance makes the properties of equality visible. At step 2, have students predict what happens if weights come off one side only. For step 6 use the socks-and-shoes analogy: what goes on first comes off last. The balance solver deliberately lets students change one side so they can see the tilt and connect it to inequalities. Note that the balance model only shows positive quantities; step 10 moves to symbolic steps for subtraction and negatives.

The “Both sides” and “Inequality systems” sections support Georgia’s grade 8 standard 8.PAR.3 on linear equations and inequalities in one variable. Students explore equations with one solution, infinitely many solutions, or no solution. The step-by-step solver uses a guided order and awards a star for the shortest route available in the tool. Students can compare moving the x-terms first with moving the constants first. The graph links the number of solutions to how two lines sit (cross, parallel, coincide) and prepares students to solve systems of linear equations. China’s five named steps (去分母, 去括号, 移项, 合并同类项, 系数化为1) are the same moves as the US approach and make a good side-by-side comparison.

Simplifying Square Roots

A square has an area of 72 square centimeters. How can you write its exact side length? Start by seeing how a square root turns area back into length.

From area to side length

In the real numbers, an expression of the form √a, with a ≥ 0, is a square-root expression. The symbol √a means the principal square root of a, the nonnegative number whose square is a. The expression a under the radical sign is the radicand.

−5
  • (√a)² = a(a ≥ 0)
  • √(a²) = |a|(a is any real number)

√25 means 5. The equation x² = 25 has two solutions, x = 5 and x = −5.

Find the largest perfect-square factor

Enter a whole number and test a perfect-square divisor. For example, 72 ÷ 36 = 2. Divide the square of area 72 into six rows and six columns. Each small square has area 2. Count along an edge to see the length of the big square.

Test a perfect square

Pair prime factors

Each pair of equal prime factors makes a perfect square. Take its square root outside the radical and leave the unpaired factors inside.

n = (7√3)² = 49 × 3 = 147

Square both sides to get the only possible n. Verify it with the simplifier.

In simplest radical form, there is no denominator inside the radical and no square factor left to take out. For a positive integer radicand, check for perfect-square factors greater than 1. For example, 3√8 simplifies to 6√2. If the radicand is a perfect square, write the whole-number result, as in √49 = 7.
Worked example · A square of area 72 cm²

Given a square with area 72 square centimeters.

Find the side length s.

Take the principal square root of the area. Factor 72 as 36 × 2, then take √36 outside the radical.

s = √(72 cm²) = √(36 × 2) cm
s = 6√2 cm ≈ 8.49 cm

The side is exactly 6√2 centimeters, or about 8.49 centimeters.

Calculate with radicals

For addition and subtraction, simplify first, then combine terms with the same radicand. For multiplication and division, combine radicands under one root when the conditions below are met.

3

When the product and quotient rules apply

  • √(ab) = √a · √b(a ≥ 0, b ≥ 0)
  • √(a/b) = √a/√b(a ≥ 0, b > 0)

The product rule applies to multiplication. For example, √(9 + 16) = 5, while √9 + √16 = 7.

Worked example · How much longer is the perimeter?

Given two squares with areas of 18 m² and 8 m².

Find the difference between their perimeters.

Find each side length, then multiply their difference by 4.

√18 m = 3√2 m
√8 m = 2√2 m
4(3√2 − 2√2) m = 4√2 m

The perimeters differ by 4√2 meters.

Estimate a root on the number line

If the radicand is a perfect square, you can find the exact root. Otherwise, find the two neighboring perfect squares around it. Their roots give the two nearest integer bounds. Zoom to tenths and square those tick values to narrow the interval.

Teaching notes and curriculum
